# The ground, the sea and the globe: what was read, and what was done with it

`sr-render/src/earth/` and `assets/shaders/earth*.wesl`, `sea.wesl`. Every colour, density and proportion is **estimated** unless a line says otherwise.

## Read

- **No tiling.** Heitz and Neyret blend three randomly placed patches a triangle and restore the histogram (<https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/722-2/>); Mikkelsen's hex tiling does it with no lookup table (<https://jcgt.org/published/0011/03/05/paper-lowres.pdf>). Here nothing is a photograph, so one small tile of gradient noise with its slopes is read at a different turn and shift for each use (`earth/grain.rs`), and what is scattered is hashed from whole numbers.
- **Layers by height, bumps from slopes.** Height-blended splatting, `max(h1 + a1, h2 + a2) - depth` (<https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/advanced-terrain-texture-splatting>); a normal from a height's screen derivatives (Mikkelsen, <https://jcgt.org/published/0009/03/04/paper-lowres.pdf>). The noise tile carries its own slopes, so the normal is a sum of them; a hollow is darkened and shut off from the sky.
- **Far and near.** Arma's terrain is a satellite picture with a mask choosing detail surfaces near the eye and grass geometry to about 125 m (<https://gist.github.com/pennyworth12345/27786cf99652e0018b9b2f0becaa0638>, <https://sites.google.com/site/islandconfigs/cfgworlds-overview/cfgworlds-clutter>); Unreal goes from detail to macro textures with distance (<https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/landscape-materials-in-unreal-engine>). Here: Blue Marble and the region's 450 m colour far off, the designed ground near, plants as geometry to 400 m.
- **Lost detail becomes roughness.** Toksvig and Kaplanyan's filters (<https://blog.selfshadow.com/2011/07/22/specular-showdown/>, <https://www.jp.square-enix.com/tech/library/pdf/ImprovedGeometricSpecularAA.pdf>); Bruneton's sea goes from geometry to normals to a slope variance in the BRDF as a wave shrinks under a pixel (<https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/~jaroslav/teaching/2012-npgr031/prezentace/Realtime%20Realistic%20Ocean%20Lighting.pdf>). The sea does exactly that; the ground's noise fades through its tile's coarser copies.
- **The sea's slopes.** Cox and Munk: mean square slope 0.003 + 0.00512 U (<https://oceanopticsbook.info/view/surfaces/cox-munk-sea-surface-slope-statistics>): 0.029 at 5.1 m/s, which the wave trains, the ripples and the unseen capillaries are set to add up to (derived). Tessendorf's FFT sea and its Jacobian foam (<https://jtessen.people.clemson.edu/reports/papers_files/coursenotes2004.pdf>) were not built: ten summed trains with trochoid-sharpened crests (GPU Gems 1, <https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models>) cost nothing to store.
- **Whitecaps** cover 3.84e-6 U^3.41 of the sea (Monahan and O'Muircheartaigh, in <https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/16/13725/2016/acp-16-13725-2016.pdf>): a thousandth at 5 m/s, so they are few.
- **Depth colour.** Pure water absorbs 0.006 /m at 440 nm, 0.057 at 550, 0.33 at 650 (Pope and Fry, <https://omlc.org/spectra/water/data/pope97.txt>): red goes in metres, green in tens. Crest drives shallows and shore foam from one depth cache (<https://crest.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user/shallows-and-shorelines.html>); here the depth is ETOPO 2022's and, off the site's beach, the web build's shelving bed and bar. Flat water mirrors 2 % squarely (<https://www.oceanopticsbook.info/view/surfaces/the-level-sea-surface>); Bruneton and Dupuy's mean Fresnel for a rough sea (<https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jdupuy/whitecaps/master/ocean.glsl>) is why the far sea is leaned toward the eye before the engine lights it.
- **The Cape.** Coastal strand: salt-pruned saw palmetto with dwarfed cabbage palm behind the sea oats' dune (FNAI 2010, <https://www.fnai.org/PDFs/NC/Coastal_Strand_Final_2010.pdf>). Ridges and swales: scrub on the drained ridges, marsh in the swales, sand mixed with shell (Schmalzer and Foster 2020, <https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205006143/downloads/Schmalzer&Foster_JTorreyBotSoc_2020.pdf>); about fifty beach ridges, one in 80 years (Rink and Forrest 2005, in <https://people.wou.edu/~taylors/g322/articles/Rink_lopez_2010_OSL_dunes_FLA.pdf>). The shoals have 5 to 11 ft over them and break only in a heavy sea (Coast Pilot 4, <https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/coast-pilot/files/cp4/CPB4_C10_WEB.pdf>). Photographs: <https://ccspacemuseum.org/facilities/launch-complex-5-6/>; the nearest aerial flight to 1961 is 1958 (<https://www.uflib.ufl.edu/aerials>). Not read: the Schmalzer and Hinkle station memoranda; no aerial was looked at for this work.

## Done, and what is simplified

- **Which water is the sea** is worked out from the shore map (`earth/coast.rs`): the largest body that is wide everywhere. Beaches, surf and dunes face it; marsh and still, stained water are a lagoon's. An inlet over about 470 m lets the sea in. **Derived.**
- **The ridges** run along lines of equal distance from the open sea's shore, 110 m apart (`ground.ts`; Rink and Forrest's spacing is unconfirmed). They are drawn in what grows, not raised: **the ground is a sphere and has no relief of its own**, only shaded lumps.
- **The made ground** (ways, clearings, paving) is handed over by the game as `earth::marks::Marks`. Handed none, the 290 m round the site is taken as cleared, which is wrong for any real complex: the roads, the fence line and the worn sand are only drawn once the game hands them over.
- **Plants** stand where their painted crowns are (`earth/flora.rs` repeats the shader's rules; keep them in step), out to 400 m, grass to 150 m. Which crown is a palmetto, an oak or a palm, and one palm to 200 crowns, are estimated. They throw no shadow of their own (the shader paints one), do not move in the wind, and take the coarse distance to the shore, so at a site within 300 m of a beach they would part from the paint by tens of metres.
- **The Sun on the sea.** The waves that can be seen carry their own glints; of the slope that cannot, most is drawn as one facet to a patch of sea a pixel across, its tilt taken from Cox and Munk's bell and held a quarter of a second, so the glint sparkles near the eye and is the smooth patch of an orbital photograph once a pixel covers 6 m (estimated). It is the engine's own light on the engine's own lobe, so it follows the clock and bloom takes it; a lobe of GGX has a longer tail than Cox and Munk's Gaussian (the web build's `GLINT_GLSL` says by how much), which is not corrected here.
- **The cumulus' shadows** are read from the clouds' own field (`clouds::field::sheet`, `lay`), by the cloud shader's own rule (`clouds.wesl` `in_shadow`): keep the two in step. The Earth bakes a second copy of that sheet.
- **Measuring.** Three switches, each a constant to set and put back: `LAND_DETAIL` and `SEA_DETAIL` at the top of `earth.wesl` (a shader: no rebuild; the part is drawn as a flat tone) and `PLANTS` in `earth/grove.rs` (rebuild). Run `--bench` with each off in turn against all on, in the same sitting.
- **Lagoon and lake beds are not known**: one shallow tone. **The surface wind** is taken to be the cumulus's drift, and the swell (0.96 m, 6.55 s) is a constant in `sea.wesl`, not yet handed over with the day. **Sea ice** is not drawn. **Out past where a float can count centimetres** (hundreds of km from the site) the sea's ripples are coarse.
