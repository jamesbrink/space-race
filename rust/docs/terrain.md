# The ground, the sea and the globe: what was read, and what was done with it

`sr-render/src/earth/` and `assets/shaders/earth*.wesl`, `sea.wesl`. Every colour, density and proportion is **estimated** unless a line says otherwise.

## Read

- **No tiling.** Heitz and Neyret blend three randomly placed patches a triangle and restore the histogram (<https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/722-2/>); Mikkelsen's hex tiling does it with no lookup table (<https://jcgt.org/published/0011/03/05/paper-lowres.pdf>). Here nothing is a photograph, so one small tile of gradient noise with its slopes is read at a different turn and shift for each use (`earth/grain.rs`), and what is scattered is hashed from whole numbers.
- **Layers by height, bumps from slopes.** Height-blended splatting, `max(h1 + a1, h2 + a2) - depth` (<https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/advanced-terrain-texture-splatting>); a normal from a height's screen derivatives (Mikkelsen, <https://jcgt.org/published/0009/03/04/paper-lowres.pdf>). The noise tile carries its own slopes, so the normal is a sum of them; a hollow is darkened and shut off from the sky.
- **Far and near.** Arma's terrain is a satellite picture with a mask choosing detail surfaces near the eye and grass geometry to about 125 m (<https://gist.github.com/pennyworth12345/27786cf99652e0018b9b2f0becaa0638>, <https://sites.google.com/site/islandconfigs/cfgworlds-overview/cfgworlds-clutter>); Unreal goes from detail to macro textures with distance (<https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/landscape-materials-in-unreal-engine>). Here: Blue Marble and the region's 450 m colour far off, the designed ground near, plants as geometry to 400 m.
- **Lost detail becomes roughness.** Toksvig and Kaplanyan's filters (<https://blog.selfshadow.com/2011/07/22/specular-showdown/>, <https://www.jp.square-enix.com/tech/library/pdf/ImprovedGeometricSpecularAA.pdf>); Bruneton's sea goes from geometry to normals to a slope variance in the BRDF as a wave shrinks under a pixel (<https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/~jaroslav/teaching/2012-npgr031/prezentace/Realtime%20Realistic%20Ocean%20Lighting.pdf>). The sea does exactly that; the ground's noise fades through its tile's coarser copies.
- **The sea's slopes.** Cox and Munk: mean square slope 0.003 + 0.00512 U (<https://oceanopticsbook.info/view/surfaces/cox-munk-sea-surface-slope-statistics>): 0.029 at 5.1 m/s, which the wave trains, the ripples and the unseen capillaries are set to add up to (derived). Tessendorf's FFT sea and its Jacobian foam (<https://jtessen.people.clemson.edu/reports/papers_files/coursenotes2004.pdf>) were not built: ten summed trains with trochoid-sharpened crests (GPU Gems 1, <https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models>) cost nothing to store.
- **Whitecaps** cover 3.84e-6 U^3.41 of the sea (Monahan and O'Muircheartaigh, in <https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/16/13725/2016/acp-16-13725-2016.pdf>): a thousandth at 5 m/s, so they are few.
- **Depth colour.** Pure water absorbs 0.006 /m at 440 nm, 0.057 at 550, 0.33 at 650 (Pope and Fry, <https://omlc.org/spectra/water/data/pope97.txt>): red goes in metres, green in tens. Crest drives shallows and shore foam from one depth cache (<https://crest.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user/shallows-and-shorelines.html>); here the depth is ETOPO 2022's and, off the site's beach, the web build's shelving bed and bar. Flat water mirrors 2 % squarely (<https://www.oceanopticsbook.info/view/surfaces/the-level-sea-surface>); Bruneton and Dupuy's mean Fresnel for a rough sea (<https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jdupuy/whitecaps/master/ocean.glsl>) is why the far sea is leaned toward the eye before the engine lights it.
- **The Cape.** Coastal strand: salt-pruned saw palmetto with dwarfed cabbage palm behind the sea oats' dune (FNAI 2010, <https://www.fnai.org/PDFs/NC/Coastal_Strand_Final_2010.pdf>). Ridges and swales: scrub on the drained ridges, marsh in the swales, sand mixed with shell (Schmalzer and Foster 2020, <https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205006143/downloads/Schmalzer&Foster_JTorreyBotSoc_2020.pdf>); about fifty beach ridges, one in 80 years (Rink and Forrest 2005, in <https://people.wou.edu/~taylors/g322/articles/Rink_lopez_2010_OSL_dunes_FLA.pdf>). The shoals have 5 to 11 ft over them and break only in a heavy sea (Coast Pilot 4, <https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/coast-pilot/files/cp4/CPB4_C10_WEB.pdf>). Photographs: <https://ccspacemuseum.org/facilities/launch-complex-5-6/>; the nearest aerial flight to 1961 is 1958 (<https://www.uflib.ufl.edu/aerials>). Not read: the Schmalzer and Hinkle station memoranda. The aerial looked at is NASA's Missile Row of 13 November 1964 (`docs/sources.md`): the ground from the air is drawn to it.
- **The Sun on the sea.** Cox and Munk (1954): slopes Gaussian with the mean square above, so the facets' normals are a Beckmann distribution; Walter and others 2007 for its masking; Zirr and Kaplanyan 2016 ("Real-time Rendering of Procedural Multiscale Materials") for drawing each pixel's count of flashing facets as a Poisson number about its mean. Water's reflectance squarely 2.05 % (n = 1.334).

## Done, and what is simplified

- **Which water is the sea** is worked out from the shore map (`earth/coast.rs`): the largest body that is wide everywhere. Beaches, surf and dunes face it; marsh and still, stained water are a lagoon's. An inlet over about 470 m lets the sea in. **Derived.**
- **The ridges** run along lines of equal distance from the open sea's shore, 110 m apart (`ground.ts`; Rink and Forrest's spacing is unconfirmed); each is its own width and strength and breaks off and starts again, so from the air they are faint curving lines, not a ruled pattern. The openings in the scrub come at three sizes (tens, hundreds of metres, a few metres) in drifts; old burns kilometres across are lower and paler; the scrub's tone is clumped with each clump's shadow thrown away from the Sun; verges are cut back 10 to 20 m along every way. All **estimated**, against the 1964 aerial.
- **Relief** (`earth/relief.rs`) is raised as geometry within 2 km of the site, on a mesh a few metres fine there (`earth/mesh.rs`): the beach rising 0.8 m to its berm, a foredune 1.5 to 4.5 m high about 60 m in (steep to the sea, broken by blowouts), hummocks behind it, and the ridges 0.8 m over the swales; made ground is flat. Every height is **estimated** (no survey read). Beyond 2 km the ground is a sphere.
- **The made ground** (ways, clearings, paving) is handed over by the game as `earth::marks::Marks`. Handed none, the 290 m round the site is taken as cleared, which is wrong for any real complex: the roads, the fence line and the worn sand are only drawn once the game hands them over.
- **Plants** stand where their painted crowns are (`earth/flora.rs` repeats the shader's rules; keep them in step), on the relief: the scrub out to 400 m, taller clumps and palms from 250 m to 1.6 km so the skyline is broken, grass tufts (three to six bent blades, their tips drier) to 150 m and a sparer far form to 320 m (`earth/sow.rs`). Shrubs are lobed and rounded. They throw shadows (the engine's), and the cumulus' shadows fall on them as on the ground (`shaders/earth_plants.wesl`). Which crown is which, every proportion, and each kind's tone against the painted scrub's are **estimated**. They nod down the wind on the flight's clock (`shaders/earth_sway.wesl`: a tip a metre up leans 5 cm at 7 m/s and 8 cm more in a gust 45 m across; **estimated**), in the shadow and motion passes too. They are also stood round the stretch of open beach nearest the site (`grove.rs` `places`: scrub to 700 m, grass to 500 m round it), where the dune's grass is sea oats, 2.5 times taller and straw-pale. Elsewhere, beyond those, the plants are only painted. Loose sand (beach, dune, strand) is wind-rippled at 10 cm, blown paler and darker in patches, and darker in damp lows. The relief has nothing finer than about 15 m, so the mesh can follow it.
- **The Sun on the sea** (`shaders/earth_glint.wesl`) is worked apart from the engine's lobe: Beckmann with masking and water's Fresnel for its mean, and the count of flashing facets in each patch of sea (a power of two across, fixed to the sea) drawn as a Poisson number about its mean every quarter second and faded from one draw to the next, so a flash lasts several frames and the temporal filter keeps it. A facet is taken as 1.2 cm across and curved enough to send the Sun back over 0.01 sr (**estimated**: this sets how dense the sparkle is); where a patch holds over 400 flashing facets the mean is drawn. The Sun's light is the engine's own (its transmittance through the air, its shadow map, the cloud's shadow); a flash is held to 60 times exposed white so bloom takes it without a firefly. Far sea is leaned toward the eye before it is lit (Bruneton and Dupuy). Measured against the engine's GGX lobe it no longer has GGX's long tail. Where a pixel covers more than about a metre of sea the glint is lifted, up to three times at 25 m and more (`HIGH_UP`): not physical, so that under the haze and the print it reads as the bright patch of orbital photographs (**estimated**).
- **The sea from the air**: the swell runs toward 250 degrees (**assumed**: the Cape's east-north-easterly swell) and is handed to `surf` within 120 to 300 m of a beach; windrows (slicks 22 m apart, hundreds long) lie along the wind; whitecaps are specks a few metres across, born, spreading and fading over six seconds, more under a gust; the inshore water is a pale green band over the bar going to deep blue over 5 to 20 m of depth; the open sea varies in tone over kilometres. Every size and tone is **estimated**.
- **The cumulus' shadows** are read from the clouds' own field by the clouds' own function (`clouds_cover.wesl` `sun_shadow`, imported by `earth_frame.wesl`), with the day's map of the cover far out, so they cannot part from the clouds. The Earth bakes a second copy of the sheet.
- **Measuring.** `SR_EARTH_AB=<seconds>` with `--bench` turns the land's detail, the sea's detail and glint, and the plants off and on by turns within one run, logging each turn (`earth/ab.rs`; `SR_EARTH_AB_PARTS` picks any of `l`, `s`, `p`); compare each turn with the mean of the turns either side, so the machine's drift cancels. The constants `LAND_DETAIL`, `SEA_DETAIL`, `GLINT` (`earth.wesl`) and `PLANTS` (`grove.rs`) take a part out of the build. To keep the cost down: the noise the cover is cut from (sixteen fields, `flora::fields`) is baked once at startup on the worker threads (`earth/bake.rs`, about 3 s) into two levels round the site, 6 m texels over 6 km and 48 m over 49 km, the wide level holding the mean of any field finer than two of its texels (so beyond 3 km the smallest openings and the oats' pattern are their mean), and read in four fetches; within 25 to 45 m of the eye, and beyond the bake, it is worked out as before. Near the eye the painted grass is clumped from the noise and no longer searched tuft by tuft (the tufts stand as plants round the site and the beach). Noise octaves under a pixel are their mean and are not read (`earth_noise.wesl`), a scrub crown is searched for only once it is three pixels across, the made ground's patches are not looked at past their reach, and the shrubs fade out from 0.9 to 1.2 km from the eye.
- **Lagoon and lake beds are not known**: one shallow tone. **The surface wind** is taken to be the cumulus's drift, and the swell (0.96 m, 6.55 s) is a constant in `sea.wesl`, not yet handed over with the day. **Sea ice** is not drawn. **Out past where a float can count centimetres** (hundreds of km from the site) the sea's ripples are coarse.
