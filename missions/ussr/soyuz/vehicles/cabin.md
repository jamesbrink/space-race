# Soyuz 1 descent cabin

The [Gagarin Cosmonaut Training Center's 7K-OK section](https://www.gctc.ru/main.php?id=299)
shows the Kazbek couch (item 5), the descent capsule (6), and the optical sight (16). It gives
the type's descent capsule as 2,160 mm long and 2,200 mm across, with 2.5 m³ of free volume.
The model takes those dimensions for No. 4. The cream lining and the relationship between the couches
and the instrument panel are read off that cutaway. The lining thickness, seams, bracket geometry,
and colours are estimated. Its window openings follow the model's descent hull.

Vladimir Komarov flew alone. The plan was for two cosmonauts to enter during an EVA from the second
Soyuz and return with him. His radio calls refer to both side portholes and to changing couches during
flight. The model places Komarov on the middle couch in a resting pose, with the two return seats
empty. The exact occupancy at each point in the flight, the couch dimensions and posture are
estimated. The outboard liners are shortened to fit the hull. [Flight transcript, Russian State Archive
of Scientific and Technical Documentation, no. 131-2, track 2, pp. 32 and 35](https://imwerden.de/pdf/istorichesky_archiv_2002_2__ocr.pdf)

Komarov's figure reuses the Voskhod light flight suit and headset as a reconstruction. The
photograph of Komarov training in a 7K-OK cabin from the RKK Energia archive and the April 1967
RGANTD commander portrait show the headset. The shared figure is a generic face at an estimated
1.70 m stature, with wool, shoes, cloth and headset details estimated or borrowed from the Voskhod
reconstruction. [RKK Energia training photo in *Earth and Universe*, 2020 no. 4, p. 99](https://epizodyspace.ru/bibl/zemlya_i_vselennaya/2020/4/94-108.pdf)
[RGANTD portrait, no. 0-151 ЦВ, p. 34](https://imwerden.de/pdf/istorichesky_archiv_2002_2__ocr.pdf)

## Sirius-7K

Yuri Tyapchenko, one of the developers, dates the first crewed flight of Sirius to Soyuz 1. The
centre board and two command-and-signal panels follow his illustrated Sirius-7K type. The
left-hand INK is a mechanical navigation globe used to show position and predict a landing area.
The four-column signal bank sits to its right. Next comes the recessed VKU cathode-ray screen,
which presented cabin and docking television as well as selected measurements through a scale
overlay. The model uses the published 84 × 84 mm active screen area. The program indicator beside
it showed timed commands, and the digital-information block sat above the cutout on the far
right. Across the lower board are the voltage/current meter, cabin gauge, clock/stopwatch,
guarded commands, range and closing-speed indicator, and volume controls.

The left and right KSU command panels have a system-selection column, a window onto a rotating
command-label drum, and paired numbered buttons. This arrangement let the crew select a system
and reuse the same numbered controls to issue its commands. The photographic reference shows the
red safety keys and drum window. Board dimensions apart from the screen area, exact legends,
switch positions, colours and mountings are reconstructed by eye. The screen is blank, the
program display and gauges are illustrative, and the globe has a fixed orientation. This is the
published 7K-OK type layout, with no serial-specific panel photograph for Soyuz 1 found.
[Yuri Tyapchenko, Sirius technical history, pp. 1–4, 11–13, 16–18, 21 and 29–32](https://www.cosmoworld.ru/spaceencyclopedia/publications/soyuz7.pdf)

The lamp's reach is baked against the modelled interior and window openings. The closed transfer hatch has an estimated inward face and handle. The forward passage, optical
sight internals, and equipment inside the parachute bays are not reconstructed.
