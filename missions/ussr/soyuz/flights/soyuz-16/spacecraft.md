# Soyuz 16 · 7K-TM No. 73

NPO Energia prepared this 11F615A12 spacecraft for the Apollo–Soyuz Test Project (ЭПАС).
The launch register identifies it as No. 73-EPSA. It was the first crewed spacecraft of this
variant and carried a simulator ring for tests that the joint flight would perform with
Apollo's docking module. [McDowell, *General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects*, R-7 launch log](https://www.planet4589.org/space/gcat/data/launch/R-7.html) ·
[NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, §1.9.3](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) ·
[NASA, *Soviet Rehearsal for Apollo-Soyuz*](https://www.nasa.gov/history/45-years-ago-soviet-rehearsal-for-apollo-soyuz/)

## Evidence and model

| Item | Record and reconstruction |

| Soyuz-U launch stack | The launch register gives Soyuz-U 11A511U K 15000-014. The model adopts the 49,300 mm height of the earlier Soyuz stack NASA uses to illustrate the approximate ASTP launch configuration and carries over the 10,412 mm rudder span of the earlier 11A511. Its fairing and escape tower use the flown Soyuz 16 launch photograph as a guide. The upper fairing shoulder is 135 mm higher than the inherited 11A511 model to illustrate the added clearance for APAS. The boosters, core and upper stage use the shared R-7 geometry. | APAS-75 | The peripheral mechanism had an open passage through its centre and three outward-facing guides around its capture ring. Either side could take the active role and extend its ring for initial capture. The movable ring could shift to align the latches with the catches, and the attenuators absorbed energy as the spacecraft came together. The active unit then drew the collars together. The gallery reconstructs the collar, ring and closed hatch from the joint-mission drawings. [NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, §1.9.2, fig. 1-22](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) |
| Apollo simulator ring | The extra ring rode on the docking port. The crew worked the standard release modes and finally exercised the emergency release, sending the ring clear on 7 December. The model estimates the ring's proportions and fittings from the surviving onboard footage and portrays its flight away as an axial slide. The slider also illustrates extension of the native capture ring for inspection. The shapes and locations of its cabin hardware, panel legends, readings and individual likenesses are estimated. ## What the model simplifies

- The launcher below the fairing shares earlier R-7 geometry. Small variant-specific differences
  in tanks, engines, wiring, markings and attachment hardware are estimated or omitted.
- The ring motion omits latch cycling, spring hardware and the other docking test sequences.
  Ring travel, panel deployment and the spread between spacecraft modules are for inspection.
- The orbital room's interior equipment, docking control signals and atmospheric changes are
  outside the exhibit. The cabin controls and suit installation represent an estimated layout.
- The launch stack and orbital configurations are available in the Gallery. The pad on the day
  of launch and the flown trajectory are outside this reconstruction.
