# Soyuz 10 · 7K-OKS No. 31

TsKBEM adapted the original Soyuz for ferry flights to its long-duration orbital station.
The first station ferry still used three couches and solar arrays. Its new active docking
assembly added a pressure hatch and a collar that could seal against Salyut's port. The
production designation was 11F615A8. Soviet histories also call this early version 7K-T.
[NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, §1.7](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) ·
[Siddiqi, *Challenge to Apollo*, appendix Table IA, p. 874](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4408pt2.pdf#page=375)

## Evidence and model

| Item | Record and reconstruction |
| --- | --- |
| Launcher | Kh15000-025 in the launch catalogue. The model assumes the early 11A511 envelope, with 49,913 mm from the main-nozzle plane to the escape tower tip and 10,412 mm across the booster rudders. The period launch film provides a silhouette check. The shroud and fine fittings use the shared reconstruction. [11A511 launch catalogue](https://space.skyrocket.de/doc_lau_det/soyuz.htm) · [NASA, *Launch of Salyut*, links to the launch film](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/50-years-ago-launch-of-salyut-the-worlds-first-space-station/) · [Launcher drawing and dimensions](../../vehicles/soyuz-11a511.md) |
| Spacecraft envelope | Portree's figure 1-18 guides the exterior. The model uses approximately 7,500 mm from the aft nozzle plane to the probe tip, a 2,720 mm maximum body diameter and 2,650 mm from the descent-module hatch datum to the forward docking face. The Czech catalogue gives 6,980 mm overall and a 3,290 mm orbital module. Neither source defines the length datums for those figures. The descent module follows the catalogue's 2,160 mm length and 2,200 mm diameter. [NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, §1.7 and fig. 1-18](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) · [Czech Academy of Sciences catalogue, 1971-034A](https://www.lib.cas.cz/space.40/1971/034A.HTM) |
| Docking mechanism and hatch | For a complete docking, the active probe first caught the station's passive cone. Pulling the probe in brought the two collars into contact to latch and seal the joint. The probe and drogue could then be moved to clear a passage through the hatches. Soyuz 10 achieved the first step. The exhibit reconstructs the approach pose and the ring, latches and hatch, with the sealed hatch modelled closed. The 808 mm clear passage in the model is an estimate from later hardware. Portree's fig. 1-19 depicts the later Mir-era system and informs the mechanism's general form. [Kamanin's diary, 25 April](https://militera.lib.ru/db/kamanin_np/71.html) · [NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, figs. 1-18 and 1-19](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) |
| Rendezvous and solar power | Igla measured the approach to Salyut. Its reconstructed booms follow the type drawing and reuse early Soyuz hinge geometry. The model keeps an 8,370 mm deployed solar span from the Czech catalogue. Portree gives 10 m for the Salyut 1 type. The arrays could supplement Salyut's electrical supply after docking. Stowed geometry, hinges and antenna travel are estimates. [Kamanin's diary, 25 April](https://militera.lib.ru/db/kamanin_np/71.html) · [Czech Academy catalogue](https://www.lib.cas.cz/space.40/1971/034A.HTM) · [NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, §1.7](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) |
| Instrument and propulsion module | The Gagarin Cosmonaut Training Center describes the move of Igla electronics into the orbital module and the deletion of a command radio link, which freed engineers to remove the aft toroidal equipment compartment. The model omits that compartment and reconstructs the engine and other service fittings from the early Soyuz type. [GCTC, 7K-T type description and cutaway](https://www.gctc.ru/main.php?id=300) |
| Descent cabin and crew | The three cosmonauts flew in soft clothing with communications headgear, without pressure suits. The centre seat represents commander Vladimir Shatalov. The flight engineer, Aleksey Yeliseyev, and test engineer, Nikolay Rukavishnikov, flank him in an arrangement informed by an Energia simulator photo. Their exact in-flight side assignments and the details of their clothing and poses are estimated. Cockpit designer Yuri Tyapchenko identifies Sirius-A8 as a development of Sirius-7K. The gallery uses the early Sirius layouts as a starting point for the globe, CRT and command panels and reconstructs the controls and instrument faces. ## What is simplified

- The Gallery depicts Soyuz 10 on its own with its docking system arranged for approach.
  The flight record describes Salyut 1, the failure and the release.
- The slider uses illustrative fairing separation, array and antenna deployment, and module
  travel. Rendezvous and recovery are recorded in the timeline. Recovery hardware is
  represented by its exterior fittings.
- Hull stations, thermal blanket seams and colour, antenna fit, propulsion fittings,
  hatch seals, individual cabin details and instrument states are reconstructed. The pressure
  hatch and docking probe are fixed for inspection, and the orbital room has illustrative stowage.
