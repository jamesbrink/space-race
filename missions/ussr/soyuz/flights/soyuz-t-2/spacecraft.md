# Soyuz T-2 · 7K-ST No. 7L

NPO Energia’s Soyuz-T replaced the two-person 7K-T ferry with a ship that could launch
three cosmonauts in pressure suits. No. 7L was the first 11F732 spacecraft to carry a crew.
Malyshev and Aksyonov tested its solar arrays, digital guidance and docking at Salyut 6.
[NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, §1.12](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) ·
[Czech Academy of Sciences Space Catalogue, 1980-045A](https://lib.cas.cz/space.40/1980/045A.HTM)

## Evidence and model

| Item | Record and reconstruction |
| --- | --- |
| Soyuz-U launch stack | The launch log identifies booster Zh15000-196. The model adopts Energia’s Soyuz-U type figures with Soyuz-T aboard, 51,100 mm overall height and 10,300 mm maximum width. The core, side boosters and Block I adapt shared R-7 geometry. The fairing and escape tower are reconstructed from the Soyuz-T launch profile. Energia describes an extra motor above the main tower motor and two pairs of motors on the shroud for escape after tower jettison. [McDowell, R-7 launch log](https://www.planet4589.org/space/gcat/data/launch/R-7.html) · [RKK Energia 1946–1996, ch. 6](https://testpilot.ru/espace/bibl/energia-50/06.php) |
| Spacecraft envelope | The catalogue gives 6,980 mm overall length, 2,720 mm maximum diameter, a descent module 2,140 mm long and 2,200 mm in diameter, and launch mass of 6,850 kg. The length datums are unspecified. The model measures 6,980 mm from the aft plane to the probe tip and 2,140 mm from the capsule heat shield to the forward hatch. It reconstructs the visible fittings. [Czech Academy of Sciences Space Catalogue, 1980-045A](https://lib.cas.cz/space.40/1980/045A.HTM) |
| Docking and rendezvous | Soyuz-T retained Igla rendezvous radar and an active probe for the station’s passive drogue. The ring around the probe sealed the passage into the station. The gallery reconstructs the probe, forward hatch, collar and exposed antenna hardware from the type drawings. A TASS image of the spacecraft in orbit guides the estimated length and angle of the deployed Igla truss. [NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, §1.12.2 and fig. 1-28](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) · [Vasilyev, Soyuz-T training account, fig. 2](https://www.gctc.ru/media/files/Periodicheskie_izdaniya/ppk_2021_4_total_41/9_stat.a_vasil.ev.pdf) · [Baturin (ed.), TASS photograph, p. 289](https://epizodsspace.airbase.ru/bibl/mpk/mpk.pdf) |
| Descent cabin | The two cosmonauts flew in Sokol KV-2 suits on the first flight of that suit design. The model reconstructs three couch positions with the third unoccupied. Seat positions, suit fittings, equipment stowage and personal likenesses are estimated. [Vasilyev, Soyuz-T training account, figs. 9–10](https://www.gctc.ru/media/files/Periodicheskie_izdaniya/ppk_2021_4_total_41/9_stat.a_vasil.ev.pdf) · [Skoog and Abramov, *The Soviet-Russian Spacesuit*](https://epizodsspace.airbase.ru/bibl/inostr-yazyki/iaa/2011/skoog_abramov_the_soviet-russian_spacesuit.pdf) |
| Neptun and Argon-16 | The new Neptun information and command console used matrix command fields and an electronic display for Argon-16 data while keeping separate instruments. The triply redundant computer was mounted in the aft instrument compartment. The panel is reconstructed from Soyuz-T references with illustrative readings. [Tiapchenko, *Information Display Systems for the Mir Space Station and the Soyuz Transport Ship*, fig. 2](https://web.mit.edu/slava/space/essays/essay-tiapchenko5.htm) · [Vasilyev, Soyuz-T training account, fig. 4](https://www.gctc.ru/media/files/Periodicheskie_izdaniya/ppk_2021_4_total_41/9_stat.a_vasil.ev.pdf) · [Przhiyalkovsky interview, MIT Soviet and Russian Space History Project](https://web.mit.edu/slava/space/interview/interview-przhiyalkovsky.htm) |
| Solar power and propulsion | The documented deployed span is 10,600 mm. Each modeled wing has four leaves, with the 1,290 mm chord estimated from the type drawing. The wings folded against the spacecraft for ascent and supplied its electrical power in orbit. The engine and attitude thrusters shared stores of nitrogen tetroxide and UDMH. Vasilyev gives the main engine thrust as 315 kgf (about 3.09 kN), while the Czech catalogue gives 4.1 kN. The geometry of individual hinges, nozzles and fittings is reconstructed. ## What the model simplifies

- The launcher shares earlier R-7 geometry below the fairing. Variant-specific piping,
  fasteners and other fine fittings are estimated or omitted.
- The slider illustrates opening, unfolding and module separation, with distances and timing
  chosen for inspection. On the way home the orbital module went first, before the braking burn.
- The probe is shown for inspection. Capture mechanics, the station drogue and the flown
  approach are outside the exhibit.
- The Neptun controls represent the installation and its visible instruments. They have no live
  electronics. The service module represents the outer propulsion and instrument hardware.
