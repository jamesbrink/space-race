# Soyuz 3 · 7K-OK(A) No. 10

No. 10 kept the active probe and the three-compartment design of No. 4. Beregovoy flew alone.
The uncrewed target, Soyuz 2 No. 11, carried the passive cone. Energia's post-accident test
programme included two uncrewed automatic dockings and a four-day test of a single ship.
The Soyuz 3 gallery uses the shared [early 7K-OK geometry](../soyuz-1/spacecraft.md) and
[11A511 launcher](../../vehicles/soyuz-11a511.md). The flight-specific differences follow below.
[Energia corporate history, pp. 157–195](https://testpilot.ru/espace/bibl/energia-50/05.php)

## Evidence and model

| Item | Record and reconstruction |

The 49,913 mm stack height and shared fairing and stage envelopes are taken from the 11A511 drawing used for Soyuz 1. Period launch and boarding images show the early escape tower and fairing. The 620 mm assignment to the probe and the individual hull stations are estimates. The 2,720 mm maximum body diameter and the orbital module's 2,200 mm diameter and 300 mm belt follow the type references. [GCTC](https://www.gctc.ru/main.php?id=299) · [Energia](https://testpilot.ru/espace/bibl/energia-50/05.php) |
| Descent module | The model uses the GCTC type's 2,200 mm diameter and 2,160 mm length. A museum association catalogue lists the surviving object at 2,300 × 2,240 mm; it gives no measurement stations and repeats those dimensions for Zond 5 and Soyuz T-3. The Energia display photograph confirms the bell profile and recessed porthole. The recovered finish and exposed equipment are post-entry evidence. [AMNIT catalogue](https://amnit.org/index/virtualnyj-muzej-pnt-kosmonavtika/0-43) · [Photograph of recovered No. 10](https://kpopov.ru/cosmos/rkk_demo_zal/soyuz-3/img_2836.jpg) |
| Solar wings | Ground tracking confirmed panel deployment after insertion. Beregovoy later described turning their plane towards sunlight to supply the electrical system. Both arrays are shown extended partway along the gallery slider. Leaf dimensions, hinges, cells and motion remain the estimates described for No. 4. [Tracking account in Zak](https://www.russianspaceweb.com/soyuz3-soyuz2-rendezvous.html) · [Beregovoy, *Three Heights*, flight chapter](https://militera.lib.ru/memo/russian/beregovoy/03.html) |
| Rendezvous equipment | The active ship had Igla radio equipment and a forward probe. Kamanin records two steady upper lamps and two flashing lower lamps on both ships. The model places four lamps around the orbital module's forward shoulder, facing the target. Their exact mounting stations, optics and brackets are estimated. [GCTC cutaway](https://www.gctc.ru/main.php?id=299) · [Kamanin, 29 October](https://militera.lib.ru/db/kamanin_np/68.html) |
| Orientation and recovery | The infrared local-vertical sensor had been added to 7K-OK starting with No. 7 and was used during No. 10's return. The external housing on the side of the instrument and propulsion module follows callout 14 in the type drawing, with its placement and shape estimated. The 45K star sensor still failed to find a reference star during an orbital test. After the Soyuz 1 inquiry, Energia describes an enlarged, polished, conical parachute container and a separate provision to release the drogue in an emergency. No. 10's parachute and soft-landing motors worked. [Energia corporate history](https://testpilot.ru/espace/bibl/energia-50/05.php) · [GCTC cutaway](https://www.gctc.ru/main.php?id=299) · [Kamanin, 28 and 30 October](https://militera.lib.ru/db/kamanin_np/68.html) |
| Orbital room | Beregovoy worked, rested and gave television reports from this room. He recalled a couch opposite a worktable and a sideboard with a mahogany-like finish. The model includes those and a portable cine camera. Furniture dimensions, finish, mounting and camera stowage are reconstructed. [Beregovoy, *Three Heights*, chapter 3, after p. 239](https://militera.lib.ru/memo/russian/beregovoy/03.html) · [Balashova collection, Moscow Museum of Cosmonautics](https://kosmo-museum.ru/news/sovetskiy-kosmicheskiy-arhitektor-galina-balashova-peredala-v-fondy-muzeya-kollektsiyu-svoih-rabot) |

The in-flight colour image shows a dark garment, pale headset, and two microphones with coloured tips. The model adapts the seated figure from the earlier Soyuz and uses the Sirius-7K type drawing for the static panel. ## What is simplified

- The gallery slider shows illustrative separation and deployment. It does not run the rendezvous
  or reconstruct the target ship, the two ships' relative attitude, or the timing of the lamps.
- Most type details carry over from No. 4, including the stations of the modules, the folded
  solar assemblies, the antenna mechanisms and the major exterior fittings. The covers suggest
  the recovery system. The revised chutes, deployment mechanism and landing motors are not
  simulated.
- No serial-specific cockpit installation drawing was found. The individual suit tailoring, cabin
  controllers, couch liners, stowage and orbital-room arrangements are reconstructed. The seated
  figure is a generic face with a 1.80 m stature, following Beregovoy's account of his fit in
  the spacecraft as reported in Siddiqi's *Challenge to Apollo*, p. 661.
