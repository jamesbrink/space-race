# Soyuz 12 · 7K-T No. 37

The crew of Soyuz 12 tested the redesigned TsKBEM station ferry on a short independent flight.
The spacecraft is identified as No. 37 (37L in Karfidov) through the records of its assembly and
checkout. Siddiqi's narrative and appendix give No. 36. The exhibit follows the more recent
serial history and uses production designation 11F615A8. [Zak, *Soyuz 12*](https://russianspaceweb.com/soyuz12.html) ·
[Karfidov, *Cosmonautics of the USSR and Russia*, vol. 6, flight 113](https://epizodyspace.ru/bibl/karfidov/karfidov-t6-2021.pdf) ·
[Siddiqi, *Challenge to Apollo*, p. 816 and appendix Table IA](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4408pt2.pdf)

## Evidence and model

| Item | Record and reconstruction |
| --- | --- |
| Launch stack | The launch register gives 11A511 S15000-026. The model assumes the early 11A511 envelope, with a nominal 49,913 mm from the main-nozzle plane to the escape tower tip and 10,412 mm across the booster rudders. Soyuz 12 introduced a lighter escape tower with its lower aerodynamic casing removed. The model estimates its narrower outline and reconstructs the exposed supports and fairing details. [McDowell, General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects, R-7 launch log](https://www.planet4589.org/space/gcat/data/launch/R-7.html) · [Narimanov, 1976 escape tower drawing reproduced by Zak](https://www.russianspaceweb.com/soyuz_sas.html) · [Launcher drawing and dimensions](../../vehicles/soyuz-11a511.md) |
| Spacecraft envelope | Portree gives about 7,500 mm total length, a 2,720 mm maximum body diameter and 2,200 mm habitable-module diameter for the battery-powered ferry type. The model measures length from the aft nozzle plane to the probe tip. The descent module length is 2,160 mm. The 640 mm side hatch is carried over from the earlier Soyuz. The Czech catalogue gives a 6,980 mm total length without specifying measurement datums. The flight catalogue gives a mass of approximately 6,570 kg. [NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, §1.8.1 and fig. 1-20](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) · [GCTC, 7K-OK dimensions](https://www.gctc.ru/main.php?id=299) · [Czech Academy of Sciences catalogue, 1973-067A](https://www.lib.cas.cz/space.40/1973/067A.HTM) |
| Forward docking interface | Period drawings cited in the mission history show a forward port and a crew transfer tunnel. A December 1973 US intelligence assessment also reported evidence of a docking assembly, with its exact identity uncertain. The gallery represents the collar, closed hatch and active probe using type geometry. The ferry's Igla antennas are shown as a type-based reconstruction. Soyuz 12 flew its test independently after the loss of DOS-3. [Zak, *Soyuz 12*](https://russianspaceweb.com/soyuz12.html) · [US National Intelligence Estimate 11-1-73, §§95–98](https://www.nixonlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/virtuallibrary/releases/may16/may16/nie-support.pdf) · [NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, §§1.8.2 and 1.8.4.1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) |
| Propulsion, orientation and power | The aft main engine raised the orbit and performed the braking burn. The ferry type carried a backup engine, thermal radiators, infrared and other orientation sensors and battery power for about two days in independent flight. The old solar arrays were removed and the radio whips moved to the sides of the service module. Mounts, exterior fittings and antenna geometry are reconstructed. [NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, §§1.8.2–1.8.3 and fig. 1-20](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part1.pdf) · [*Nauka i zhizn*, *Flights Continue*](https://www.nkj.ru/archive/articles/52879/) |
| Descent cabin and suits | The two cosmonauts wore Sokol-K pressure suits for ascent and return. The removed couch made room for suit support hardware. The gallery places Lazarev in the command position and Makarov beside him. The precise side assignment, equipment layout, cabin fabrics and suit details are reconstructed. The Sirius-A8 instruments are represented by a reconstruction based on the earlier Sirius-7K globe, television display and command panels. [RGANTD, *Flight of the Urals*](https://mail.rgantd.ru/news/pamyatnye-daty/polet-uralov/) · [Skoog and Abramov, *The Soviet/Russian Spacesuit History*, pp. 356–358 and fig. 13](https://epizodsspace.airbase.ru/bibl/inostr-yazyki/iaa/2011/skoog_abramov_the_soviet-russian_spacesuit.pdf) · [Tiapchenko, display systems, table entries 8 and 11](https://web.mit.edu/slava/OldFiles/space/essays/essay-tiapchenko1.htm) · [Shared cabin reconstruction](../../vehicles/cabin.md) |
| LKSA-3 camera | The Space Research Institute records nine lenses across three independent 35 mm film channels and six bands of Earth photographs. Its published photograph shows the instrument at the spacecraft window. The exhibit reconstructs the housing and position. [Space Research Institute, *IKI at 50*, pp. 156 and 167, fig. 3](https://iki.cosmos.ru/sites/default/files/publications/2015iki50.pdf) |

## What is simplified

- The slider illustrates removal of the ascent hardware, antenna deployment and separation of the main modules.
  The docking mechanism and forward hatch are fixed for inspection.
- Body stations, blankets, harnesses, wiring, antenna mounts, instrument states and individual
  suit fittings use simplified reconstructions. The orbital module has representative stowage.
- The service module shows the main radiator, engines, side whips and command-radio antenna.
  The small transfer-frame radiator, battery packs and internal wiring are omitted.
- The flight record describes the Earth photographs, the suit checks and the parachute descent.
  The exhibit depicts the vehicle and crew as static hardware.
