# Voskhod (USSR, 1964–1965)

The Vostok altered to carry more than one man, flown twice with crews: once with three aboard and
no pressure suits, once with two and an airlock through which one of them went outside. OKB-1 under
Sergei Korolev; both flights from Site 1 at Tyuratam on the 11A57, the R-7 with the larger third
stage built for the planetary probes. Neither ship had a launch escape system or ejection seats, and
both crews landed inside the descent module, the first to do so.

Both rows have been checked against sources read for this repo: the spacecraft's numbers in
Siddiqi's text and launch log, in Kamanin's diary and, for Voskhod 2, in OKB-1's own report of
6 June 1965; the launch vehicles' numbers (R15000-04 and R15000-05) in McDowell's launch log only;
the dates, times and crews in the TASS reports of the days. Each flight's README has where the
sources disagree.

Before each flew a ship of the same kind without a crew: Kosmos 47 (3KV No. 2, 6 October 1964, a
day in orbit with dummies, landed whole) and Kosmos 57 (3KD No. 1, 22 February 1965, which blew
itself up on its third orbit when two ground stations sent it the same command at once). The ring
the airlock leaves on the ball was then flown on a reconnaissance satellite, Kosmos 59, to see that
a ball so fitted came down safely.

**Not flown with a crew.** A manifest of February 1965 had five more ships (Siddiqi, pp. 506 to 511
and 522 to 526): 3KV No. 5, which flew as Kosmos 110 in February 1966 with two dogs;
No. 6 as Voskhod 3, a flight of 18 to 20 days for Volynov and Shonin (earlier Katys, then Gorbatko);
No. 7 as Voskhod 4, a medical flight and later an experiment in artificial gravity; 3KD No. 8 and
No. 9 as Voskhod 5 and 6, more walks outside, one of them planned for a crew of women. Voskhod 6 was
dropped in November 1965 and the rest by June 1966, as the Soyuz came on. None of this is from
anything but Siddiqi.

## The spacecraft, in general

A Vostok with the ejection seat taken out. The **descent module** is the same ball, 2.3 m across;
the **instrument module** behind it is the Vostok's, with a new radio, television of 25 frames a
second and an orientation system that could find its heading at night. What was new:

- **Couches** in place of the seat, turned a quarter turn from the line it had lain on: three in the
  3KV, two in the 3KD. The instrument board and the Vzor sight stayed where they were, so the crew
  read them sideways.
- **A reserve braking engine**, solid, on the forward pole of the ball. A Vostok had one engine and
  flew low enough to fall out of orbit unaided within its ten days of supplies; a Voskhod had
  supplies for two or three days at most, and a second engine instead.
- **A landing in the ball**: two main parachutes and a solid rocket in their lines, fired by a probe
  1.2 m long that reaches the ground first.
- On the 3KD, **the Volga airlock**: an inflatable tube 2.5 m long, folded to 0.7 m for launch, on
  the hatch a Vostok used for assembly; pressure suits for both men, and a pack on the back of the
  one who went out.

What was given up was every means of escape a Vostok had. There were no ejection seats. There was
no escape rocket: the one meant for the Soyuz was not ready. On the first flight there were no
pressure suits either. OKB-1's own plan for the 3KV said that for the first 25 to 44 seconds of the
climb rescue would be "difficult".
