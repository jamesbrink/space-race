# The G5C suit

What [g5c_suit.py](g5c_suit.py) builds, and how far to trust it. The G5C was the suit made for
Gemini 7's fourteen days, and Frank Borman and James Lovell wore it to the pad on 4 December 1965.
It is built as the G3C's garment ([g3c_suit.py](g3c_suit.py), [g3c-suit.md](g3c-suit.md)) made over
as the record and the photographs of the day have it (`Garment`): every size and place not named
below is the G3C's, and as estimated as it is there. Like the G4C ([g4c-suit.md](g4c-suit.md)) the
two men stand on the deck at Complex 19, each a man the launch site is walked as, and are not built
seated; what is said of their parts in the viewer is [g5c-suit.parts.ts](g5c-suit.parts.ts).

## Sources, in short

- **GVII**: the Gemini VII mission report, MSC-G-R-66-1: 3.1.2.9.2 (the suit), 5.1.10.3.1 and
  7.1.2.3.3 (how it was worn).
- **K7**: NASA release 65-362, the Gemini 7/6 press kit: "Gemini 7 Suit" (p. 49) and the crew
  biographies (pp. 66 and 67).
- **P**: NASA's photographs of 4 December 1965: S65-59932 (the two leaving the suiting trailer at
  Complex 16, hoods on, the flag on each left sleeve, Lovell's ventilator), S65-60981 (Lovell on the
  ramp at pad 19, close: the hood, its visor, its zip, his tag and the insignia under it),
  S65-61848 (Lovell at the elevator).

Lovell's tag reads "...OVELL JR" in S65-60981 and is lettered "J.A.LOVELL JR"; Borman's is not
legible in what was read and is lettered "F.BORMAN", as the others are. Their build beside
figurelib's man is estimated; the faces are the shared head.

## What the model gets wrong or leaves out

Everything [g3c-suit.md](g3c-suit.md) lists of the G3C's garment, and:

- **The hood's form is the hard helmet's.** The G5C's was cloth over a crash helmet, seamed, rounder
  at the crown and creased; it is built smooth, the helmet's size, with the visor's seam as the
  G3C's seal. The crash helmet inside is not built: the head is bare behind the visor.
- **The zip is a ring.** On the suit it runs on a slant, from the front at his left round under the
  hood to the back.
- **The suit is not made lighter.** Without its restraint layer it hung looser and creased more than
  the G4C; it is built as the G3C's cloth.
- **What each man carried to the pad is not built**: the portable ventilator, its hoses and the
  yellow overshoes (P: S65-59932).
- **Not built seated**, nor folded behind the seat as the suit Lovell took off was for most of the
  flight.
