# Project Gemini (USA, 1961–1966)

NASA's second human spaceflight programme, between Mercury and Apollo: two men in a ship that could
change its orbit, stay up for two weeks, find another vehicle and dock with it, and be flown down
to a chosen place. Ten crewed flights in twenty months, all in the two-man spacecraft McDonnell
Aircraft built in St. Louis, all on Martin's Titan II from Launch Complex 19.

Crews, dates and durations are the programme's own summary table (*Project Gemini: Technology and
Operations, a Chronology*, NASA SP-4002, Appendix 1, Table A), read for this page. Its table prints
"Nov. 1, 1966" for the last launch, which its own Table B and every other account have as the 11th.
Two unmanned flights came first: Gemini-Titan 1 (8 April 1964, into orbit, not recovered) and
Gemini-Titan 2 (19 January 1965, a ballistic test of the heat shield). Only Gemini-Titan 3 has been
researched beyond that table; what is said below of the later ships is from the sources read for
it, and is to be checked when each is built.

## The spacecraft, in general

A re-entry module 11 ft long, shaped like a Mercury capsule with half as much room again inside,
on a white adapter that widens to the Titan's 10 ft. The crew sit side by side under two hatches, on ejection
seats: there is no escape tower. Everything needed only in orbit is in the adapter, which is thrown
away in two pieces round the retrofire: [gemini-spacecraft.md](vehicles/gemini-spacecraft.md).

The ship changed from flight to flight, which is why each flight here will have its own model:

| | No. 3 | No. 4 | Later |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Power | 3 batteries of 400 Ah in the adapter | 6 | fuel cells on No. 5 and most after |
| Rendezvous radar | none | none | from No. 5 |
| Sideways thrusters | dummies | real | real |
| Going outside | no provision | an umbilical and a hand-held gas gun | |
| Suit | G3C | G4C | |

The first column, and the power, thrusters, umbilical and suit of the second, are documented
([the flight's spacecraft.md](flights/gt-3/spacecraft.md)); the rest is general knowledge.
