# GATV-5004

The Atlas and Agena were lost together on 17 May 1966. A replacement target reached orbit on 1 June, giving Stafford and Cernan a second chance to fly the rendezvous.

Atlas SLV-3 5303 launched this uncrewed target from Cape Kennedy Launch Complex 14 on 1966-05-17 at 15:15:03.422 UTC. It carried docking adapter 5. The gallery shows the target separately at scale. The [assembled Atlas](../../vehicles/atlas-slv3.md) stands at the dated launch site with its shroud closed. The exterior is shown with the launch shroud removed to expose the docking equipment. This is an exhibit configuration for the lost vehicle.

Sources include NASA SP-4002, Appendix I, Table B, and NASA CR-1106, pp. 334–336. See the [hardware notes](../../vehicles/agena-target-vehicle.md) for the geometry and its confidence.

## Flown

Fly flies the launch as the record had it fail, from Convair’s evaluation of Atlas 5303 in the Gemini IX-A report (12.7): the B2 booster chamber lost at 120.63 s, the stack pitched down from 67 to 298 degrees from the vertical by 150 s and back to 283, its sequence run on its backups, the Agena let go at 301.7 s and its engine never started (the range’s thrust termination). It comes down at T+437 s about 150 nautical miles east of the pad; the record has it still at 97,000 ft at 436 s and an impact 107 nautical miles north-east. Not flown, for want of figures: the turn to the north, the pitch between its two documented ends (turned evenly), how long the back-off took. The weights are Gemini VIII’s (assumed). The Atlas is the Mercury-Atlas flights’ (the same plan pieces), its weights this launch’s report’s and its thrusts Atlas 5302’s; its steering is a stated law solved for the target’s insertion, since the Atlas’s programme and radio guidance were not found. Every input that is not documented, and every check missed, is listed in [orbital.ts](orbital.ts), [target-plan.ts](../../vehicles/target-plan.ts) and src/sim/misses.ts.
