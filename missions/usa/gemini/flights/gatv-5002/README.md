# GATV-5002

The target was lost during ascent on 25 October 1965. Schirra and Stafford were still on the pad. They flew in December to meet Gemini VII instead.

Atlas SLV-3 5301 launched this uncrewed target from Cape Kennedy Launch Complex 14 on 1965-10-25 at 15:00:04.490 UTC. It carried docking adapter 2. The gallery shows the target separately at scale. The [assembled Atlas](../../vehicles/atlas-slv3.md) stands at the dated launch site with its shroud closed. The exterior is shown with the launch shroud removed to expose the docking equipment. This is an exhibit configuration for the lost vehicle.

Sources include NASA SP-4002, Appendix I, Table B, and NASA CR-1106, pp. 334–336. See the [hardware notes](../../vehicles/agena-target-vehicle.md) for the geometry and its confidence.

## Flown

Fly flies the launch as the record had it fail: the Atlas and the coast normal, the Agena’s engine started at 367.53 s and shut down 0.91 s later, from the Gemini VI-A report (MSC-G-R-65-5). The steering law is solved on the flight as planned. What fell is flown as one Agena and comes down at T+939 s at 27.2° N 49.2° W, against the range’s vacuum impact point, 27.5° N 49.0° W. Not flown, for want of figures: the engine’s hard start itself, the breakup at about 375.7 s and the five pieces the radar tracked. The weights are Gemini VIII’s (assumed). The Atlas is the Mercury-Atlas flights’ (the same plan pieces), its weights this launch’s report’s and its thrusts Atlas 5302’s; its steering is a stated law solved for the target’s insertion, since the Atlas’s programme and radio guidance were not found. Every input that is not documented, and every check missed, is listed in [orbital.ts](orbital.ts), [target-plan.ts](../../vehicles/target-plan.ts) and src/sim/misses.ts.
