# Gemini-Titan 10 (Gemini X)

Gemini X docked with its own Agena in the fourth revolution. Young flew the joined spacecraft on the Agena's engines, up to an apogee of 412 n mi and back down into phase with the dormant target left by Gemini VIII, then undocked and finished that second rendezvous on the spacecraft's own thrusters. Collins worked through an open hatch for the first EVA. On the second, he crossed to the older Agena and recovered its micrometeoroid package.

## At Launch Complex 19

The launch site shows the Titan on its thrust mount with the erector up at the start of the count.
The two crew models stand by the ramp before boarding and can be used to walk the complex. The
Agena stands beside the vehicle in the gallery. The gallery also carries the spent Gemini VIII target GATV 5003, as Collins found it, on its own stand.
The flight deck and orbital trajectory are pending.

Young and Collins were in the white room by about 15:15 EST and settling into the spacecraft around 15:20. Both hatches were shut around 15:27. Launch Control reported the room being cleared at T−77 min 46 sec and the erector down by T−42 min 45 sec. The terminal hold at T−3 took 5 min 26 sec. A stage II fuel vent and topping umbilical failed to release and part of the line went up with the booster. The pad draws the usual disconnect.

Countdown events use the [NASA PAO launch commentary](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/gt10-pao.pdf)
and the [Gemini X mission report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750067644). Estimates in the slider include when individual platforms
folded and when the umbilicals came to rest. The Cape clock uses EST, as in the mission report.
The launch-axis origin of the modeled deck is 28.506909° N, 80.554297° W, derived from the remains of
the thrust mount as described in [the site's record](../../../places/cape-canaveral/lc-19.md).

At about 17:00 EST the launch area had scattered cloud at 2,200 ft and a high overcast. Wind was from 010° at 12 knots, visibility 10 miles and the temperature 81 °F with a 74 °F dew point. The NWS summary codes the 2,200 ft layer as scattered, variable broken. The scene draws the recorded high overcast over the low cloud field, with estimated cloud proportions and high-cloud height.

Launch-area readings come from [the mission report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750067644), section 12.2, and
[the NWS Spaceflight Meteorology Group history](https://www.weather.gov/smg/gemini).
The surrounding cloud cover is estimated. The hour-by-hour surface weather and vertical profile
come from ECMWF ERA5 interpolated to the pad.

The walking figures start on the concrete approach to the erector ramp. The command pilot starts at site X/Z 15/−7 m and the pilot at 21/−7 m, facing the vehicle. These standing marks are estimated from the model’s open ground. The ramp leads to the erector elevator.
