# Gemini spacecraft No. 11

Charles Conrad and Richard Gordon flew this spacecraft from 12 to 15 September
1966. The [Gemini XI mission report](https://tothemoon.im-ldi.com/download_document?file_name=gemini_11_mission_report.pdf&project=GEMINI)
(MSC-G-R-66-8, October 1966) records changes from
[spacecraft 10](../gt-10/spacecraft.md). The [common spacecraft reference](../../vehicles/gemini-spacecraft.md)
describes the basic structure. [GATV-5006](../../vehicles/agena-target-vehicle.md)
carried the other end of the tether experiment.

## Launch configuration

| Feature | As flown | Confidence and source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Axial stations | Z13.44 at the Titan interface, Z103.44 at the cabin/adapter boundary, Z239.28 at the launch tip | Documented, mission report Fig. 3.0-1(a), p. 3-2. Derived launch length 225.84 in and adapter length 90.00 in |
| Apollo sump demonstration | A one-eighth-scale transparent plastic model of the Apollo service-propulsion sump tank sat at the centre of the equipment adapter. A sheet-metal shroud held eight backlights, a mirror for a second view, and a clock. Two wide-angle 16 mm cameras sat on a separate bracket on the retrograde rocket access panel | Documented, §3.1.1. Fig. 3.1-1 labels the assembly in the adapter. The cameras remained in the adapter after the curtailed EVA |
| EVA provisions | Foot restraints faced the pilot forward into the equipment section, where a fold-out curtain flap and an auxiliary light gave access to the sump. The hand-held nitrogen unit and chest pack followed spacecraft 10, with a 30 ft umbilical in place of the 50 ft one | Documented, §3.1.2.4 and Table 3.1-I |
| Docking and tether | Retractable docking index bar, plus a panel switch that could jettison it separately from the retrograde adapter. Gordon transferred the 100 ft Dacron tether and clamp from the Agena target docking adapter to the bar during EVA. The clamp belongs on the target in the launch configuration | Documented, §§3.1.1.2, 3.1.2.3.1, 3.4, 7.1.2 and Fig. 3.1-2, p. 3-16 |
| Windows and seats | Jettisonable launch window covers, two interior Polaroid filters to reduce sun glare during launch, and guards on the ejection seats' lap-belt buckles | Documented, §§3.1.2.3.2, 3.1.2.3.4 and 7.1.2.18.1. Cover geometry assumed from spacecraft 9 |
| Propulsion and panels | No significant propulsion changes from spacecraft 10. Added independent docking-bar jettison, an OAMS regulated-helium reading on the pressure/temperature selector, and camera/light control on a previously unused oxygen high-rate switch position | Documented, §§3.1.2, 3.1.2.3.1 and Fig. 3.1-3, p. 3-17 |

Section 3.3 documents spacecraft weights of 8,374 lb at launch, 5,577 lb at
retrograde, 4,734 lb at 0.05 g reentry, 4,340 lb at main-parachute deployment and
4,230 lb at touchdown without the parachute.

## Finish and evidence limits

- The dark cabin shingle finish is visible in flight and recovery photographs.
  The white adapter and gold-plated inner skins follow McDonnell SEDR 300 vol. 2,
  p. 2-17. The full flag and UNITED STATES layout and dimensions are assumed
  from the common configuration. The surviving capsule preserves postflight
  surfaces only. [U.S. Navy flight and recovery photographs](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/exploration-and-innovation/navy-and-space-exploration/Gemini_11_NASA.html)
  [Smithsonian A19680260000](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/capsule-gemini-xi/nasm_A19680260000)
- Overall sump shroud dimensions, camera spacing, curtain cut and the foot
  restraints' mounting stations remain estimates. One-eighth scale describes
  the tank itself. The report's overall spacecraft drawing identifies the
  experiment but supplies no dimensioned installation detail.
- The numbered consoles and the precise stowage arrangement can be traced from
  Figs. 3.1-3 and 3.1-4. Complete depths, internal wiring and fine fittings
  require spacecraft-specific drawings.
