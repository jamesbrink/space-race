# Gemini-Titan 11 (Gemini XI)

Gemini XI docked with its Agena 94 minutes after liftoff, in the first revolution. The Agena then raised their apogee to a record height. Gordon's first EVA ended early after the work of attaching their tether overtaxed him. On the third day, a slow rotation kept the tether stretched between the two vehicles.

## At Launch Complex 19

The launch site shows the Titan on its thrust mount with the erector up at the start of the count.
The two crew models stand by the ramp before boarding and can be used to walk the complex. The
Agena stands beside the vehicle in the gallery. 
The flight deck and orbital trajectory are pending.

Conrad and Gordon reached the white room around 07:25 EST and were seated about a minute later. The two hatches had just closed at the 07:39 update. A suspected leak around the command pilot's left hatch stopped the count at T−97. The team resealed the hatch, repeated the checks and resumed from T−103; the crew estimated a 15-minute delay. The erector began lowering around T−57 min 42 sec. The terminal hold at T−3 lasted 2 min 21 sec.

Countdown events use the [NASA PAO launch commentary](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/gt11-pao.pdf)
and the [Gemini XI mission report](https://tothemoon.im-ldi.com/download_document?file_name=gemini_11_mission_report.pdf&project=GEMINI). Estimates in the slider include when individual platforms
folded and when the umbilicals came to rest. The Cape clock uses EST, as in the mission report.
The launch-axis origin of the modeled deck is 28.506909° N, 80.554297° W, derived from the remains of
the thrust mount as described in [the site's record](../../../places/cape-canaveral/lc-19.md).

## Sky and weather

At liftoff, the local geometric elevations and true bearings from the repo's DE440-based ephemeris are:

| Body | Azimuth | Elevation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sun | 115.9° | 45.7° |
| Moon (7.3% waning) | 126.9° | 76.7° |

At 09:46 EST the launch area had scattered cloud at 900 and 1,600 ft with broken cloud above. Wind was from 200° at 3 knots, visibility 10 miles and the temperature 82 °F with a 73 °F dew point. The mission report prints 1,300 ft for its third layer and also records high broken cloud. NWS codes the third layer BKN130 (13,000 ft), the altitude used for the middle deck. The two scattered low layers are drawn as a single field.

Launch-area readings come from [the mission report](https://tothemoon.im-ldi.com/download_document?file_name=gemini_11_mission_report.pdf&project=GEMINI), section 12.2, and
[the NWS Spaceflight Meteorology Group history](https://www.weather.gov/smg/gemini).
The surrounding cloud cover is estimated. The hour-by-hour surface weather and vertical profile
come from ECMWF ERA5 interpolated to the pad.
