# Gemini-Titan 12 (Gemini XII)

The last Gemini docked with its Agena in the third revolution. An anomaly during the Agena's own ascent ruled out the planned boost to a higher orbit. With footholds and handholds to brace himself, Aldrin completed a two-hour tethered EVA with work on both spacecraft, in addition to two stand-up EVAs at the open hatch.

## At Launch Complex 19

The launch site shows the Titan on its thrust mount with the erector up at the start of the count.
The two crew models stand by the ramp before boarding and can be used to walk the complex. The
Agena stands beside the vehicle in the gallery. 
The flight deck and orbital trajectory are pending.

Lovell and Aldrin reached the white room around 13:44 EST and were aboard by the 13:46 commentary. Both hatches were shut around 13:58. The white room was clear by T−70 and lowering began at about T−58 min 18 sec. The terminal hold at T−3 lasted 3 min 29 sec. Secondary autopilot faults had postponed the launch twice.

Countdown events use the [NASA PAO launch commentary](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/gt12-pao.pdf)
and the [Gemini XII mission report](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/Gemini12MissionReport.pdf). Estimates in the slider include when individual platforms
folded and when the umbilicals came to rest. The Cape clock uses EST, as in the mission report.
The launch-axis origin of the modeled deck is 28.506909° N, 80.554297° W, derived from the remains of
the thrust mount as described in [the site's record](../../../places/cape-canaveral/lc-19.md).

## Sky and weather

At liftoff, the local geometric elevations and true bearings from the repo's DE440-based ephemeris are:

| Body | Azimuth | Elevation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sun | 236.1° | 19.7° |
| Moon (0.8% waning) | 245.0° | 13.5° |

At 15:46 EST the launch area had scattered high cloud, covering four tenths of the sky. Wind was from 080° at 5 knots, visibility 10 miles and the temperature 76 °F with a 66 °F dew point. NWS identifies the high cloud as cirrus. Its base and optical depth are estimated. The observation gives no low or middle cloud layer.

Launch-area readings come from [the mission report](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/Gemini12MissionReport.pdf), section 12.2, and
[the NWS Spaceflight Meteorology Group history](https://www.weather.gov/smg/gemini).
The surrounding cloud cover is estimated. The hour-by-hour surface weather and vertical profile
come from ECMWF ERA5 interpolated to the pad.

The walking figures start on the concrete approach to the erector ramp. The command pilot starts at site X/Z 15/−7 m and the pilot at 21/−7 m, facing the vehicle. These standing marks are estimated from the model’s open ground. The ramp leads to the erector elevator.
