# Gemini spacecraft No. 12

James Lovell and Buzz Aldrin flew this spacecraft from 11 to 15 November 1966.
The [Gemini XII mission report](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/Gemini12MissionReport.pdf)
(MSC-G-R-67-1, January 1967) describes its changes from
[spacecraft 11](../gt-11/spacecraft.md). See [the common spacecraft reference](../../vehicles/gemini-spacecraft.md)
and [GATV-5001](../../vehicles/agena-target-vehicle.md) for the separately
launched Agena. Both vehicles carried an EVA work station.

## Launch configuration

| Feature | As flown | Confidence and source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Axial stations | Z13.44 at the Titan interface, Z103.44 at the cabin/adapter boundary, Z239.28 at the launch tip | Documented, mission report Fig. 3.0-1(a), p. 3-2. Derived launch length 225.84 in and adapter length 90.00 in |
| Docking bar cap | A thin overhanging cap on top of the bar provided a mating surface for a fly-in tether connection and retained the tether. The bar was retracted at launch, and the clamp rode on the outboard surface of the target's tether container | Documented, §§3.1.1, 3.4 and Figs. 3.1-2 and 3.4-1 |
| Equipment adapter | The EVA work station replaced the Apollo sump experiment and hand-held maneuvering unit propellant installation. The reserve OAMS oxidizer F-package and its associated hardware were removed, saving 35.83 lb | Documented, §§3.1.1 and 3.1.2.2 and Fig. 3.1-3 |
| Adapter work station | Fixed foot restraints and waist-tether rings supported tasks at a board with detachable handholds, hose and electrical connectors, tether hooks, six 3, 4 and 5 in hook-and-loop strips, tool pouches and a wrench target with one 0.5 in high bolt head. The camera bracket moved to the left handhold so Aldrin could fit the camera while restrained | Documented, §§3.1.1 and 3.1.2.5.1 and Fig. 3.1-6, p. 3-24. Fig. 3.1-6 also shows the instrumented board in the adapter |
| Telescoping rail | Stowed inside the right hatch, 36.49 in long stowed and 99.39 in extended. One end clipped to a modified shingle bolt between the hatches and the other engaged a receptacle on the target docking cone during EVA | Documented, §3.1.2.5.1, Table 3.1-II and Fig. 3.1-7, p. 3-25 |
| Pilot window | The retrograde reference lines on both surfaces were removed for sextant and photographic work. An additional sun filter was provided for the command pilot’s left window | Documented, §§3.1.1 and 3.1.2.4.3. External launch covers assumed from spacecraft 11 |
| Retrograde adapter | Closed S012 micrometeorite collector door at launch. The door was opened by ground command in orbit, and Aldrin recovered the package during the first standup EVA | Documented, §8.10 and Fig. 8.10-1, p. 8-55 |
| EVA suit and umbilical | G4C for both crew. Aldrin's EVA coverlayer used nylon in place of the heat-protective cloth from spacecraft 9's fit and had rectangular torso quilting. The ELSS umbilical was a single 25 ft hose assembly | Documented, §§3.1.2.5 and 3.1.2.5.2 and Table 3.1-I |

Section 3.3 documents spacecraft weights of 8,296.47 lb at launch, 5,627.87 lb
at retrograde, 4,802.18 lb at 0.05 g reentry, 4,403.74 lb at main-parachute
deployment and 4,292.93 lb at touchdown without the parachute.

## Finish and evidence limits

- Dark ribbed shingles are visible in NASA's exposure of Aldrin at the open
  hatch. The white adapter and gold-plated inner skins follow McDonnell SEDR 300
  vol. 2, p. 2-17. The complete flag and UNITED STATES layout and dimensions
  are assumed from the common spacecraft. The Smithsonian specimen is the
  recovered capsule with mission wear and no adapter. [NASA Gemini XII gallery](https://www.nasa.gov/gallery/gemini-xii/)
  [Smithsonian A19680265000](https://www.si.edu/object/capsule-gemini-xii%3Anasm_A19680265000)
- Figure 3.1-2 fixes the shape and orientation of the retracted cap in section.
  Its plan dimensions remain estimated. Fig. 3.1-6 establishes the work board's
  two levels and equipment layout, with no overall dimensions. The board's
  envelope, mounting frame and ordinary fittings require estimates.
- The extended handrail belongs to the docked EVA configuration. The mission
  report documents its stowage and two ends and supplies both lengths, but
  finer mounting details inside the hatch remain unverified.
