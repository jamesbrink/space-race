# CSM-108 · Yankee Clipper

Charles Conrad Jr. flew in the left couch, Richard Gordon in the centre and Alan Bean on the right. Launch Control reported Conrad aboard at 08:33 EST. Bean followed about six minutes later and Gordon entered at 08:46. The closeout crew shut the hatch at about 09:05. [Launch Control voice record](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/01launch_to_earth_orbit.html#t-0024856)

## Bean’s telemetry switch

The bottom row of main display panel 3 carried switches for the signal conditioning equipment (SCE) and premodulation processor (PMP), beside the tape-recorder controls. The exhibit uses the two switch positions registered from North American’s CM-106 panel drawing. The Apollo 12 flight drawing identifies their order, the NORM and AUX legends and Bean’s right-hand station. The installed switch dimensions, panel boundary and lever inclinations are estimated. The gallery shows both in their normal launch positions. [Panel 3 drawing](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pics/panel-03.png) · [Switch detail](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pics/sce-to-aux.png)

The first electrical discharge at 36.5 seconds disconnected all three fuel cells from the main buses. A second disturbance occurred at 52 seconds. Selecting SCE auxiliary power restored conditioned measurements to telemetry. The fuel cells continued operating and the crew reconnected them separately. [MSC-01855, §§7.2 and 14.1.1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/a12-missionreport.pdf) · [Launch voice record](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/01launch_to_earth_orbit.html)

## Drinking water and recovery

The new inline hydrogen separator operated between the fuel cells and the water valve panel. The crew also tried improved gas-separator cartridges on the water gun and food-preparation unit. The cutaway places a separate inline casing beside the dispenser for inspection. Its envelope, fittings and installation are estimates. The dispenser uses the CM-107 envelope as a reference for the revised cartridge. [MSC-01855, §§7.9.3 and A.1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/a12-missionreport.pdf)

Apollo 12 substituted one nylon lifting loop for the recovery cable and auxiliary loop. The cutaway shows the sling stowed with the forward recovery gear. The folded outline and attachment lugs are estimated. A mechanical pin secured the sea-dye marker against premature deployment. [MSC-01855, appendix A, p. A-1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/a12-missionreport.pdf)

## Shared geometry and limits

- The Block II shell, five window apertures, docking probe, later folding couches, service-module sectors and antenna use the programme reconstruction. The measured Columbia window outlines and CM-106 hatch-pocket and console layouts are assumed shared for CM-108. The service-engine exit to the docking plane measures 404.25 inches from the shared stations. [Apollo CSM geometry](apollo-csm.md)
- The switches are static. Most switches outside the two telemetry-power positions retain CM-106’s reconstructed layout. The relocated S-band squelch override, modified entry-monitor scribe emulsion, abort logic rewiring, service-engine actuator filters and added temperature measurements are omitted. [Apollo 12 Press Kit, pp. 56–57](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A12_PressKit.pdf) · [MSC-01855, appendix A](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/a12-missionreport.pdf)
- The cutaway omits internal separators, the food-preparation cartridge, the sea-dye pin and detailed sling hardware. Gauge readings, legends elsewhere on the console, seat coverings and reflected cabin light are exhibit reconstructions.
