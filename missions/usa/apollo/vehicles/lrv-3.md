# LRV-3 · Apollo 17

Eugene Cernan drove from the left seat and Harrison Schmitt occupied the right. The Gallery shows the vehicle prepared for the second traverse. Schmitt’s rear and side photographs at the surface electrical properties site show its load a few minutes after Cernan left Challenger. The rear rack carries the blue gravimeter beside the white right-rear fender extension. [AS17-135-20542 and 20544](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a17/images17.html#20542)

## The chassis and crew

The common three-section frame has a 90 in wheelbase, 72 in track, 32.19 in diameter mesh tires and 14 in clearance at rated load. These drawing dimensions are assumed from the common J-mission chassis. The Apollo 17 press kit’s power-system description gives two 36 V, 23-cell silver-zinc batteries rated at 121 Ah apiece. Each traction drive and fender steers with its wheel upright. The rear suspension carries the repaired extension through steering and wheel travel. The two A7LB EVA riders are scaled from the flight biographies to 6 ft for Cernan and 5 ft 9 in for Schmitt. [Common chassis, drive and suspension](lunar-rovers.md) · [Apollo 17 press kit, “Lunar Roving Vehicle” and crew biographies](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A17_PressKit.pdf)

## Equipment on EVA-2

| Equipment | Flight evidence and reconstruction |
| --- | --- |
| Right-rear fender | Cernan’s hammer caught the original rear extension during EVA-1. His first repair came loose. For EVA-2 the crew taped four laminated map sheets together and fastened them with two clamps from the lunar module's portable lights. The exhibit has the surviving amber fender, the curved sheets, transverse and longitudinal tape, and two screw clamps. The printed map texture is a crop of the mission’s EVA-2 contour chart. Its scale and placement on the four sheets are estimated. [Flight photo AS17-137-20979](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a17/images17.html#20979) · [Flight article and repair record](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a17/A17NewFender.html) · [EVA-2 contour chart](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a17/a17.eva2.contours.jpg) |
| Traverse gravimeter, S-199 | The instrument rode inboard of the replacement fender. Its published envelope was 50.8 cm high, 27.9 cm wide and 24.8 cm deep. The blue housing has a flat mounting side, curved outer surface, side fitting, covered controls, handle and three feet. Measurements could be taken with the rover stationary or with the case lifted onto the ground. [NASA RP-1317, S-199 entry](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/Lunar/urn-nasa-pds-apollodoc/document_common/apollo_experiment_operations_catalog.pdf) · [Instrument drawing, fig. 2-26(a)](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a17/a17.tge.jpg) · [Apollo 17 preliminary science report, chapter 13](https://an.rsl.wustl.edu/apollo/data/a17/station0/tge/docs/tge_psr.pdf) |

## Reconstruction limits

- Instrument locations, rack pickups, handle shapes, sheet curvature and overlap, tape width, clamp geometry and bag fullness are photo-based estimates. The terrain and start pose are described in the [flight record](../flights/apollo-17/README.md).
- The exhibit shows a loaded rover ready for a traverse. Changes of sample load, dust accumulation and deterioration of the map extension later on EVA-3 are omitted. The two crew members are posed for driving.
- The science cases have external equipment geometry. Instrument electronics, recorded data and field deployment are outside the model.
