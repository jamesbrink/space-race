# Saturn V SA-511 and spacecraft adapter SLA-20

SA-511 launched Casper and Orion from Pad 39A on 16 April 1972. SLA-20 sat above IU-511, S-IVB-511, S-II-11 and S-IC-11. At the top of the nominal 363-foot stack, the four adapter panels enclosed the service engine and the folded lander. The postflight mass reconstruction gives 6,537,238 lb at ground ignition and 6,429,658 lb at hold-down-arm release. ## Eight retrorockets

A pair of solid retrorockets pointed forward under each of the four S-IC engine fairings. SA-510 had carried four motors, in fairings A and C. Apollo 16 restored the B and D pairs to increase separation margin if a motor failed. SA-511 was the first flight with eight retrorockets **and** a 1.7-second interval between outboard engine cutoff and the retro ignition signal. Postflight reconstruction found about 51 inches of minimum lateral clearance between the S-II engine bells and the separating S-IC. Select any fin pair to see the two nozzles, case bands and mounting lugs behind the fairing. ## Two auxiliary propulsion modules

Two self-contained modules on opposite sides of the S-IVB aft skirt rolled the stage during powered flight, held its attitude during the coast and settled the propellants for restart. In each module the two propellant tanks sit below the helium pressurant sphere. Three attitude engines look radially out and to either side. A fourth, smaller engine points aft. For lunar targeting, ground controllers commanded the ullage engines on for 54 seconds. Select either pod to inspect its pressure vessel, tank restraints, feed lines and engine cluster. [Flight evaluation, §7.12 and figs. 7-19 to 7-25](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025090) · [NASA NESC-IB-22-05, appendix D, figs. D.1–D.3 and tables D.1–D.2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220013375)

The first pod lost helium externally. The second pod leaked from its high-pressure side into its low-pressure circuit. Those readings contributed to the decision to revise lunar targeting. The IU tracking radio, powered by battery 6D20, lost downlink at 27:09:59. Seismometers at the Apollo 12, 14 and 15 sites later recorded the stage’s impact. The June 1972 postflight report gave a preliminary time of 75:08:02. NASA’s later mission chronology lists 75:08:04. The lack of tracking in the final two days limited the accuracy of the reconstruction. ## Reconstruction limits

The F-1 and J-2 contours, stage shells, tanks, markings, adapter hinges and S-II centre-engine pogo accumulator use the [shared Saturn V reconstruction](saturn-v.md). The first-stage retro motor cases, 7.5° mount cant and internal clearances follow the [S-IC-10 installation](sa-510.md). The eight-motor count, cutoff interval and flight separation clearance come from SA-511's report. The Gallery slider is an inspection sequence.

The opposed APS locations and directions and sizes of the tanks, fairings, racks, apertures, bells and feed lines are reconstructed from the S-IVB illustrations. The lower bays show simplified valve housings. The model omits the internal regulators, seals and detailed plumbing. The IU equipment cases and white paint patch use the adjacent-flight geometry. The model shows the hardware carried at launch.
