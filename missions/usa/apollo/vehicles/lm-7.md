# Aquarius · Grumman LM-7

Lovell, Swigert and Haise lived in Aquarius after the loss of Odyssey’s fuel-cell oxygen supply. Its descent engine made three burns, lasting 34.3, 264 and 13.7 seconds. Its reaction-control jets made the final course correction. Both stages were still joined when the crew released the entire lunar module at 141:30 GET. The Gallery slider separates them for inspection. The stowed spacecraft in SLA-16 has its landing gear folded. The return-flight exhibit has the four legs extended, as Haise confirmed at 061:00:10 before the first burn. The 31 ft deployed span and 37 in diameter footpads follow the flight press kit. ## Equipment for Fra Mauro

Quadrant II held two pull-out ALSEP pallets. Subpackage 1 carried the empty SNAP-27 generator, surface drill, drill stems, tool carrier and seismometer stool. Subpackage 2 carried the central station and dust detector, passive seismometer, charged-particle detector, cold-cathode gauge and heat-flow experiment. The drill would have bored holes for two heat-flow probes. The fuel capsule rode separately in a graphite cask on an external cradle. The flight press kit documents the cask at 23 in long by 8 in diameter and the generator at about 18 in tall by 16 in across its cooling fins. The instruments stayed aboard on the return to Earth. The seismic signal from the S-IVB impact came from the seismometer Apollo 12 had already placed on the Moon. [Mission Report, appendix A.3, figs. A-1 and A-2 and §4](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap13fj/pdf/a13-mission-report-197007.pdf) · [Press Kit, p. 65](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Apollo_13.pdf)

The MESA rack beside the ladder carried a color television camera, two sample return cases and tools. A black-and-white backup camera also flew. The changed MESA release used a looped cable. The upper and lower case positions and spare life-support cartridges follow photographs of the training rack. The training camera visible there was black-and-white. Grumman also increased the outer forward hatch shielding from 0.004 to 0.010 inch and saved 27.2 lb by modifying the landing gear insulation. ## The improvised carbon dioxide filter

The LM’s two-person suit circuit supported three people. When its lithium hydroxide supply ran short, the crew sealed the outlet side of square command module cartridges in plastic liquid-cooling-garment bags and taped each bag to a suit hose. Two cartridge-and-hose assemblies drew cabin air in parallel. After about 20 hours the crew stacked a second cartridge on each. The return-flight exhibit shows one of the initial assemblies, with its square face grille, mesh, tape, bag and hose. The stack exhibit shows the equipment carried at launch. ## Reconstruction limits

- The shared pressure cabin, docking rings, engines, propellant tanks, radar, gear linkage and crew stations use the [Grumman lunar module reconstruction](lunar-module.md). The 166 in descent-stage width, 92 in crew-compartment diameter and 32 in forward hatch are assumed from earlier vehicles. Both cabin stations are available from approximately the first LM inspection through jettison. [Mission Operations Report, 28 April 1970](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a13/A13_MissionOpReport.pdf)
- The positions, sizes and surface details of individual stowed instruments, blanketing, MESA fittings, hammock rolls and the filter assembly are estimated. The science pallets show representative instrument packages. The dust detector, seismometer stool, backup TV camera, hatch shielding thickness, all drill accessories and the internal plumbing changes are omitted.
- The isolated cabin shows one representative filter hose and simplified folds and tape. Other hose assemblies, later stacked cartridges, and the crew’s successive stowage arrangements are omitted. The return exhibit has an open transfer tunnel. The stowed probe and drogue are omitted. The launch exhibit shows the drogue installed. Gear and antenna hinge paths and dish orientation are approximate. The mission report (§6.3) records that the steerable S-band antenna remained unpowered.
