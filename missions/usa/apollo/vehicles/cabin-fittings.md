# Command module fittings: the hatch, the equipment bays, the forward compartment

Four modules build what is fitted to CM-101's structure round the crew, each into its part's node, in the source's
inches converted once:

| Module | Part (`flight.ts`) | What it builds |
|---|---|---|
| `side_hatch.py` | SideHatch | the unified hatch's outer thermal cover and its window outline, on the nominal CM wall |
| `hatch_interior.py` | SideHatch | the hatch's pressure door, latch housings, drive rods and pump handle, seen from the cabin |
| `cabin_bays.py` | CabinEquipmentBays | the cabinets round the couches and the navigation station in the lower bay |
| `forward_compartment.py` | ForwardCompartment | the forward tunnel and hatch, and the parachutes packed round them |

## The side hatch

The outward-opening unified hatch, with its round pressure window, as North American's *Apollo News Reference*,
pp. 45–49, shows it, and as JPL TM 33-425 (Walkover, Hart and Zosky, 1969), figure 2, arranges its mechanism: the door
face, fifteen latch housings, the exposed linkage and the pump handle. The cover's outline and window are measured from
the Smithsonian's survey of the detached CM-107 hatch, registered to the CM-107 exterior survey, and assumed for CM-101
as shared Block II hardware ([reference/README.md](reference/README.md) has the registration and its residuals); a
modern replica (Project Egress) serves only to bridge the two scans' poses and to cross-check the outline, and none of
its dimensions is used. The
pressure door follows the fixed aperture at its perimeter. The lightweight inner-door pockets of SC 106 and later are
not used (NASA TN D-7780, p. 12).

## The equipment bays

The cabinet faces, catches, compartments and colours follow the News Reference's equipment-bay illustrations, pp. 48–55;
the Apollo 7 press kit names the sextant and scanning telescope at the navigation station, whose eyepieces are drawn
simply. Cabinet sizes and door divisions are estimated.

## The forward compartment

The tunnel joins the common measured cabin entry to the nominal 30-inch forward hatch (News Reference p. 47, with its
six latches). The press kit gives two 16.5-ft drogues, three pilot chutes and three 83.3-ft mains; the three stowed main
packs fill separate sectors round the tunnel, with their deployment webbing and five canisters, their outlines and
places following North American's Earth Landing illustration P-131, printed p. 93. Individual CM-101 pack dimensions
were not found. No docking probe is installed: CSM-101 had none.

## Simplifications and things still wrong

- The hatch's door, latch housings and linkage are estimated in their mountings and sections; its seals,
  counterbalance and operation are not built, and it does not open.
- The cabinets' sizes, door divisions, latches, optics housings and finishes are estimated; nothing stowed in them, and
  no electronics, is drawn.
- The tunnel's 108-inch top station, its wall section and the hatch's six-latch detail are estimated.
- The parachute packs' shapes, webbing, stations and colours are estimated; deployment, the recovery electronics and the
  uprighting bags are left out.
