# CSM-103 · Apollo 8

The command module kept its three windows across the sides and hatch and two forward rendezvous windows. The 154-inch-diameter Block II shell, surveyed openings, internal pressure structure, glazing, service propulsion engine and service-module compartments use the shared geometries described in [the Apollo CSM notes](apollo-csm.md). Their type drawings and the surviving CM-107 survey establish an assumed common structure. The CM-101 stowage and service-module installations remain estimates for this unit. Apollo 8 had no active docking probe.

## Simplifications

- The exhibit freezes the couches in launch attitude. Its harnesses and heel restraints lie empty. Fabric tension, locks, controller armrests and the head and foot mount interfaces lack unit-specific detail.
- The antenna has one deployment motion. The pointing gimbals and three beamwidth settings remain static. The dark reflector surfaces represent the perforated metal in S68-52190 without modelling individual holes.
- The hatch exterior, latch geometry, insulation and seals follow the published arrangement schematically. The sextant has its adapter collar and the telescope its filter cell. Optical internals, camera installation and individual markings remain unfinished.
- The mission report also records the strengthened service-module bulkhead, thicker tension ties and RCS look-angle blankets. The cutaway retains the common bulkhead envelope. Local reinforcement, tension-tie sections and blanket tailoring lack as-built geometry.
- The DSKY uses common nominal cover and glass drawings. Its keycap profile, glass mounting, enclosure and placement remain estimates. The gallery shows unpowered panels.
