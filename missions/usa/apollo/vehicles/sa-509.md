# Saturn V SA-509 and spacecraft adapter SLA-17

SA-509 launched Kitty Hawk and Antares from LC-39A on January 31, 1971. SLA-17 enclosed LM-8 above the IU-509 guidance ring, S-IVB-509, S-II-9 and S-IC-9. The stack was nominally 363 ft high. The reconstructed mass was 6,505,548 lb at ground ignition and 6,417,871 lb when the holddown arms released it. Table 16-2 distinguishes both from the 6,508,444 lb ignition prediction. [AS-509 Flight Evaluation Report, p. xv, tables 16-2 and 16-9](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_saturn-v-fer.pdf) · [Apollo 14 Press Kit, pp. 90–93](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A14_PressKit.pdf)

## A change above the centre engine

On Apollo 13, vibration in the S-II shut the centre J-2 down early. The first flight of a helium-filled accumulator on the centre engine’s oxygen line was SA-509. The chamber surrounded the pipe above the oxygen pump and damped pressure oscillations. The flight data showed the device suppressed the coupling between the feedline and the structure. Each J-2 also had a two-position mixture-ratio control valve, and a separate cutoff device provided a backup against excessive centre-engine vibration. Four ullage motors stood around the lower interstage. [AS-509 Flight Evaluation Report, §§6.8–6.9, fig. 6-11 and §8.2.3](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_saturn-v-fer.pdf) · [Apollo 14 Press Kit, pp. 92–93](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A14_PressKit.pdf)

## The stage’s last three days

After translunar injection the spacecraft separated, leaving the S-IVB and instrument unit on their own. The instrument unit’s 350 Ah fourth battery, 6D20, supplied the radio during the coast. The stage struck the Moon at 82:37:52 ground elapsed time and the Apollo 12 seismic station recorded it. The model’s radio cases, switch, battery, cold plate and white cork patch follow the installation drawing for SA-508. SA-509’s flight report confirms the fourth battery and its use but gives no equivalent equipment layout. [AS-509 Flight Evaluation Report, §11.5, table 11-4 and §§17.2–17.5](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_saturn-v-fer.pdf) · [SA-508 Flight Manual, fig. 7-4 sheet 2 and §VII](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/MSFC-MAN-508%20Saturn%20V%20Flight%20Manual%20SA-508%201-March-1970.pdf)

## Reconstruction limits

The shared Saturn V structure supplies the stage stations, tank domes, engine contours and separation hardware. The accumulator’s vessel, visible connections, finishes and pipe bends are estimates based on the flight report’s schematic. The relief and control valve network, backup cutoff sensor, valve internals and most stage plumbing are omitted. The S-II systems tunnel and ullage fairings use their adjacent-flight profiles and clocking. The instrument unit’s equipment sizes and paint boundaries are estimates.
