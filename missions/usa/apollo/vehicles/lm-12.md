# Challenger · Grumman LM-12

Eugene Cernan flew the left standing station and Harrison Schmitt the right. The Gallery shows Challenger on approach, with all four legs extended and its surface payload aboard. In the launch stack the same hardware sits above the instrument unit with legs and antennas folded. The Gallery station times run from lunar-orbit separation at 107:47:56 through docking with Ron Evans in *America* at 187:37:15. Challenger touched down at 110:21:57 and lifted off at 185:21:37. Mission Control subsequently commanded the uncrewed ascent stage into the Moon. [Apollo 17 Mission Report, table 3-I](https://an.rsl.wustl.edu/apollo/data/A17/resources/A17_MissionReport.pdf)

## Packed for Taurus–Littrow

| Location | Hardware on this flight |
| --- | --- |
| Quadrant I | The central chassis of LRV-3 rode upright, with front and rear frames folded against it and the four wheels turned inboard. The model has four intact fenders at launch. The [deployed rover](lrv-3.md) shows the map extension fitted at the start of EVA-2. |
| Quadrant II | The first Array E subpackage carried the central station and instruments including the lunar atmospheric composition experiment. The second carried the radioisotope generator and further instruments including the lunar ejecta and meteorites experiment. The two packed envelopes measured 25.2 × 27.1 × 21.6 in and 25.2 × 27.2 × 21.6 in, with published weights of 137.11 and 99.83 lb. The fuel capsule travelled in a cask outside the bay. |
| Quadrant III | The rover equipment pallet rode here with the traverse gravimeter, the surface electrical properties transmitter and receiver, and two transport modules for the seismic survey. The flight stowage drawing shows the two transport modules inboard. Their combined load was 41.24 lb. The mission's portable gravimeter measured 50.8 × 27.9 × 24.8 cm. Both the blue gravimeter and the boxed radio receiver use the same geometry here and on the deployed rover. The transmitter and its antenna reels stayed on the ground. |
| Quadrant IV | The modularized equipment stowage assembly held the two-section neutron probe, sample containers and surface equipment. The assembled probe measured 2.4 m long and 2 cm in diameter. The crew inserted it into the drill-core hole on the first excursion and recovered it during the third. |
| Ascent cabin | The cosmic-ray experiment travelled in a sample bag. Its aluminum sun box and sliding shade detector had pull rings on opposite ends. The astronauts took them outside in a transfer bag and separated them for exposure. Both faces came home. |

## Propulsion and supplies

The descent stage launched with 7,521.7 lb of fuel and 12,042.5 lb of oxidizer. The ascent stage carried a separate 2,026.9 lb of fuel and 3,234.8 lb of oxidizer. The two descent-stage water tanks contained 202.6 and 204.6 lb. The model uses the J-series elongated tanks and the ten-inch descent nozzle extension documented for [LM-10](lm-10.md). [Apollo 17 Mission Report, §§8.10.1–8.10.5](https://an.rsl.wustl.edu/apollo/data/A17/resources/A17_MissionReport.pdf)

## Reconstruction limits

- The [shared lander drawing](lunar-module.md) sets a 31 ft deployed footpad span and a 23 ft 1 in antenna crest. The [Apollo 17 press kit, p. 99](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Apollo_17.pdf) gives an overall height of 22 ft 11 in without specifying the upper measurement point. The shared drawing puts the model crest two inches higher. The three deployed contact probes extend below the pad plane. Section profiles, engine interfaces, battery cases and cabin fittings use the documented J-series or earlier common components with the assumptions described for LM-10. Exact tank stations, the two pilots' eye positions and local hardware placements are estimated.
- The folded rover represents its external frames, suspension, wheels, seats and blanket. The standalone rover carries the full deployed running gear. The different configurations represent separate mission times.
- Array E displays the central station, instrument envelopes, generator and handling hardware. Small mechanisms and the internal instruments are omitted. Local mounting positions, enclosure proportions, fabric covers, pallet brackets and stowed antenna routing are reconstructed from the figures. The survey transport cases remain closed.
- The cosmic-ray box is shown without its bag for inspection. Its precise cabin attachment, dimensions and opposing rings are reconstructed. The neutron probe sections show their external tubes and end caps. The surface experiment deployment sequence and changes in payload loading are omitted from the exhibit.
