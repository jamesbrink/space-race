# Antares · Grumman LM-8

Shepard and Mitchell flew Antares from lunar orbit to Fra Mauro on 5 February 1971. Roosa had used Kitty Hawk’s engine to lower their shared orbit before separation. During powered descent Antares’s landing radar initially failed to acquire the surface. The crew cycled the radar circuit breaker and received altitude data in time to land. The Gallery shows Antares before touchdown, with the three contact probes extended and its surface equipment packed. In the launch exhibit the legs and probes fold inside SLA-17. The Gallery’s two standing stations cover the independent flight from undocking at 103:47:42 to docking at 143:32:51, elapsed from liftoff. [Apollo 14 Mission Report, table 6-I, table 8-V and §14](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_mission-report.pdf)

## Three exterior payloads

- **Quadrant IV, modular equipment transporter.** The cart travelled folded against the underside of the MESA. Its thermal blanket caught when Shepard tried to lower it. Releasing the upper pins freed the cart. The crew unfolded its wheels, legs and tow handle on the surface. The flight report gives an empty Earth weight of 25 lb and a design load of about 140 lb. The press kit gives deployed dimensions of 86 × 39 × 32 in and 16 × 4 in pneumatic tires. The model uses that tire section and the stacked wheels visible in the unstowage drawing. Isolate the cart to see it under the protective wrap. The MESA also carried the flag kit, sample containers and color television camera. [Mission Report, §3.7 and appendix A.2.1](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_mission-report.pdf) · [Press Kit, p. 68](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A14_PressKit.pdf) · [Final Lunar Surface Procedures, §3.1, figs. 3.1-2, 3.1-3 and 3.1-5](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a14/as14-flsproc.pdf)
- **Quadrant I, laser ranging retroreflector.** The crew deployed the covered panel about 100 ft west of the ALSEP central station. McDonald Observatory received a return before Antares lifted off. The exterior carrier in the Gallery shows the frame and dust cover. [Mission Report, §3.2, fig. 3-6 and appendix A.2.1](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_mission-report.pdf)
- **Quadrant II, portable magnetometer.** The folded tripod, readout and cable reel rode outside the science bay. On the second moonwalk the crew carried the instrument to two locations and reported field strengths of 102 ± 10 gammas at station A and 41 ± 10 near Cone Crater. The cable proved difficult to rewind. [Mission Report, §§3.3, 14.4.3 and appendix A.2.1](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_mission-report.pdf)

## Equipment behind the blankets

The two quadrant II pallets follow the flight inventory. Subpackage 1 carried the central station and its dust detector, passive and active seismic experiments and charged-particle detector. Subpackage 2 carried the generator, suprathermal ion detector, cold-cathode gauge and hand-tool carrier. The fuel cask rode outside the bay in its cradle. The flight press kit gives the generator envelope as approximately 18 in high and 16 in across the fins and the graphite cask as 23 in long and 8 in diameter. [Mission Report, appendix A.4, table A-I](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_mission-report.pdf) · [Press Kit, pp. 38–39](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A14_PressKit.pdf)

The four descent and two ascent batteries carry revised covers with their stiffening ribs on the outside. The assumed nominal cases measure 16.94 × 9.04 × 9.96 in for the descent type and 35.75 × 4.95 × 7.70 in for the ascent type. The ascent battery’s negative terminal moved to the opposite end of the case. These changes accompanied revised vent manifolds and potting after the current transients observed aboard Aquarius. [Apollo Experience Report on batteries, table I](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19720024228/downloads/19720024228.pdf) · [Mission Report, appendix A.2.2](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_mission-report.pdf)

## Reconstruction limits

- The cabin, gear linkages, pressure vessels, propulsion hardware and crew stations use the [shared Grumman reconstruction](lunar-module.md). The nominal 31 ft deployed span and 23 ft 1 in vehicle height describe the pads and antenna, respectively. The deployed contact probes extend below the pads. The 166 in descent-stage width and 92 in crew-compartment diameter are assumed from earlier LMs. Blanket panel seams, colors and folds are estimates.
- The folded cart frame, handle, wheel centers and retaining blanket are reconstructed from the procedures illustrations. The cart stays packed in both exhibits. Tire inflation changes, detailed pin mechanisms, geology tools loaded during the EVA, wheel treads and surface deployment motions are omitted.
- The exterior instrument brackets, MESA rack, battery rib pitch, cable paths and pallet positions are estimated. The science bay represents the closed instrument packages. The dust detector, individual corner cubes under the cover, internal experiment components, battery potting and vent manifolds are omitted. The portable magnetometer’s cable stays wound.
- The selectable antenna, gear and staging motions are inspection aids. Their hinge angles and travel are approximate. Both crew hammocks remain rolled, and the crew stations have static instruments.
