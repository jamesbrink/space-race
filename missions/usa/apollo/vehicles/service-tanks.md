# Inside service module 101

Apollo 7 carried its main engine in a 44-inch central compartment. Two spherical helium bottles sat above the engine. Four tall propellant tanks filled the sectors surrounding it. Oxygen, hydrogen and three fuel cells occupied a fifth sector. The sixth was available for NASA equipment. The Apollo 7 press kit, p. 26a, identifies the contents sector by sector. The *Apollo 7 Mission Report*, MSC-PA-R-68-9, §§5.8.1, 5.9 and A.1.8, records what the fuel cells, cryogenic system and engine did in flight.

The gallery keeps the propellant and power equipment whole while the barrel, bulkheads and radial webs open. The four propellant tanks, two helium bottles and power equipment stay with the barrel when it separates from the engine. As in Gemini's equipment adapter, interior equipment builds at the inventory level of detail. The nozzle extension remains on every exterior model.

NASA TN D-7375, *Service Propulsion Subsystem*, p. 6, describes the hemispherical heads and shortened straight sections retained for Block II. Each surface is revolved about its own table centreline. Apollo coordinates map to Blender as `(−ZA, YA, XA − 706)` before adding the launch stack offset. The polar fittings and simplified external transfer and feed lines are **estimated** from the installation photograph P-212 and aft view P-214, *Apollo News Reference*, pp. 161, 163. The illustrated connections meet under the aft bulkhead.

The *News Reference*, p. 162, gives 40 inches across each aluminium helium vessel. The model **assumes** this Block II size. The two centres at XA916 and 964 are **estimated** from the stacked arrangement on Apollo 7 press-kit p. 26a. Plumbing internal to the tanks is outside this reconstruction.

## Electrical power bay

The three powerplants made electricity and water. Cell 2 sat inboard and ran warmer than 1 and 3, a difference discussed in the mission report, §5.8.1, pp. 5-40–41. The oxygen pair sat together below the powerplant shelf. One hydrogen tank stood above the other. The press-kit plate and training plate ST-1355 show this arrangement. Numbers on the two hydrogen tank installations have not been independently identified.

| Hardware | Common nominal size used on 101 | Source and confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Three Pratt & Whitney fuel cells | 22 in diameter × 44 in high | NAR *Apollo News Reference*, Electrical Power Subsystem, pp. 100–101, photograph P-141. **Assumed** nominal dimensions |
| Two oxygen dewars | 26.55 in outer diameter | Same, p. 100. **Assumed** outer jacket diameter |
| Two hydrogen dewars | 31.80 in outer diameter | Same, p. 100. **Assumed** outer jacket diameter |

The **estimated** oxygen centres are at XA920. The hydrogen centres are at XA858.5 and 892.1. Cell jackets sit on the shelf at XA943.5. In the model these positions reproduce the sector arrangement and keep the vessels clear of each other and the outer wall. The jacket, upper accessories and exposed rack on each fuel cell follow P-141. The oxygen accessory head follows the installation photograph P-69 on p. 58. Accessory contours, exact mounting locations, finishes and fittings are **estimated**. Published outer diameters describe the spheres separately from the neck assemblies.

## The radiator panels

The two environmental-control radiators occupy sectors II and III and sectors V and VI, 130 degrees per side. The dedicated *Apollo News Reference* Environmental Control Subsystem chapter, p. 121, gives about 49 square feet per panel. Page 133 confirms their integration into the shell. The overview in the Service Module chapter on p. 58 gives a conflicting approximate area of 30 square feet. At a 77-inch radius, 49 square feet over 130° gives a **derived** height of 40.39 inches. NASA's photograph S68-42513 of spacecraft 101 being mated to its adapter on 5 August 1968 supports that proportion against the 154-inch barrel. The model uses the subsystem chapter's area as an **assumed** common nominal and places the lower edge **an estimated 10 inches** above the aft bulkhead. The eight fairing radiators alternate with eight plain panels, per the flight's press kit p. 26a and the *News Reference* pp. 55, 58. Their nominal arrangement and counts are known. Their exact clocking and finish on 101 are **estimated**. The model shows the flight surfaces and omits the ground covers and red removal tags visible in the photograph.

## National markings

NASA photograph **S68-42513**, taken on 5 August 1968 before mating to the adapter, shows the two-line black UNITED STATES above a fifty-star flag. Both straddle the beam seam between the two visible quadrants. The +Z CM/SM umbilical is on the right in this view, which places the marking over the II/III seam (model azimuth about 135°). Only the visible set is reproduced. [NASA photograph](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S68-42513)

In the 1514 × 1920 image, the barrel is about 620 pixels across for 154 inches and about 676 pixels high on the marked meridian for 155 inches. The line widths are about 122 and 127 pixels; letter height is 28 pixels. The projected horizontal width depends on azimuth. Correcting for the roughly 14° offset of the seam from the centre of the image gives **estimated** widths of 30.8 and 32.3 inches and capital height 6.5 inches. The top edges are 3.3 and 11.3 inches below the forward bulkhead. The flag top is 19.6 inches below it and its height is about 7.4 inches. The text uses the repository's bold face and shares the fifty-star flag texture with Mercury and Gemini. The visible stencil outlines and pigments are approximated. The 0.12-inch display stand-off is for depth separation.

## Structure and engine

The 50°, 60° and 70° sectors occur in opposite pairs, documented on press-kit p. 26a. NAA A-512S, *Structures and Mechanical Subsystems*, January 1969, Block II plate on printed p. 66, locates the six radial beams relative to +Y and +Z. The model uses an **estimated absolute clocking** of 65°, 135°, 195°, 245°, 315° and 15° from Blender +X, with the relative sector widths from the flight plate. The *News Reference*, p. 55, gives the central compartment diameter and describes the milled beams and two bulkheads. The bulkheads use the XA838 and 993 preflight stations. The model's 0.35-inch web section and 0.65-inch deck section are **estimated**. The aft tank openings follow the intersection of the nominal domes with the inner face of the deck, plus an **estimated** 0.8-inch clearance. The two deck faces and hole rims have finite depth.

The engine was gimbal-mounted at the aft bulkhead. Press-kit p. 26a, mission report §A.1.8 and *News Reference* figures P-211, P-213 and P-214 identify the visible extension, ablative chamber, injector and valve assembly, gimbal ring, struts, two actuators and feed elbows. NAA drawing ST-304A locates the extension attachment 20 inches aft of the bulkhead. The model retains the 112-inch extension and 94.5-inch exit diameter of the *News Reference*'s detailed description on p. 162. The overview on p. 159 gives 41 inches for the engine above the extension. The later handbook, p. 2.4-43, gives 98.4 inches inside at the exit and a 159.944-inch maximum engine length. These are conflicting published reference envelopes. The installation on 101 is **assumed**, with the intermediate contour, wall, shoulders, mount fittings and accessory profiles **estimated** from the illustrations. Both rim and bore are modelled, with the throat continuing into the chamber at full detail.

## Simplifications and things still wrong

- The tank reference surfaces use nominal dimensions. The handbook describes the propellant diameters as inside measurements. The manufactured sheet, insulation, skirts, internal reservoirs, gauges and actual clearances are unresolved.
- External plumbing shows the principal connections in simplified form. Flight-specific routes, flex lines, heat exchangers, control hardware, wiring and instrumentation remain incomplete.
- The six machined webs have a uniform model section. Bulkhead laminate, exact cut-outs, fasteners, sector-panel fixings and forward compression/tension fittings remain simplified. The central engine and helium mounts still lack their detailed attachment hardware.
- Fuel-cell accessories are reconstructed from a single common hardware photograph. The reconstruction omits the sector's relay box, servicing panel, sequencers, fuel-cell cooling circuits and individual connections.
- The gallery supplies visual equipment geometry. It assigns no propulsion performance or flight controls from it.
