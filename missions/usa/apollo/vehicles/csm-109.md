# CSM-109 · Odyssey

James Lovell flew in the left couch, Jack Swigert in the centre and Fred Haise on the right. Swigert turned Odyssey back toward the spent third stage and docked with Aquarius. The docking tunnel stayed in use during the return, when the crew lived in Aquarius and began preparing Odyssey for entry. [Apollo 13 Mission Report, MSC-02680, §§3–4](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a13/A13_MissionReport.pdf)

## Sector IV at launch

Three fuel cells occupied the upper shelf. Cell 2 sat behind cells 3 and 1 as seen through the bay opening. Below them, two oxygen tanks shared a removable shelf. Tank 2 sat forward in the photograph, to the viewer’s left. The hydrogen tanks were stacked below, No. 1 above No. 2. Figures 4-5 through 4-8 show SM-109 at North American Rockwell before delivery to Kennedy. Figure 4-6 shows the actual oxygen shelf. It was first installed in SM-106, then removed and later installed in SM-109. [Report of Apollo 13 Review Board, ch. 4, part 2, figs. 4-4–4-8 and “Assembly and test”](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170000913/downloads/20170000913.pdf)

The independent shelf view shows the two insulated jackets, the accessory heads and their common equatorial support. Each tank was designed for 320 lb of oxygen at an operating pressure of 865–935 psia. The **assumed** outer diameters follow North American’s Block II dimensions, 26.55 in for oxygen and 31.80 in for hydrogen. The cell envelope is **assumed** at 22 in across and 44 in high. The oxygen centres at XA920, the two hydrogen centres at XA858.5 and XA892.1 and the cell shelf at XA943.5 are **estimated** stations in the common service-module reconstruction. [Review Board, ch. 4, pp. 4-2–4-7](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170000913/downloads/20170000913.pdf) · [Service tank dimensions and drawings](service-tanks.md)

## Inside oxygen tank 2

Beech serial 10024XTA0008 had two fan motors and two thermostatically controlled heaters in one tube. A separate probe measured oxygen quantity by capacitance. The probe’s inner cylinder also served as the fill and drain line. The isolated view opens the side of the heater tube to expose the motors at opposite ends and both windings. The vessel remains enclosed in the assembled shelf view. [Review Board, ch. 4, part 2, figs. 4-1 and 4-2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170000913/downloads/20170000913.pdf)

Tank 2 failed at approximately 55 hours 55 minutes into the flight. The board concluded that combustion inside tank 2 led to loss of containment and identified the closure or conduit as the likeliest failure sites. The sector IV cover departed, and the spacecraft lost its oxygen supply to the fuel cells. After jettisoning the service module before entry the crew photographed the open sector. The Gallery models the intact installation carried at launch. [Review Board, ch. 4](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170000913/downloads/20170000913.pdf) · [MSC-02680, §§3 and 7](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a13/A13_MissionReport.pdf)

## Shared hardware and limits

- The measured Block II window openings come from Columbia, the hatch pocket and console layouts from CM-106. These and the common folding couches, docking hardware, water dispenser, antenna and service engine are **assumed** for CSM-109. The service-engine exit to docking plane measures 404.25 in in the reconstruction. The mission report records use of command-module drinking water and surge oxygen during shutdown. [Apollo CSM](apollo-csm.md) · [MSC-02680, §7, consumables](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a13/A13_MissionReport.pdf)
- The shelf outline, mounting bands, head contours and line runs are **estimated** from the flight photographs and figures. The opened heater tube, windings, motor housings and probe show arrangement. Their dimensions, turns, colours and clearances are **estimated**. The model omits fan impellers, blankets, internal wiring, valves, fill-line couplings and thermostat details.
- The gauges are static. The exhibit omits in-flight damage, module icing and condensation, temporary cabin hoses, and the later survival equipment arrangements.
