# LM-4 Snoopy

John Young photographed the complete spacecraft after undocking in lunar orbit and photographed its ascent stage again at rendezvous. From the front, the ladder, forward hatch, round tracking light and rendezvous radar lie on the same centreline. The larger steerable S-band antenna stands to one side. The ladder foot carries a surface probe, giving Snoopy four probes in all. The upper struts are insulated, the lower pistons and footpads are exposed, and the underside of the descent engine is visible between them. [NASA AS10-34-5090, before staging](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap10fj/photos/34-m/hr/as10-34-5090.jpg) · [NASA AS10-34-5112, at rendezvous](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap10fj/photos/34-m/hr/as10-34-5112.jpg) · [Apollo 10 Press Kit, plates 42b, 43a and p. 48](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A10_PressKit.pdf)

A shield protected each landing-gear deployment mechanism from descent-engine exhaust. Twin handrails flanked the forward porch, as the press kit's plate 43a and the flight photographs show them. The exhibit models the shields, the handrails, the tracking lamp's bezel and lens, and the flight article's contrasting bay closure, dark blankets, metallic edging and country placard. The preflight image shows temporary covers too, including the yellow one over the descent engine. The flight model shows the exposed nozzle. [Prelaunch Mission Operation Report M-932-69-10, p. 45](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap10fj/pdf/a10-prelaunch-rep.pdf) · [NASA S69-17807, LM-4 at stacking](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a410/ap10-S69-17807HR.jpg)

Open the cutaway to find the four descent batteries. NASA moved two to quadrant I and retained two in quadrant IV, putting a pair on either side of the forward leg. The crew drew 440 ampere-hours from their combined 1,600 ampere-hour nominal capacity before staging. The electrical system could supply the docked ascent stage from the command module after the descent stage had gone. The equipment cases are visible. The switches and power transfer are static. [Apollo 10 Mission Report, MSC-00126, sections 8.3, A.2.1 and A.2.3](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/A10_MissionReport.pdf)

## Scale and reconstruction limits

- The deployed model uses the [LM-3 dimensioned reconstruction](lunar-module.md). Page 42 of the flight press kit gives 31 ft across the feet and 22 ft 11 in in height. The model measures 23 ft 1 in to the antenna crest using LM-3 stations. The two-inch difference is unresolved. The Apollo 10 press kit quotes a 14 ft 1 in descent-stage diameter. The model's 166 in is the width across the blanket flats in the LM-3 drawing. [Apollo 10 Press Kit, p. 43](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A10_PressKit.pdf)
- The separated ascent stage retains the cabin, docking socket, radar, antennas, engine and upper tanks. Stage stations, fine panel layouts and propulsion envelopes follow the shared LM reconstruction. The four RCS outriggers use the type geometry in MIT R-567 §6.4.1.4, assumed for LM-4. The mission report records controller-isolation switches, the revised attitude-control selectors, VHF ranging and redundant power feeds. The cabin represents the common arrangement. Individual wiring and mission-specific switch legends are omitted. [Mission Report, appendix A.2](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/A10_MissionReport.pdf)
- The black forward surrounds represent the anti-glare finish. The crew saw paint blister outboard of the right window. Individual paint defects, loose insulation, wrinkles, and local seam paths are unresolved. The profiles of the deployment shields, hatch fittings, bay closure, bracketry and battery cases are estimated. The porch is modelled from the mission plate. Its surface texture is simplified. [Mission Report, section 8.2](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/A10_MissionReport.pdf)
- The mission carried supports and mass simulators for the modular equipment storage assembly. The enclosed simulators, experiment-package deployment mechanism, inactive radioisotope-generator cask and its instrumentation are omitted. The stack shows illustrative unfolding and staging movements. [Mission Report, sections 8 and 15 and appendix A.2.1](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/A10_MissionReport.pdf)
