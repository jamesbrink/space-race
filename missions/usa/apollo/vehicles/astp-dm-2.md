# Apollo–Soyuz docking module DM-2

The last Saturn IB carried DM-2 in its adapter with the narrow Apollo tunnel facing the engine of CSM-111 and the wide compatible docking system DS-5 at the bottom. After reaching orbit and separating, Apollo reversed direction, docked its probe to the Apollo end and pulled the module free of its three-point launch truss. [Apollo–Soyuz press kit, pp. 75–83](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750015551) · [Mission evaluation, §2 and appendix A](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154)

## Pressure vessel and interfaces

Two rolled aluminium half-cylinders were welded to make the 1.42 m internal diameter pressure vessel. The press kit gives a wall thickness of 1.58 cm. A tapered bulkhead and short tunnel formed the Apollo end. A bolted bulkhead on the other end carried the compatible docking system. The two interfaces were 3.15 m apart. The structural ring compressed a seal after six attenuators absorbed the approach and three guide petals aligned the capture ring. Eight structural latches secured the hard dock. Both hatches opened inward and each carried a pressure gauge and equalisation valve. DM-2 weighed 2,006.3 kg at insertion. [Mission evaluation, appendix A §§A.1.1 and A.2, and table F-I](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154) · [Press kit, pp. 76–79](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750015551)

## Airlock and instruments

The Apollo end carried the two oxygen bottles and the Soyuz end the two nitrogen bottles. The external sphere pairs had multilayer insulation and protective jackets. Inside, an equipment bay carried life support, communications and bilingual controls. The crew could close both hatches and adjust the gas mix and pressure before crossing to the other spacecraft. The external experiment housings carried ultraviolet spectrometry and Doppler tracking hardware. The three VHF/FM whips and the circular Soyuz periscope target are separate shapes. [Mission evaluation, figs. A-4, A-5 and A-27](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154) · [Prelaunch mission operation report, p. 12](https://www.nasa.gov/history/diagrams/astp/apol_soyuz.htm)

## Simplifications

- The cradle station is estimated from the launch diagram. The truss members, seating brackets and exact mounting azimuth are reconstructed, with a small inspection gap at the brackets in the stowed view.
- The airlock’s rounded vessel and taper follow reported dimensions and the profile drawing. The shape of the stowed whips, gas covers, mount details and thermal finish are estimated from drawings and the in-flight view AST-32-2691. The jacket seams and fasteners are simplified.
- The cutaway shows representative bay cabinets, switches, hatches and handholds. Controls and pressure equalisation are inspection geometry. The Gallery’s axial extraction motion represents separation from the cradle. It omits the transposition turn and the in-orbit APAS ring extension.
