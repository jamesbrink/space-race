# Intrepid · Grumman LM-6

Charles Conrad Jr. and Alan Bean flew Intrepid to the Ocean of Storms in November 1969. The Gallery spacecraft represents the two-stage LM in free flight, with its gear and antennas extended. The launch-stack exhibit folds the same gear and antennas into SA-507’s adapter. The independent-flight interval runs from undocking at 107:54:02 to docking at 145:36:20 mission elapsed time. The lower stage and everything attached to it stayed on the Moon. [Apollo 12 Mission Report, table 5-I and appendix A](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/A12_MissionReport.pdf)

## Exterior and cabin

Four 37-inch pads carried the landing loads. A 68-inch contact probe projected below each of three pads. The ladder leg had the flag container and commemorative plaque. The landing gear has the common 167.57-inch tread radius of the Grumman landing system. Four large truss-mounted deflectors shielded the descent stage below the downward-facing thrusters. For LM-6 Grumman changed the thermal shielding on the secondary struts and removed part of the smaller deployment-truss shield. Light metal on the secondary struts and a shortened hood represent those changes. The copper, gold and dark blanket pattern follows Conrad’s surface photography. [Apollo 12 press kit, Lunar Module](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A12_PressKit.pdf) · [Mission Report, appendix A.2](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/A12_MissionReport.pdf) · [AS12-46-6726, ladder and flag tube](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/images12.html#6726) · [AS12-46-6777, descent stage](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/images12.html#6777) · [S69-53326, LM-6 plaque](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/images12.html#S6953326)

Two hammocks gave Conrad and Bean a place to sleep between excursions. The cutaway shows two restrained rolls in the cabin. The stations, hatch, docking tunnel and drogue, tankage, engines, life support, power and landing radar use the shared Grumman geometry. The 166-inch descent-stage width, 92-inch cabin diameter and 32-inch forward hatch are assumed from those earlier vehicles. ## Scientific equipment bay and external cask

Two experiment pallets occupied the Scientific Equipment Bay on quadrant II. Facing it, the narrow left door swung sideways and the wider right door lifted. Subpackage 1 carried the central station, passive seismometer, magnetometer and solar wind spectrometer. Subpackage 2 carried the SNAP-27 generator, suprathermal ion detector with its cold cathode gauge, and the tool carrier. The deployed instruments acquired different shapes from their stowed packages. The gallery shows the stowed pallets, boom rails, folded magnetometer arms, generator fins and folding tool frame. [Apollo 12 Mission Report, appendix A.3, figures A-1 and A-2](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/A12_MissionReport.pdf) · [AS12-46-6783, right-hand pallet coming out of the open bay](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/images12.html#6783)

The empty generator measured about 18 inches high by 16 inches across its heat-radiating fins. Its fuel travelled separately in a sealed cask 23 inches long and 8 inches in diameter with hemispherical ends. The cask stood upright in its own cradle just left of the bay. On the surface the astronauts tipped it down before transferring the capsule into the generator. The nominal electrical output was at least 63 watts. [Apollo 12 press kit, pp. 43–44](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A12_PressKit.pdf) · [AS12-46-6777, upright cask](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/images12.html#6777) · [AS12-46-6786, surface unloading position](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/images12.html#6786)

## MESA and color television camera

The modular equipment stowage assembly beside the forward leg carried the two lunar sample return cases and a refurbished Westinghouse color camera originally flown on Apollo 10. The camera had a white thermal finish and a zoom lens. It initially transmitted from the rack. The crew lost the picture while transferring the camera to its surface position. The model includes the carrier, white housing, zoom barrel and cable connector. [Apollo 12 press kit, p. 28](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A12_PressKit.pdf) · [Apollo 12 Mission Report, section 14.3.1](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/A12_MissionReport.pdf) · [AS12-46-6756, the television camera](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a12/images12.html#6756)

## Reconstruction limits

The thermal blanket pattern, extent of the trimmed truss shields, individual instrument envelopes, pallet arrangement, rack and cradle attachments, camera dimensions and mount, and both hammock stowage positions are estimated. The booms, tapes, cabling, cloth and individual tool fittings are simplified. The plaque is represented by its cover. The slider illustrates inspection and separation, with approximate deployment hinge paths. The exhibit stops before surface unloading, instrument deployment, probe buckling or landing-gear compression. The shared cabin and propulsion installations retain the assumptions in the [lunar module hardware notes](lunar-module.md).
