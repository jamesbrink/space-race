# Saturn V SA-503 and the LTA-B payload

Apollo 8 left Pad 39A on 21 December 1968 on the third Saturn V. The gallery shows its launch configuration. The adapter opens in the exploded exhibit to expose LTA-B, the instrumented article that stayed with the third stage.

## Dimensions and datums

The model uses inches, with the lower plane of the five F-1 exits at zero. To place a point given in Saturn vehicle stations, add 115 in: the engine exits were at approximately STA −115. NASA’s launch configuration drawing distinguishes stage separation planes from the ends of overlapping pieces. The S-II extends aft to STA 1541, but the first separation is at STA 1564. Summing the individual rounded lengths would count the overlap twice.

## What is inside

The S-IC has separate tanks below and above an intertank. The smaller RP-1 tank is aft. The S-II has a common bulkhead, with oxygen below hydrogen. Stage cutaways show the domed ends and ring baffles. SA-503’s S-IVB-500-series upper stage shares the Apollo 7 exhibit’s Douglas S-IVB tank geometry and single J-2 engine geometry, registered at the upper-stage forward plane. Its conical interstage widens to the preceding S-II. The earlier Saturn IB has a cylindrical interstage there. Five more J-2s are visible inside the lower collar. The F-1 bells carry their broad exhaust manifolds and lower extension bands.

The instrument ring follows the equipment layout drawn for SA-503. MSFC-MAN-503 Figure 7-4, sheets 2–5, places four batteries on cold plates 4 and 5, the access door at 8, flight control computer at 16, data adapter and digital computer at 19, inertial platform at 21, and its nitrogen supply at 22. The schematic cases, cold plates and vessels are estimates of the illustrated envelopes.

The four upper panels on SLA-11 sprang clear of the spacecraft. LTA-B’s steel cylinder and four triangular aluminium supports remained attached inside the fixed section. Its six accelerometers measured the loads during flight. The assembly follows NASA’s prelaunch side elevation. The forward lip frames a grille of two beams each way, visible in crew photograph AS08-16-2582. The lip width, beam widths, pitch and recess are estimated from the photographed face.

## Simplifications

- The F-1 pitch circle, intermediate bell profiles of the F-1, fairing and fin sections, stringers, markings, colours, flag sizes and individual attachment stations are estimates. The font is the project’s Liberation Sans substitute.
- Each F-1 above its bell is a plain chamber and dome carried to the heat shield by a short post. The turbopump, the gimbal block, the actuators and their struts, the thrust posts and the holddown posts are not modelled, and the heat shield is a flat disc without its openings. The retrorockets inside the fairings are omitted.
- Complete as-built tank profiles remain unresolved. The intertank crowns, engine mount heights and LOX ring interfaces have source controls above. The profiles between them, other weld stations, baffles and material finishes are estimates. The exhibit omits detailed feed lines, most thrust framework, engines’ control hardware, interstage ordnance, retro motors, ullage motors and exterior umbilical fittings.
- The S-IVB-205 tank reconstruction supplies assumed common geometry for the -503 hardware. Changes in restart plumbing, helium equipment and individual stage fittings remain unresolved.
- The instrument-ring cases show locations from the SA-503 drawing. Case sizes and depth, absolute roll orientation, plumbing, harnesses and connectors lack an installation survey.
- LTA-B’s accelerometers, mount fittings, hidden end construction and internal ballast are unresolved. Panel thickness, spring hardware and the path of each jettisoned petal are approximate. The exploded view is an exhibit sequence.
