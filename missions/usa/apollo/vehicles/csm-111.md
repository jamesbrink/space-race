# CSM-111 · Apollo–Soyuz Test Project

CSM-111 was built for a lunar mission and reconfigured to fly in Earth orbit with Thomas Stafford, Vance Brand and Donald Slayton. It retained the Block II command module and its probe-and-drogue docking system. During transposition the Apollo probe captured the drogue in the American end of the docking module. The other end of that module carried the new androgynous docking mechanism for Soyuz. [Apollo–Soyuz Mission Evaluation Report JSC-10607, §§3 and A.1, fig. A-3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154)

## Cabin

Modifications included experiment controls, command-module-to-docking-module umbilicals, a video tape recorder and equipment for the ATS-6 relay. Video from any of five input stations could go to that recorder, whose capacity was 30 minutes. The station in Soyuz and the two in the docking module fed video to the same Apollo equipment by cable. The exhibit presents a reconstructed recorder control face and a separate experiment panel. The main display console follows the later Block II reconstruction. [JSC-10607, §A.3.1 and figs. A-20 and A-26](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154) · [ASTP Operations Handbook JSC-08469, chapter 1, spacecraft prelaunch checks](https://ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/19750065564_1975065564.pdf)

The crew carried equipment for a contingency return with two additional occupants. The launch stowage drawing identifies restraints and helmets for the Soyuz crew alongside experiment equipment. NASA also prepared a different load for a prelaunch decision to mount a rescue. The Gallery depicts the normal three-couch arrangement. [JSC-10607, §§A.3.2 and A.3.3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154)

## Service module

The Earth-orbit spacecraft carried two SPS sump tanks and one pressurant sphere. In contrast to the Skylab ferries, it used three fuel cells and flew without the added descent battery pack and auxiliary water tank. Fuel cells 1 and 2 powered bus A and cell 3 powered bus B. The main engine fired nine times for a cumulative 17 seconds. This included insertion into a circular orbit, rendezvous burns, Doppler tracking manoeuvres and the deorbit burn. Short firings blistered the protective film on the nozzle. [JSC-10607, §§4.2.9–4.2.10, table A-I and fig. A-20](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154)

The auxiliary propellant module could feed the four reaction-control clusters through its distribution manifold. The exhibit assumes the Skylab tank envelopes and arrangement for the ASTP rack. The shared steerable high-gain antenna is mounted around the aft perimeter at the 52°15′ bearing in the ASTP press kit. On the flight its upgraded radio link used the Applications Technology Satellite 6 in geosynchronous orbit as a relay. [ASTP press kit, pp. 71–72](https://www.nasa.gov/history/diagrams/astp/apol_soyuz.htm) · [JSC-10607, §§A.3, A.4 and fig. A-29](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154) · [Skylab Operations Handbook vol. I, §2.4](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160013589)

## Science bay

Three fixed instruments were mounted in service module bay I. A proportional counter detected soft X-rays for MA-048. The telescope for MA-083 had an optical assembly, filter wheel and detector. The four electron multipliers for MA-088 looked at the helium lines at 584 Å and 304 Å. Each instrument had a remotely controlled cover. The reconstructed small cover openings leave the surrounding service-module shell in place. Use the assembly slider to swing the covers and use the *Bay I instruments* view to examine the optics. [JSC-10607, §§6.2, A.5.9, A.5.11, A.5.12 and fig. A-29](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154)

## Simplifications

- The aft service bulkhead, skin, common Block II cabin, hatches, probe, engine and antenna profiles use the established Apollo reconstruction. Nozzle blistering, radio plumbing, modified dock wiring and most mission stowage are omitted.
- The remotely controlled covers and instrument stations are reconstructed from the experiment location diagram. Detailed shapes, hinge and sweep, coatings, instrument apertures and the internal arrangement of the bay are estimates. The hinge shafts seat against the rim while the covers swing clear of the surrounding panels. The models expose the detector faces and housings, with optics and electronics represented by their exterior envelopes.
- Cabin recorder and science panel cases, switch positions and legends represent the documented functions. The added controls are presented as reconstructions with estimated placement and dimensions.
