# Eagle, lunar module 5

Eagle is displayed twice. In SLA-14 its legs, probes and steerable S-band antenna are folded. The independent exhibit shows the spacecraft after extraction and gear deployment, before touchdown. The footpads define the landing plane. Three probes extend below it. There was no probe beneath the forward ladder. The model uses the common gear reconstruction, 167.57 inches from centreline to each pad centre and 37 inches across a pad. The 67-inch sensing reach is nominal. The April processing photograph S69-32396 still shows the forward probe. The July configuration follows the flight report and Collins’s inspection photograph. [Apollo 11 Mission Report, appendix A.2.1–2](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A11_MissionReport.pdf) · [NASA image AS11-44-6574](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/images11.html#6574) · [Common gear and stage dimensions](lunar-module.md)

Four deflectors sit on tubular trusses under the downward-firing attitude jets. During ascent the thrusters rose with the crew while the trusses, deflectors and gear stayed on the Moon. The crouched upper cabin, copper and black descent-stage blankets, forward ladder, flag carrier and covered commemorative plaque follow the flight photographs. Armstrong used the porch and ladder to reach the surface. The silver tube beside the ladder carried the flag and staff. [Grumman News Reference, p. The equipment rack in forward quadrant IV held the black-and-white television camera and the sample return boxes. Armstrong released the rack by lanyard from the porch. Its camera transmitted the first steps while still attached. The camera body measures 10.6 × 6.5 × 3.4 inches. Across the descent stage in quadrant II, the crew unloaded the passive seismic and laser retroreflector packages through the scientific equipment bay doors. Facing the bay, Aldrin withdrew the seismic package on the left. Its folded solar wings flank the sensor drum. The laser reflector on the right rode under its protective cover, above a pallet with an alignment handle. The left door swung sideways and the right door lifted. ## Reconstruction limits

Shield profiles, truss attachments, individual bay envelopes, stowage brackets, package details, camera mounts, flag carrier and plaque cover are estimated. Foil panels simplify the overlapping blankets. The rack and science packages are shown stowed. The film magazine, instruments and crew stations represent the layout visible in the mission photographs. Small legends, loose equipment, wiring and individual instrument settings are incomplete. The shared pressure-cabin and propulsion-system envelopes retain the assumptions in the [lunar module hardware notes](lunar-module.md). The cutaways and slider are inspection arrangements. The ground exhibit does not reproduce probe buckling or landing-gear compression.
