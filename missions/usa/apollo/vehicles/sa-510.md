# Saturn V SA-510 and spacecraft adapter SLA-19

SA-510 launched Endeavour and Falcon from LC-39A on July 26, 1971. The nominal 363-foot stack put SLA-19 over IU-510, S-IVB-510, S-II-10 and S-IC-10. The postflight reconstruction gives 6,494,415 lb at ground ignition and 6,394,652 lb at holddown-arm release. The lower parking orbit, changed propellant allowances and lighter staging hardware helped carry the first lunar rover and the service module’s scientific instrument module. [AS-510 Flight Evaluation Report, p. xv and table 16-2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025086) · [Apollo 15 Press Kit, pp. 130–133, 136](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A15_PressKit.pdf)

## Four retrorockets behind two fins

The forward-facing retrorockets were hidden beneath the first stage’s tapered fairings. On SA-510 only fins A and C carried them, two at each fin, canted 7.5° from the vehicle centreline. Their exhaust broke through the forward fairing skin when they fired. In the Gallery, the inspection views remove the surrounding skin so the case bands, mountings and nozzles can be seen. The lower interstage lacks the four external S-II ullage-motor fairings seen on SA-509. [Saturn V Flight Manual SA-510, §IV and fig. 4-20](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap15fj/pdf/as15-sa510-flight-manual.pdf) · [Flight evaluation, §10.6 and appendix B](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025086)

The first and second stages separated, but the F-1 engines’ residual thrust exceeded the prediction used to time the separation. The J-2 exhaust reached the spent first stage closely enough to damage its telemetry equipment. The review reinstated eight retro motors for subsequent first stages. The S-II centre engine retained its helium-filled oxygen-line accumulator, now on its second flight. [Flight evaluation, §§6.9, 10.6 and 15.3.2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025086)

## After Falcon left the adapter

The upper stage and instrument unit continued toward the Moon after Worden docked Endeavour and extracted Falcon. SA-510’s flight manual shows the CCS transponder, power amplifier and coaxial switch mounted above battery 6D20 on cold plate 24. The transponder was still operating at impact, 79:24:41.55 into the flight, corrected for signal travel time. Earth tracking stations recorded the loss of signal at 79:24:42.85–42.90. The Apollo 12 and 14 seismometers recorded it. [Flight manual, fig. 7-4 sheet 2](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap15fj/pdf/as15-sa510-flight-manual.pdf) · [Flight evaluation, tables 11-4 and 17-5](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025086) · [Apollo 15 Mission Report, §13](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a15/a15mr-13.htm)

## Reconstruction limits

The stage shells, tank domes, F-1 and J-2 contours and common bulkheads use the shared Saturn V reconstruction. The SA-510 manual identifies the occupied fairings and cant. The retro case sizes, nozzle and lug profiles, colours, distances between the pairs and positions inside the fairings are estimates from figure 4-20. The firing harnesses and frangible fairing panels are omitted. The S-II systems tunnel and accumulator use adjacent-flight profiles. IU radio cases use the arrangement in SA-510 figure 7-4, sheet 2. Their dimensions and the white cork patch follow the SA-508 reconstruction. The Gallery slider separates the stages for inspection.
