# Saturn IB SA-210

SA-210 was the last Saturn launched. Its S-IB-10 had already been finished and static-fired in 1967 when NASA put it in storage. After checks at the Cape, it carried CSM-111 and DM-2 off Pad 39B on 15 July 1975. The primary configuration lists S-IVB-210, S-IU-210 and SLA-18. The backup vehicle was SA-209. [Apollo–Soyuz press kit, pp. 80–84](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750015551) · [ASTP technical data, US vehicle configuration](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750008514)

## Stages

- **S-IB-10** used eight H-1s, each rated at 205,000 pounds-force at sea level. The four fixed inboards stopped at 136.6 s and the four gimballed outboards 3.6 s later. Four retromotors helped the empty booster clear the S-IVB. The white outer fuel tanks and the dark and light division of the upper skirt follow NASA's rollout photo S75-24007. [Press kit, p. 82](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750015551) · [Mission evaluation, §13.3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154) · [NASA rollout photographs](https://www.nasa.gov/history/astp/kipp.html)
- **S-IVB-210** used a single J-2 rated at 225,000 pounds-force and reached insertion at 595.7 s. The launch team added two orbital-coast hydrogen vent events to accommodate the CSM's approach and docking with the airlock. On revolution four, the upper stage started its deorbit sequence. [Press kit, p. 82](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750015551) · [Mission evaluation, §13.3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760023154)
- **S-IU-210** housed guidance, sequencing and telemetry. After the docking module was extracted, the instrument unit commanded the stage’s deorbit on revolution four. **SLA-18** had four upper panels that swung away and jettisoned after CSM separation. The remaining structure carried the launch support for [DM-2](astp-dm-2.md). [ASTP technical data, US vehicle configuration](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750008514) · [Press kit, pp. 75 and 83](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750015551)

## Dimensions and reconstruction

The press kit gives about 224 ft overall. The model uses the SA-206 manual's reference stations in inches, with the S-IB top at 962.304, stage separation plane at 1186.804, instrument unit from 1662.859 to 1698.859 and the aft service module interface at 2034.859. The Q-ball is at 2681.859 in (68.119 m). The first-stage fin span is 40.7 ft, assumed from sister vehicle drawings. [Press kit, p. 82](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750015551) · [Saturn IB flight manual, fig. 1-1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740021163)
