# Orion · Grumman LM-11

Young and Duke flew Orion to the Cayley plains in the Descartes highlands. The free-standing Gallery exhibit shows the approach to touchdown, with four legs extended, three contact probes and the surface payload still aboard. The same builder folds the gear for launch. The standing stations span undocking at 096:13:31 through redocking at 177:41:18 mission elapsed time. Orion’s ascent stage returned to Mattingly in Casper. The descent stage and the rover remained on the Moon. [NASA SP-4029, Apollo 16 timeline](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4029/Apollo_16i_Timeline.htm)

## Packed around the descent stage

- **Quadrant I carried the second lunar rover.** Its front and rear frames folded against the center chassis, and the four independent wheel assemblies turned inboard. Each 32 × 9 inch wheel had a mesh tire, an inner bump stop and chevron treads. The extended rover was 122 inches long on a 90-inch wheelbase. New dust-covered bags rode beneath the crew seats. Young and Duke reset the walking hinges before lowering it and had to lock three wheels by hand. [Apollo 16 Press Kit, pp. 105–111](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A16_PressKit.pdf) · [Apollo 16 Mission Report, p. 9-21 and appendix A.3.2](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a16/A16_MissionReport.pdf)
- **Quadrant II held the ALSEP subpackages.** The central-station pallet carried the passive seismometer, magnetometer and active seismic equipment. Alongside it, the second pallet carried the SNAP-27 generator, heat-flow probe and electronics boxes and the stowed active seismic equipment. The generator’s fuel capsule rode separately in its cask outside this bay. The heat-flow cable broke during deployment. [Mission J-2 Science Planning Document, figs. 2-14 to 2-18](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/ALSEP/pdf/ALSEP%20%23702%20-%20Mission%20Sci%20Plan%20Doc_Apollo%2016_040572.pdf) · [Apollo 16 Mission Report, §4](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a16/A16_MissionReport.pdf)
- **On the neighboring aft wall, a four-panel cosmic-ray detector collected particles during the outbound flight.** Each stack rode above the previous one in a single frame. The exposed area of panel 2 was 14.7 × 22.6 cm. Pulling a lanyard on the surface uncovered fresh detector material on panel 4. The crew collected all four panels during their final excursion. The arrays recorded particles from the April 17 solar flare as well as the quieter wind. [Mission J-2 science plan, fig. 2-32](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/ALSEP/pdf/ALSEP%20%23702%20-%20Mission%20Sci%20Plan%20Doc_Apollo%2016_040572.pdf) · [NASA SP-315, §15A](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730013020) · [S-152 final report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19730022038.pdf)
- **Quadrant III carried the far-ultraviolet camera and a separate rover equipment pallet.** The camera’s three-inch Schmidt aperture, film transport, spectrograph, pointing circles, tripod and battery are individually visible in the Gallery’s isolated view. Young placed the telescope in Orion’s shadow and its battery in sunlight. The 35 mm film transport returned to Earth. The neighboring pallet carried the hand-tool carrier, portable magnetometer, penetrometer, geology tools and sample bags. [Final Lunar Surface Procedures, fig. 3.4-1](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a16/a16.lsp194.jpg) · [Apollo 16 Press Kit, pp. 68–73](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A16_PressKit.pdf) · [Mission J-2 science plan, fig. 2-33b](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/ALSEP/pdf/ALSEP%20%23702%20-%20Mission%20Sci%20Plan%20Doc_Apollo%2016_040572.pdf) · [Flight photograph AS16-114-18439](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a16/AS16-114-18439.jpg)
- **Quadrant IV held the MESA.** Young lowered it before Duke left the cabin. The rack carried the surface drill, crew cameras, ground-commanded TV equipment, sample return containers and tools. [Final Lunar Surface Procedures, fig. 3.1-2](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a16/a16.lsp025.jpg) · [Apollo 16 Mission Report, pp. 9-20–9-21](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a16/A16_MissionReport.pdf)

## Tanks, engines and crew stations

The four descent propellant tanks carried 7,530.4 lb of fuel and 12,028.9 lb of oxidizer at launch. Its two oxygen tanks held 46.7 and 46.6 lb. Its two water tanks held 193.9 and 199.4 lb. The ascent stage carried a separate 2,017.8 lb of fuel and 3,224.7 lb of oxidizer. Battery case separators, vent limits and plate tabs changed for this flight. A manual shutoff valve on the descent battery cooling loop allowed the crew to control temperature. Young flew from the left station and Duke from the right. [Apollo 16 Mission Report, §§7.8.1–7.8.5 and appendix A.2](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a16/A16_MissionReport.pdf)

## Reconstruction limits

The Apollo 16 press kit gives 31 ft across the extended gear, four 37-inch footpads, three 68-inch probes and an overall height of 22 ft 11 in. Its height endpoints are unspecified. The [shared Grumman drawing](lunar-module.md) puts the steerable antenna crest 23 ft 1 in above the pad plane. The model retains that crest, two inches above the press kit figure, and extends the three probes below the pad plane. [Apollo 16 Press Kit, pp. 144–146](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A16_PressKit.pdf) The dimensions of the J-series propellant tanks, extended descent-engine skirt and battery cases follow the [LM-10 reconstruction](lm-10.md). Local tank stations, battery terminals, skin panels, docking hardware and cabin details are approximations. The internal battery changes, coolant valve and instrument glass changes are described in the exhibit text.

The rover stays folded. Suspension angles, dust-flap shapes, blanket contours and exact MESA pallet positions are estimated. The stowed camera’s orientation, bag profile, exposure of the fittings and local case sizes are reconstructed from the J-2 equipment drawings and surface photographs. The other three detector faces assume the exposed area documented for panel 2. Their internal sheet stacks, sliding window and detector materials are represented by external plates. The aft pallet shows the portable magnetometer’s readout, cable reel, folded sensor support, recording penetrometer and tongs. Their exact mount positions, straps and cases are estimated within the packed envelope. The other loose tools, internal mechanisms, operational camera movement and surface deployment sequence are omitted.
