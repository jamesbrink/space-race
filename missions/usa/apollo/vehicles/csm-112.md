# CSM-112 · Endeavour

Al Worden flew Endeavour alone while David Scott and Jim Irwin explored Hadley-Apennine. The upper gallery includes the three assigned couches, navigation station, flight instruments, working side hatch and Block II probe-and-drogue interface. A set of EVA handrails led Worden back from the command module to the film cassettes on the service module. [Apollo 15 Mission Report, MSC-05161, §§5, 9 and appendix A.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19720021182)

## Sector I on the first J mission

At launch the science bay lay behind a jettisonable curved panel. At **74:06:47** elapsed the separation charge cut the door free. The crew saw the panel traveling outward perpendicular to the longitudinal axis. The exhibit opens an actual sector in the inner and outer service-module walls. The upper cryogenic tank panel and the edges of the neighboring sectors stay in place. The handrails straddle the opening. The gallery’s four-metre separation distance is **estimated** for inspection. [MSC-05161, table 3-I, §9.5.3 and appendix A.1.1, figs. A-1–A-3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19720021182)

The instrument pallet attached to radial beams 1 and 6, the cryogenic tank panel and the aft bulkhead. Sandwich shelves carried the camera systems and spectrometers. The upper shelf held the **3-inch** mapping camera, its film cassette and the laser altimeter. The **24-inch** panoramic camera occupied the longer bay below. The two focal lengths are **documented**. The EVA foot restraint was beside the panoramic camera. Worden made separate trips on the homeward EVA to collect the two cassettes. The pan camera’s gold blanket, cassette faces, handles and black subsatellite container follow the preflight photograph. [Apollo 15 Press Kit, pp. 61–64 and 139](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A15_PressKit.pdf) · [NASA preflight photograph S71-2250-X](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/apollo_15_l-1_month_6_sim_bay_during_preflight_processing_jun_1971_s71-2250-x-hr.jpg) · [MSC-05161, appendix A.4.2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19720021182)

The aft shelf shows the gamma-ray and mass spectrometer heads at their retracted sleeves, along with the fixed alpha-particle and X-ray detector housing. The flight report gives **25 ft** for the gamma-ray boom and **24 ft** for the mass spectrometer boom. The gamma spectrometer sampled lunar composition while the mass spectrometer sampled gases. [MSC-05161, §§5.1–5.2 and appendix A.4.2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19720021182)

A spring launcher delivered the particles and fields subsatellite into lunar orbit. A dedicated view looks inside its housing at the hexagonal solar-covered body and the three folded booms. The **30-inch** axial dimension follows the postflight report. The press kit’s preflight figure was 31 inches. The **14-inch** width follows the press kit, interpreted in the mesh as the diameter over opposite vertices. The extended booms were five feet long. One carried a magnetometer while the other two balanced the spinning spacecraft. [MSC-05161, appendix A.4.2, fig. A-12](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19720021182) · [Apollo 15 Press Kit, p. 64](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A15_PressKit.pdf)

## Power and emergency oxygen

Both cryogens had third tanks on CSM-112. The science pallet and the extra reactants shared sector I. The original two pairs, three fuel cells and the auxiliary battery occupied sector IV. The press kit gives **26 lb** hydrogen capacity for the new tank and **415 Ah** for the silver oxide–zinc auxiliary battery. The oxygen isolation valve moved to the forward bulkhead. The cutaway retains the redesigned three-element oxygen heater and quantity probe from the preceding flight. The **26.55-inch oxygen** and **31.80-inch hydrogen** vessel diameters and the **16.94 × 9.04 × 9.96-inch** battery case are **assumed** from common equipment. [Apollo 15 Press Kit, pp. 136–139](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A15_PressKit.pdf) · [MSC-05161, appendix A.1.2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19720021182) · [Service tank dimensions](service-tanks.md) · [Battery and oxygen heater reconstruction](csm-110.md)

## Reconstruction limits

- The common 404.25-inch length from the service-engine exit to docking plane, 10-inch active probe stroke, antenna, drinking-water dispenser, console and hatch contours are **assumed** from sister ships. The gallery omits the instrument controls on panels 181 and 230, cover switches on panel 278 and EVA monitoring panels. The avionics displays are static. [Apollo CSM](apollo-csm.md) · [MSC-05161, appendix A.1.4](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19720021182)
- The science bay follows the flight drawing and June processing photograph. Shelf stations, door boundaries, local camera dimensions, handholds, cable runs, substrate finishes, and the hydrogen tank’s exact position are **estimated**. Instrument views expose the camera optics and detector heads for inspection. Individual covers, cassette extraction, boom deployment, the subsatellite ejector’s action, and the internal optical trains are omitted. The satellite view shows the folded booms and an estimated division of the solar cells into banks. [MSC-05161, figs. A-1–A-3 and A-12](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19720021182)
