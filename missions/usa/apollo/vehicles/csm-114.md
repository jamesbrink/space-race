# CSM-114 · America

Gene Cernan, Ron Evans and Harrison Schmitt occupied the left, centre and right couches at launch. America and Challenger weighed **66,893 lb** and **36,274 lb** respectively at transposition and docking. On the way home Evans worked outside America to bring three film cassettes into the cabin. The mission report times run from launch range zero and exclude the 2 h 40 min advance to the crew clock. [Apollo 17 Mission Report, tables 3-I and A-II](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730015117)

## Opening sector I

The sector I door was jettisoned at **81:32:40** elapsed, before lunar orbit insertion. The upper pallet carried the mapping camera, stellar camera and laser altimeter. The lower pallet held the panoramic camera. The 3-inch mapping camera and 24-inch panoramic camera returned film alongside a third instrument that recorded lunar radar echoes on film. On Evans’s spacewalk he released all three cassettes from the bay, using a foot restraint and handholds. [Apollo 17 Press Kit, pp. 56a–56c](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Apollo_17.pdf) · [Apollo 17 Mission Report, table 3-I, §§5 and 9](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730015117)

- **Lunar sounder S-209.** The coherent synthetic-aperture radar used two HF frequencies and one VHF frequency to sound the subsurface. HF elements paid out from opposed reels to form an 80-foot dipole. A seven-element VHF Yagi with a **274 cm** boom unfolded from beneath the aft heat shield when the adapter panels left. The Gallery shows the transceiver, recorder, cassette, antenna guides and folded array. [Press kit, pp. 56b–58](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Apollo_17.pdf) · [Mission report, fig. A-18](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730015117)
- **Far-ultraviolet spectrometer S-169.** The entrance baffle faced 18° right and 23° forward. A 0.5 × 6 cm entrance slit, Ebert mirror and 10 × 10 cm diffraction grating fed a photomultiplier. Evans used the instrument to look for gas above the Moon. Its separate optics view exposes the light path and grating drive. [Press kit, p. 59](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Apollo_17.pdf) · [Mission report, fig. A-16](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730015117)
- **Infrared scanning radiometer S-171.** A motor turned a tilted scan mirror. The collecting optics focused the infrared signal onto a thermistor bolometer. The survey distinguished warm objects, including rocks, on the lunar night side. [Mission report, fig. A-17](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730015117)

## Reconstruction limits

The 404.25-inch span from the service engine exit to the docking plane, crew pressure vessel, hatch and probe stroke follow the [shared Block II reconstruction](apollo-csm.md). Service tanks, fuel cells and the auxiliary battery use the adjacent J-series installation. Console faces approximate the common cabin drawings. [Apollo 16 hardware](csm-113.md)

The SIM pallet follows the flight's front elevation. Local instrument stations, instrument enclosures, detector depths and finishes are estimated from the diagrams. The optical elements show their arrangement. The model omits microscopic grating rulings, scan actuation and internal recorder mechanisms. The HF reels and VHF Yagi stay in launch stowage. The door and cutaway slider motion let the visitor inspect the pallet. They do not reconstruct a jettison trajectory or Evans’s spacewalk.
