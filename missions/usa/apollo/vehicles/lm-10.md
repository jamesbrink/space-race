# Falcon · Grumman LM-10

Falcon was the first extended-stay lunar module. The Gallery shows the spacecraft as Scott and Irwin approached Hadley–Apennine, with the landing legs out, the contact probes extended and the surface payload still packed. A second instance rides inside SLA-19 with its legs folded for launch. The Gallery’s standing stations span undocking at 100:39:16.2 through redocking at 173:36:25.5. The descent stage, its spent engine, the rover and the science equipment stayed on the Moon. [NASA SP-4029, Apollo 15 summary](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4029/Apollo_15a_Summary.htm)

## Four quadrants for a three-day stay

- **Quadrant I.** The 122-inch-long rover folded against Falcon with the aft end of its center chassis pointing up. Both end frames folded over this deck and the four independent suspensions swung inward. A strut at the top and two tripod supports at the bottom secured the package. Each wheel measured 32 inches across and 9 inches wide. Woven wire supported titanium chevron treads around a spun aluminum hub and an internal bump stop. The short amber fiberglass fenders are visible in the Kennedy stowage photograph. The crew extended them after deployment. [Apollo 15 press kit, pp. 78, 83, 85, 91, 93–95](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Apollo_15-1.pdf) · [KSC-71PC-345, Scott with the flight rover](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a15/ap15-KSC-71PC-345.jpg) · [KSC-71P-206, folded flight rover on arrival](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a15/ap15-KSC-71P-206.jpg)
- **Quadrant II.** The two ALSEP subpackages are visible with the blanket removed. The central-station pallet carried the passive seismometer, magnetometer, solar-wind spectrometer and dust detector. The other pallet carried the ion detector with its cold-cathode gauge and the heat-flow probe box and electronics. The SNAP-27 generator rode behind the heat-flow pallet, with its fuel cask outside the bay. [Mission Science Planning Document J-1, figs. 2-14–2-16](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/ALSEP/pdf/ALSEP%20%23204%20-%20Apollo%20Mission%20J-1_Mission%20Sci%20Doc%20Plan_Rev_051571.pdf)
- **Quadrant III.** The rover’s aft equipment pallet held its folding hand-tool carrier. A second pallet held the laser reflector. The 96-cube panel folded face down over part of the 204-cube panel and the entire assembly, including supports, stowed in a space 27.375 in wide, 25.50 in long and 11.75 in high. The smaller leaf swung level with its neighbor on the surface, giving observatories on Earth 300 corner cubes to range on. [ALSEP-MT-05, rev. A, table 2-2 and figs. 2-5–2-7](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/ALSEP/pdf/ALSEP%20%2323%20-%20LRRR%20Familiarization%20Manual_RevA_050171.pdf)
- **Quadrant IV.** The enlarged modular equipment stowage assembly (MESA) had a gross capacity of 600 lb. Its portable pallets made the extra stores easier to reach from the ground. This bay also accommodated a second oxygen vessel, a second water vessel and a five-gallon tank for urine and backpack condensate. The original water and oxygen vessels occupied quadrants II and III respectively. Five 415 Ah batteries supplied the descent buses from the aft bulkhead. [Apollo 15 Mission Report, appendix A.2](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a15/a15mr-a.htm) · [LM-10 Operations Handbook, §1.3, p. 1-16 and §2.11.8](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/LMA790-3-LM10-ApolloOperationsHandbookLunarModuleLM10AndSubsequent-Volume1-SubsystemsData-SearchableText.pdf)

## Propulsion and cabin

The descent-engine exit cone was lighter, the chamber lined with quartz and the nozzle extension 10 inches longer than on LM-8. The four propellant tanks were each 3.36 inches longer. The model extends the related meshes while keeping the chamber, gimbal and mount at their original stations. The flight report puts the increase in combined propellant capacity at about 1,200 lb. Above this stage, Scott and Irwin worked standing up at two triangular forward windows. Scott also used the overhead hatch for a stand-up survey after landing. [Apollo 15 Mission Report, appendix A.2.4](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a15/a15mr-a.htm) · [Apollo 15 press kit, “Lunar Module Changes”](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Apollo_15-1.pdf) · [NASA SP-4029, Apollo 15](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4029/Apollo_15a_Summary.htm)

## Reconstruction limits

- The [shared Grumman reconstruction](lunar-module.md) supplies the assumed 166-inch descent-stage envelope, 92-inch crew compartment, landing gear, cabin, drogue and ascent engine. The nominal 31-foot deployed span is measured over the pads and the 23 ft 1 in height runs from their plane to the S-band antenna. The extended contact probes project below the pads. The descent engine is shown before the skirt buckled during landing. Its altered exit radius and tank contours are estimated.
- The rover stays folded. Its wheel centers, strut fittings, shortened wire pattern, chevron spacing, seat packs, fender contours, cover and hold-down locations are reconstructed from the flight photographs and deployment diagram. The separately carried tool pallet and reflector stay stowed. The corner cubes under their dust covers, loose geology tools and operating rover controls are omitted.
- The science instruments have representative closed envelopes. Internal mechanisms, dust-detector components, individual drive units, tank fittings and most cables are omitted. Battery case sizes are assumed from the shared descent and ascent types. Local battery locations on the aft bulkhead, vessel centers, MESA fittings and panel seams are estimated. The cabin uses the shared controls and stores, including two rolled hammocks. The additional J-mission locker fittings and switch changes on panel 14 are omitted.
