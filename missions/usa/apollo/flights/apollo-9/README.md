# Apollo 9 (AS-504)

On 3 March 1969 at 16:00 UTC, James McDivitt, David Scott and Russell Schweickart lifted off from Kennedy Space Center’s Pad 39A. Scott stayed aboard **Gumdrop** while McDivitt and Schweickart flew **Spider** away, staged and returned to dock. All three splashed down in the Atlantic on 13 March. The mission tested the two spacecraft together in Earth orbit. [Apollo 9 Mission Report, sections 1–3 and table 3-I](https://coastalreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Apollo-9-mission-report.pdf)

### Flying the two dockings

**Separation and transposition** opens at the CSM’s separation from the adapter. The CSM departs, pitches through 180°, rolls 60°, holds about 60 ft clear, and returns. When the probe retracts, extraction remains a separate action. Four service-module jets back the pair away in the two reported pulses. **Transfer crew** changes the occupied spacecraft separately from the docking mechanism.

**LM rendezvous** starts at terminal phase initiation, 97:57:59. The LM starts at its tracking fix. The CSM starts at the radar geometry of 27.5° elevation and 10 nautical miles differential altitude, with the speed and flight-path angle propagated backwards from station-keeping tracking. This assumes a shared orbital plane. The LM receives the published terminal initiation and two midcourse impulses. The source reports 30, 20, 10 and 5 ft/s at 6,000, 3,000, 1,500 and 500 ft, followed by roughly 100 ft of separation for photography. The feedback controller limits thrust and the line-of-sight closing rate at these gates. Its path and valve commands between observations are reconstructed. The nominal run still has several hundred metres of axial offset at the report’s end of braking and settles into formation later. Docking time is an input to the final approach. **LM final approach** starts at the last two metres with the same port and mass properties.

The simulated 3.08-inch lateral offset and 0.300-ft/s closing speed in the first approach can be compared to the 3.2 in and 0.3 ft/s reported from film. The two RCS midcourse pulse durations use equivalent paired-thruster impulses. The first encounter uses the folded LM and S-IVB; the second uses the bare ascent stage. The five-day intervening engine programme, propellant expenditure and lateral CG offsets are outside these chapters. Sources: Apollo 9 mission report, §§5.1, 5.6 and 10.2, tables 5-I, 7-II and A.5-I; NASA TN D-6854, table II.

## Launch site

At [Pad 39A](../../../places/cape-canaveral/lc-39.md), select the white room to see the access arm at the command module. The mobile launcher is ML-2. At liftoff the computed Sun was at azimuth 143.6° and elevation 48.0°. The Moon was below the horizon at azimuth 333.5° and elevation −47.1°.
