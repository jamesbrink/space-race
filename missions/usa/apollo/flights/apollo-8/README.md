# Apollo 8 (AS-503)

On 21 December 1968 Frank Borman, Jim Lovell and Bill Anders left Earth on the first Saturn V to carry people. They spent Christmas Eve in lunar orbit. On each of their ten revolutions the Moon itself cut off radio contact with Earth. The crew had to start the engine that would bring them home on the far side. [NASA, *Apollo by the Numbers*, Apollo 8](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4029/Apollo_08a_Summary.htm)

In the Gallery, open the cutaway to look through the three rocket stages to the tanks and engines. Follow the stack upward to the instrument unit and the tapered adapter. The cylinder on four supports inside is LTA-B. It measured the loads carried by the lunar module mountings and stayed with the spent S-IVB when the crew separated. Move the assembly slider to see the four adapter panels clear the spacecraft and the high-gain antenna unfold from beside the service engine. The slider spreads events apart for inspection. [NASA, *Apollo 8 Mission Report*, appendix A, pp. A-2, A-12 and A-14](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700033031/downloads/19700033031.pdf)

**Sit in the cabin** to take Borman’s left couch, Lovell’s centre couch or Anders’s right couch. On the main console, look just below the middle panel’s long row of switches. The crew could read antenna pitch, signal strength and yaw there, point the high-gain antenna and then hand it over to automatic tracking. The panel follows the drawing for command module 103. [North American Aviation, V36-976020, sheet 1](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/V36-976020_Design_Layout_MDC_CM-103.pdf)

**Boarding window.** Borman crossed the hatch sill at 04:58 EST, Anders at 05:02 and Lovell at 05:07. The pad crew closed the hatch at 05:34. Against the 07:51 liftoff, the recorded boarding interval runs from T−2 h 53 min to T−2 h 17 min (−10,380 to −8,220 s). The seat data uses the first astronaut’s entry as the opening of that interval. [NASA launch commentary, 21 December 1968, tape 10, pp. 10/1–10/2](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/as08-pao.pdf)

## Scope

This flight opens in the Gallery and appears to scale in the Lineup. Its seats, windows, cabin lighting and static instruments can be inspected. The Launch site places SA-503 on mobile launcher 1. The timeline records the historical flight. Configuration and reconstruction limits are in [spacecraft.md](spacecraft.md).

## Launch site

At [Pad 39A](../../../places/cape-canaveral/lc-39.md), select the white room to see the access arm at the command module. The mobile launcher is ML-1. At liftoff the computed Sun was at azimuth 121.4° and elevation 6.8°. The Moon was below the horizon at azimuth 110.8° and elevation −17.5°.

The site opens at the start of that boarding window. At T−56 min 23 s, launch control reported the white room moving into its 12° standby position. [NASA launch-day commentary](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/as08-pao.pdf)
