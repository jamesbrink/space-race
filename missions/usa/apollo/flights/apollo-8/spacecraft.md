# CSM-103, Apollo 8

The launch stack carries spacecraft 103 on Saturn V SA-503. Under the service module, adapter SLA-11 encloses LTA-B. The instrumented cylinder stayed on the S-IVB after separation. The command and service modules went on to the Moon together. [NASA, *Apollo 8 Mission Report*, appendix A, pp. A-1, A-12 and A-14, fig. A.5-1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700033031/downloads/19700033031.pdf)

The complete space vehicle weighed 6,133,880 lb at liftoff. NASA reports 282,237 lb placed in Earth orbit and 46,743 lb placed in lunar orbit. Each figure describes a different configuration. [NASA-TM-X-70154, Apollo 8, printed p. 55 (PDF p. 56)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19740013403/downloads/19740013403.pdf)

The principal sources for these figures are the [Apollo 8 press kit](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A08_PressKit.pdf), [Honeywell training manual](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/apollo_scs_training_manual_block_ii.pdf) and [Apollo 8 Mission Report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700033031/downloads/19700033031.pdf).

## At the crew’s stations

The [CSM-103 hardware record](../../vehicles/csm-103.md) gives the hinge, hatch and couch configurations. The earlier flight supplies the shared Block II shell, five window assemblies, outward-opening side hatch, pressure vessel and service-module equipment. CM-103’s forward tunnel closes with one combined pressure and ablative hatch, making the tunnel part of the pressurized cabin. Each of the three couches could fold, freeing floor space after launch. At the navigator’s station a sun filter on the scanning telescope and a camera adapter on the sextant supported observations on the lunar voyage. [Mission report, appendix A, pp. A-1 and A-3, figs. A.1-1 to A.1-3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700033031/downloads/19700033031.pdf)

CM-103 has its own [console layout data](../../vehicles/reference/apollo-console-layout-103.json). The two attitude-ball centres in the engineering drawing register it to the shared console structure. At a third point, the drawn DSKY centre is 0.34 cm from its independently reconstructed centre. This measures how the drawings align at that point. The drawing title block says “DO NOT SCALE PRINT”, so the locations, widths and depths reconstructed from the panel view remain estimates. The dimensions of the fixed structure come from the [structural survey](../../vehicles/main-console.md). [North American Aviation, V36-976020 revision B, sheet 1](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/V36-976020_Design_Layout_MDC_CM-103.pdf)

The arrangement includes the central panel’s antenna controls and indication, the antenna scan-limit warning, the four service-engine injector-valve indicators and the right-hand electrical controls. Instrument faces and pointers are static. The DSKY has nineteen keys. Its PRO key and annunciator arrangement are visible in the CM-103 drawing. The detailed front, key sections and glazing use the [earlier instrument reconstruction](../../vehicles/dsky-front.md) as an assumed configuration. Its flown serial number has not been established here. [North American Aviation, sheet 1](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/V36-976020_Design_Layout_MDC_CM-103.pdf)

## What is simplified or missing

- The window and hatch outlines come from a survey of Apollo 11. The glass is shown clear for inspection. The Apollo 8 crew reported fogging of the hatch and both side windows during flight. [Mission report, §12.2 and fig. 12-2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700033031/downloads/19700033031.pdf)
- Couch dimensions, camera eye positions, packed parachutes, optics fittings, cabinet depths and cabin light levels are estimated. The exhibit holds the couches in the launch position. The wiring, loose stowage and individual strut lockout mechanisms are incomplete.
- Switches share simplified bodies, several control groups have abbreviated legends, and the side panels and individual guard shapes are incomplete. The entry monitor has a simplified display. The real ship carried a scroll calibrated for lunar return and a lunar-flight guidance program. [Mission report, p. A-3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700033031/downloads/19700033031.pdf)
- The antenna deployment shows the installation and sweep. Reflector weave, pointing servos and tracking are simplified. Service-module reinforcement, thicker tension ties, internal valve changes and the flight qualification instrumentation are omitted. [Mission report, pp. A-1 to A-4](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700033031/downloads/19700033031.pdf)
