# CSM-107 and LM-5 aboard SA-506

The launch vehicle evaluation identifies the stages as S-IC-6, S-II-6 and S-IVB-6N, followed by IU-6 and SLA-14. At first-stage ignition the vehicle mass was 2,941,221 kg. The rollout photograph shows the black-and-white roll pattern and the covered spacecraft above the tapered adapter. The stack uses the shared stage stations, five F-1 engines, five second-stage J-2s and the restartable J-2 on the third stage. The 363-foot nominal launch height is measured to the escape tower tip. The escape system and adapter retained the Apollo 10 configuration. [AS-506 Flight Evaluation Report, §2](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS506-Apollo11.pdf) · [Rollout photograph 69-HC-620](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/images11.html#69HC620) · [Saturn V reconstruction](../../vehicles/saturn-v.md)

The flight report's reconstructed masses at transposition and docking are 63,473.0 lb for Columbia and 33,294.5 lb for Eagle. The ascent stage alone weighed 5,738.0 lb at final docking. Columbia’s modeled length from the service-engine exit to the docking plane is 404.25 inches, derived from shared Block II stations. The probe projects beyond that plane. [Mission Report, appendix A, table A-I](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A11_MissionReport.pdf) · [Columbia’s hardware record](../../vehicles/csm-107.md) · [Eagle’s hardware record](../../vehicles/lm-5.md)

The Motors tab uses the nominal ratings published for this flight. The Apollo 11 press kit gives about 1,530,771 lbf per F-1 at liftoff and 224,003 to 231,000 lbf per second-stage J-2 over the flight. The third-stage rating runs from 178,161 to 203,779 lbf. The tab displays the upper rating for each J-2. The same kit gives the spacecraft propulsion ratings and distinguishes Eagle’s throttleable descent engine from its fixed-thrust ascent engine. [Apollo 11 Press Kit, pp. 88, 93, 106, 109 and 114–115](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A11_PressKit.pdf)

### On the mobile launcher

The tower stood north of SA-506. As seen from above, No. 1 F-1 was northeast of the vehicle axis and No. 2 northwest. The four outboard engines and their fins lay on the diagonals of the square exhaust opening. Between them, four points on the lower thrust ring bore on the launcher. The exhibit places the bearing soles 19.44 ft above the F-1 exits, at the lower edge of the reconstructed stage. The four contact points and engine indexing follow the Saturn V reference plan. The local lug shapes, their dimensions and their precise contact elevation are estimates. The black roll pattern continues from the S-IC across the interstage onto the S-II. SA-506 internal tank structure and equipment-bay arrangements use adjacent Saturn V drawings. Roll markings and blanket colors use photographic estimates.
