# Apollo 10 (AS-505)

On 18 May 1969 Thomas Stafford, John Young and Eugene Cernan left Pad 39B at Kennedy Space Center. Young turned **Charlie Brown** (CSM-106) around to dock with **Snoopy** (LM-4) and draw it out of the Saturn V adapter. Three days later the service engine slowed the joined spacecraft into lunar orbit. [NASA, Apollo 10 Mission Report, sections 1–3 and table 3-I](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/A10_MissionReport.pdf)

Stafford and Cernan took Snoopy down to a radar-measured 47,400 feet above the lunar surface. After testing the radar over the approach to a candidate landing site, they left the descent stage in orbit and brought the ascent stage back to Young. With all three aboard Charlie Brown, the crew released the ascent stage. Ground controllers fired its engine to depletion. The command module splashed down in the Pacific on 26 May, 192 hours, 3 minutes and 23 seconds after range zero. [Mission Report, table 3-I and figure 6-6](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/A10_MissionReport.pdf)

At Pad 39B the wind was 19 knots from 142° true, measured 60 feet above ground. Cumulus covered four tenths of the sky at 2,200 feet, with altocumulus at 11,000 feet and a complete cirrus layer above. The published account gives no cirrus base. [NASA SP-4029, p. 73](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/sp-4029.pdf)

The [spacecraft and launch vehicle](spacecraft.md) occupy two Gallery exhibits. On the launch stack, open the adapter to inspect the folded lunar module, or select the command module to see the couches, panel and docking probe. On the separate Snoopy exhibit, open the cutaway to inspect its cabin and batteries. Move the assembly slider to leave the legs and descent engine behind and follow the ascent stage that returned to Charlie Brown. The exhibit uses inspection movements. The mission timeline uses the elapsed times in table 3-I of the flight report. [NASA mission overview](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/apollo-10/)

## Launch site

At [Pad 39B](../../../places/cape-canaveral/lc-39.md), select the white room to see the access arm at the command module. The mobile launcher is ML-3. At liftoff the computed Sun was at azimuth 141.3° and elevation 78.7°. The Moon was at azimuth 82.0° and elevation 58.8°.
