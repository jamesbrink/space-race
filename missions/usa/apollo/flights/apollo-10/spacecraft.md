# CSM-106, LM-4 and SA-505

Young had two ways to measure the distance to Snoopy. The LM rendezvous radar tracked the transponder in Charlie Brown, and the new VHF ranging system let the CSM measure the round trip of its own signal. Young could read the range on the entry monitor. The main console follows the CM-106 design layout. The lighter side hatch has sixteen recessed pockets traced from the surviving flight door. [Charlie Brown’s hatch and console](../../vehicles/csm-106.md) · [Mission Report, appendix A.1](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/A10_MissionReport.pdf)

In the launch stack, the service engine sits over the folded lunar module. To transfer between spacecraft, the crew first had to reverse that arrangement. Young separated the CSM, turned it, docked the probe with Snoopy’s drogue, then drew Snoopy out of the adapter. The CSM weighed 63,560 lb before transposition. Snoopy weighed 30,735 lb at liftoff and 31,166 lb at separation in lunar orbit with the two crewmen aboard. The ascent stage alone weighed 8,273 lb just after staging. [Mission Report, tables 3-I and A.4-I](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/A10_MissionReport.pdf)

In the deployed exhibit, the four landing legs carry contact probes. The forward porch has handrails, and each gear deployment mechanism has a protective shield. Opening the descent stage exposes the four batteries split across two forward quadrants. After staging, the docking socket and the rendezvous radar travel with the crew. When the two spacecraft were about 20 feet apart, the LM crew aligned Snoopy and held attitude. Charlie Brown made the final approach. [Snoopy’s construction and photographs](../../vehicles/lm-4.md) · [Mission Report, §9.9.7](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/A10_MissionReport.pdf)

The crew televised the first docking from the command module. A field-sequential colour camera sent the pictures, while the crew aimed it using a monochrome monitor with a 3 × 2¼ in picture tube. A separate black-and-white camera also flew in Charlie Brown. The television camera carried aboard Spider on Apollo 9 was deleted from Snoopy. [Mission Report, §A.1.9](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/A10_MissionReport.pdf)

The launch stack is 363 ft to the escape tower. The common Saturn V reconstruction supplies the stage shells, interiors, engines and instrument unit. The SA-505 mission configuration called for early cutoff of the S-II centre engine. The specifications give the nominal stage ratings from the Apollo 10 press kit separately from the vehicle mass at ground ignition. ## Reconstruction limits

The Block II crew compartment and docking hardware use shared structural stations. These give 404.25 in from the service-engine exit to the mating plane. Probe support fittings, its capture-hook motion, couch eye points, cabin lighting and the prelaunch boarding interval are estimated. The gallery slider separates the hardware for inspection. Structural dimensions inside SA-505 are assumed from adjacent Saturn V vehicles. Console instruments are static. The portable television cameras and monitor, internal radio and power-transfer wiring, and the individual thermal-blanket layers are omitted. The hardware notes record the remaining hatch, panel and LM envelope uncertainties.
