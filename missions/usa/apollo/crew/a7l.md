# Apollo 7 intravehicular pressure suits

Schirra, Eisele and Cunningham wore the A7L in its Earth-orbital configuration. The exhibit follows the white pressure garments, communications carriers, clear helmet bubbles, neck and wrist disconnects, hose connections, gloves and boots visible in Apollo 7 crew photographs. Each figure uses the shared articulated walking skeleton. The cabin figures fit the left, centre and right unitized couches, respectively. Their restraints follow the occupied garment surfaces.

## Sources

- [Schirra’s flown pressure garment, Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/pressure-suit-a7-l-schirra-apollo-7-flown/nasm_A19730228000).
- [Apollo 7 Press Kit, NASA, October 1968](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A07_PressKit.pdf). Crew equipment and crew biographies.

## Simplifications

The fits, helmet curvature, small connectors, boot lacing and seated poses are photographic estimates. Faces use shared human geometry. Soft-goods folds, individual stitching, portable ventilators and detailed insignia are omitted. The seated figures depict the launch configuration throughout the exhibit.

## Apollo 16 launch garments

Young and Duke wore the A7LB-EV with a side-entry closure and the flexible waist needed to sit on the rover. Mattingly wore the rear-entry A7LB-CMP, which he later used with an umbilical to retrieve film from Casper. The white launch garments, clear pressure helmets, dark gloves and Young’s red identification bands follow the suit-up photographs from April 16. The worn heights are reconstructed from the individual statures in the flight press kit. Young was 69 inches tall, Mattingly 70 and Duke 71½. The helmet and boot add to each figure’s unsuited height. [Apollo 16 Press Kit, pp. 126, 147, 150 and 152](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Apollo_16.pdf) · [NASA launch-day photographs, including 72-HC-305 and KSC-72PC-0170](https://www.nasa.gov/history/apollo-16-launches-to-the-moon/)

The flight emblem on the left breast follows Duke’s garment in the White Room photograph. The texture is a reduced copy of NASA’s mission insignia, S71-56246. Its diameter of about 8.4 cm and its placement are estimates. The figures share the same facial geometry. Stitching, the portable ventilators used on the way to the pad and the small sleeve insignia are omitted. [NASA S71-56246](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/apollo_16_launch_2_patch_s71-56246.jpg)

## Apollo 17 launch garments

Cernan and Schmitt wore the side-entry A7LB-EV with its six chest connectors and flexible waist. Evans wore the rear-entry A7LB-CMP. He later used it to retrieve the lunar sounder and camera film outside America. The three standing figures are scaled to their respective unsuited heights of 72, 71½ and 69 inches in commander, command module pilot and lunar module pilot order. The fittings, helmet and boots add to those heights. [Apollo 17 Press Kit, p. 80 and crew biographies, pp. 101, 103 and 105](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Apollo_17.pdf)

The December 6 suit-up photograph shows Cernan’s red bands, black launch gloves, the mission emblem on his left breast and the NASA insignia on his right shoulder. The White Room photograph shows his US flag on the left sleeve. Both shoulder markings follow the upper-arm joints. The circular flight patch uses NASA’s image of the Apollo and eagle insignia. [NASA KSC-72PC-613, suit-up](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/apollo_17_launch_5_as17-468-72pc-613-12.6.72.jpg) · [NASA KSC-72PC-627, White Room](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/apollo_17_launch_7_as17-479b-72pc-627-12.6.72.jpg) · [NASA S72-49079, Apollo 17 insignia](https://images.nasa.gov/details/s72-49079)

The chest emblem is 8.4 cm across in the model. The flag measures 10 × 6.1 cm and the NASA disc 7.6 cm. All three sizes and their positions are estimated from the photographs. The modeled CMP glove and its wrist disconnect are fitted together about the wrist. Hand size is estimated from stature, and individual cuff measurements are unavailable. The figures share facial geometry. Embroidery, individual suit folds, the walkout ventilators and their hoses are omitted.
