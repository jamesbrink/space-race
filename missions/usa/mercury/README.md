# Project Mercury (USA, 1958–1963)

NASA's first human spaceflight programme: put a man in orbit, observe how he functions there, and
bring him and the spacecraft back. Six crewed flights, two suborbital on the Redstone and four
orbital on the Atlas D, all in the one-man capsule McDonnell Aircraft built in St. Louis.

## The spacecraft, in general

A blunt cone 74.5 inches across the heat shield, flown blunt end first on entry. From the bottom:
retropack, heat shield, conical afterbody around the pressure cabin, a cylindrical recovery
compartment holding the parachutes, and an antenna canister. A launch escape tower rides on top
until the booster has done its work.

The capsule changed from flight to flight, which is why each flight here has its own model:

| | MR-3 (No. 7) | MR-4 (No. 11) | Orbital flights |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| View out | two 10 in portholes, periscope | trapezoidal overhead window | window (periscope deleted on MA-9) |
| Side hatch | mechanical latches (mechanism 69 lb) | explosive, 23 lb | explosive |
| Heat shield | beryllium heat sink | beryllium heat sink | ablative fibreglass and resin |
| Suit gloves | zipped to the sleeves | ring lock with wrist bearing | ring lock |

All of them carried a landing bag, and all had beryllium shingles on the recovery compartment.
