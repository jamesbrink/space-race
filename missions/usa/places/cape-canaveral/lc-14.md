# Launch Complex 14, Cape Canaveral

Where the four orbital Mercury flights left from: Friendship 7 (20 February 1962), Aurora 7 (24 May 1962), Sigma 7
(3 October 1962) and Faith 7 (15 May 1963). Built as it stood on 20 February 1962, which is how it stood for Aurora 7
too as far as anything read says ("What stood when").

How it is put together (the pattern Complex 5 set, and the one to follow for the next pad):

| What | Where | Is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The pad on the Earth | `lc-14.ts` | the `SiteDef`: where the frame's origin is, which way its +X bears, the grade, the zone |
| The frame, the key dimensions, the materials | `lc14/site.py` | feet, as the sources give them; every other module imports from it |
| The pieces | `lc14/*.py` | one module a thing; none knows a flight; each says when what it builds stood (`sitelib.stood`) |
| What goes in which model | `lc_14.py` | `build(date)`: the pad's own hardware, a flight's `pad.glb`. `build_place(date)`: the rest, the place's GLB |
| The place's dated states | `lc14/model/<date>.py` | `1962-02.py` builds `public/models/usa/places/cape-canaveral/lc14/1962-02.glb` (`pnpm model lc-14`) |
| A flight's choice | `mercury/flights/<flight>/model/pad.py` | names its day and calls `lc_14.build` (`pnpm model ma-6`) |
| The parts as data | `lc-14.parts.ts` | the pad's parts, their moves, functions and actions; a flight gives its morning (`PadDay`) |
| A flight's morning | `mercury/flights/<flight>/pad.ts` | where its count falls on the slider, its words, where its pilot stands |
| The ground round it | `lc-14.ground.ts` | the clearing, the roads, where nothing grows, where the land is let down (`ground.ts` `PadGround`) |

Sources are named in full in [docs/sources.md](../../../../docs/sources.md), "Launch Complex 14". The two that carry
most: **HAER** is HAER No. FL-8-7, written data (pages as printed); **Results** is *Results of the First United
States Manned Orbital Space Flight* (NASA MSC, 1962). **OSM** is OpenStreetMap, which maps what survives: every
place taken from it is derived. Photographs are scaled in feet off the Atlas (10 ft across its tank, 95 ft 4 in
tall) and are estimated; each is named where it is used.

## The site's frame

The origin is **the vehicle's axis at the stand's deck**. +Z is up (the viewer's +Y).

| | Bears | Confidence | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| +X | **105° true**: the vehicle's pitch axis, and the transfer table's travel | documented | "the launch pad is oriented so that the pitch axis of the launch vehicle is alined on an azimuth of 105 degrees" (Results, p. 46) |
| +Y (the viewer's −Z) | 015°: along the stand and ramp, the way the service structure rolls off; the ramp runs away along −Y | derived | OSM ways 195727091 (stand) and 1264624135 (ramp): 014.5° and 194.5°, square to the pitch axis within half a degree |

Until November 2026 the frame's +X was "the side the hatch faced", taken as 60°. The frame is now the documented
line, and the vehicle stands turned in it (`HATCH_TURN`, below).

### Where the vehicle stood

OSM's outline of the stand (way 195727091) is what survives: 53 by 68 ft. The stand was 60 by 78 ft, and its
"northern portion ... was razed in December 1976" (HAER, pp. 20 and 21). So the middle of the ruin is not where
the launcher stood.

| Step | Figure | Confidence | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The stand's south edge, where the ramp comes on | 34 ft south of the ruin's middle | derived | OSM ways 195727091 and 1264624135 |
| Its north edge | 78 ft north of that: 44 ft north of the ruin's middle | derived | HAER, p. 20: the stand "approximately 60' wide x 78' long". With the ramp's foot (OSM) that makes 430 ft against HAER's 432 |
| The vehicle's axis | **14 ft north of the ruin's middle** (`VEHICLE_NORTH`): 48 ft from the stand's south edge and 30 ft from its north | estimated, ±6 ft | NASA GPN-2000-000609 and KSC-63PC-0034 (7 May 1963, from the west-south-west): the stand's north end, the vehicle and the tower's foot fall where the model puts them to a few feet; the flume leaves north on the vehicle's line (OSM way 1264624137) |

`lc-14.ts` gives the origin's latitude and longitude from that (28.491152° N, 80.546887° W: derived). Everything
OSM places is given in the modules as OSM has it, from the ruin's middle, and moved by `site.osm()`.

### Which way the hatch faced

**On 240° true (±10°): `HATCH_TURN` = 225° round from +X toward +Y.** The capsule's window then faces 285°,
toward the blockhouse, over the Atlas's long pod on the pitch axis.

| Step | Confidence | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The two booster engines and their pods lie on the pitch axis | documented | NASA SP-45, Figures 5-4 and 5-10 (vehicles/atlas-lv3b.md, "Which way round") |
| The window is over the long pod; the hatch is 45° round from it | estimated | photographs of MA-6 on the stand (the same) |
| So the hatch is on 60° or on 240° | derived | |
| It is 240° | estimated | NASA S62-00490: the open hatch seen through the bascule's net with the surf line behind the capsule, so it faces inland. NASA KSC-LOD-62-475 (22 January 1962, from the deck's west-south-west) and KSC-62C-0128: the name and the window toward the camera, the bascule meeting the capsule at its right. NASA GPN-2000-000998 (MA-9, from the ramp): the hatch 37° left of centre. A capsule turned heads-up after a yaw turnaround has the pilot's head uprange, 285°, before the roll programme |

The Atlas rolled to its heading after liftoff (Results, p. 46), so nothing on the stand was turned for a flight:
every Mercury-Atlas stood the same way.

## The grade

The deck stands **22 ft over the paving at its foot**: the ramp "gradually increased to a height of 22'" (HAER,
p. 20: documented; the Cape Canaveral Space Force Museum has 22 ft, the NHL nomination "a 20-foot ramp"). The land
is drawn 12 cm under that paving, as under the pads at Complex 5 (rendering.md), so `lc-14.ts` `grade` is
−(22 ft + 0.12 m) = −6.826 m (derived). In the scripts `APRON` is the paving (−22 ft) and `GROUND` the land.

## What the model gets wrong or leaves out

- **The service structure's form is a reading of photographs.** Its height, its fourteen decks, its inverted U and
  how it left are published; its plan, its taper, the levels' heights, the stairs' and the lift's places are not.
  Whether "14 movable decks" means seven levels of two leaves, as built here, is a reading.
- **The transfer table and its pit are estimated whole**: a table there was; its size, the pit's depth and whether
  the tracks were in a pit at all are not in anything read. The steps into the pit are ours.
- **One tower stands where the record names two.** The Results' Figure 4-5 labels an umbilical tower and an egress
  tower; the photographs of 1962 and 1963 show one tapered tower carrying both the umbilical and the bascule, and
  that is what is built. Whether the old mast survived inside it is not known.
- **The umbilical's boom swings clear, with its hose.** How the capsule's umbilical was drawn away is not in
  anything read; the hose does not fall.
- **No vehicle umbilicals, no propellant lines.** The booster's own umbilicals, the liquid oxygen and fuel lines
  from the storage areas to the stand (954 ft and 418 ft by HAER, p. 25), the helium and nitrogen lines and the
  water mains are not built. The stand's pipes are a gallery of three lines and a deluge ring, estimated.
- **The launcher is a sketch**: two arms, four posts, a platform. It did turn on its side to take the vehicle from
  its trailer (Results, p. 50); that is not built.
- **Every room is shut** (above). The blockhouse's control room, its tunnels to the stand and the rooms under the
  ramp are not built.
- **The dome is cut square on the west** and its curve is by eye.
- **Not built, though they stood**: the perimeter fence and its gates (not placed by anything read); the cherry
  picker "stationed behind the blockhouse" as a spare for the bascule (Results, p. 48: Complex 5's is not copied
  here without a photograph of where it stood); the rescue and firefighting vehicles of Figure 4-9; the tube-bank
  trailers of the aerials of May 1963; the camera mounts and the theodolite base (OSM node 11751537150, 172 ft
  south-west of the stand: undated); the Water Demineralization Station and its tank at the entrance; the two fuel
  oil tanks; the Azusa and command antennas; the people.
- **The floodlights' places on the paving, the fire nozzles' places and the signal's are read off one aerial.**
- **The ramp's rise is a reading of one sentence** (above).
- **From another pad the complex is still its stand-in.** `lc_14.build_place(date, pad=True)` builds the complex
  whole for that; wiring it in as Complex 5's is (`scenery.ts`, a `Complex`'s `own`) waits on what Freedom 7's site
  can hold as a phone (rendering.md, "Memory").
- **The ground round it** is the Cape's scrub with a round clearing: the fences, the drains OSM maps, the firebreaks
  and the exact edge of the mown ground are not drawn.
