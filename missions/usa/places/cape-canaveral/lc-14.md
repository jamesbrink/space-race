# Launch Complex 14, Cape Canaveral

Where the four orbital Mercury flights left from: Friendship 7 (20 February 1962), Aurora 7 (24 May 1962), Sigma 7
(3 October 1962) and Faith 7 (15 May 1963). The pad's own model is not built yet: the complex is a stand-in in the
Cape's scenery (`scenery.ts`, seen from the other pads), and its `SiteDef` (`lc-14.ts`) gives the sky, the grade and
the frame a flight from it stands in. The research behind this record, with every figure's source, is issue #25
(the notes of 14 November 2026); the figures used here are quoted from it with their pages.

## The site's frame

The origin is the middle of the launch stand, on the deck the Atlas's launcher stood on (OpenStreetMap way 195727091,
the stand's surviving footing: 28.491115° N, 80.546898° W). +Y is up. **+X is the side the capsule's hatch faced: 60°
true, estimated.** How far the documents go:

The choice of 60° rested on a photograph (not named in the record) read as having the service structure rolled back
south along the ramp, at the frame's left, with the hatch toward the camera: so a camera east of the stand, and the
hatch on its side. The structure did not park there. It rolled north off the stand onto a rail-mounted transfer table,
which carried it about 300 ft east to its parking area (HAER FL-8-7, p. 22; the NHL nomination, Item 7, p. 9:
documented); the table's two tracks run on 105°, 44 ft apart, their centre line about 65 ft north of the stand's
middle, from about 50 ft west of it (OpenStreetMap ways 1264975420 and 1264975421: derived). Parked, it stood about
290 ft east and 13 ft south of the stand's middle (derived: 300 ft along 105° from where the stand's axis meets the
tracks, 65 ft north of the stand's middle). Read again
with that, a frame with the parked structure (on 093° from the stand) at its left was taken from somewhere between
west and east of the stand by way of north (a camera on a bearing from 273° round through 360° to 093°); the hatch
toward that camera is 60° if it stood between north-north-west and east (330° to 093°), 240° if between west and
north-north-west (273° to 330°). Without the photograph the record cannot tell which.

Nothing else documented places the hatch. The blockhouse stands about 750 ft west (HAER, p. 24; OpenStreetMap's
outline, 822 ft on 286°: derived), which is on the pitch line within a degree and says nothing of the hatch's side.
The emergency egress tower stood beside the umbilical mast on the stand, its platform lowered to the hatch's door
(*Results*, p. 48, Figures 4-5 and 4-8): it would settle it, but neither is placed or dimensioned in anything read.
**So 60° is kept, and 240° is as likely.** What would settle it: that photograph, found and read again; a photograph
of Glenn going aboard that shows the parked structure or the blockhouse; or the Pan Am as-built drawings of 1964
(01-01684-040, -041, -116 to -118), held at KSC and not cleared for release (HAER, pp. 17, 31 to 33).

## The grade

The deck stands **22 ft over the ground** (`lc-14.ts` `grade`, −6.706 m): the ramp "gradually increased to a height of
22' over the northern six bays" (HAER FL-8-7, p. 20: documented), and the stand is the bay north of them, its deck at
the ramp's top (derived). The NHL nomination rounds it to "a 20-foot ramp"; the Cape Canaveral Space Force Museum gives
22 ft. The ground there is taken as about 2 m above mean sea level, as at Complex 5, so the deck is about 8.7 m above
it, which `height` (−20 m on the ellipsoid) agrees with to a metre.

## What stood when

Nothing physical is found to have changed at the complex between 20 February 1962 and 15 May 1963 (issue #25: HAER and
the NHL nomination date the Mercury modifications 1959 to 1960, and the next, for Atlas-Agena, 1963 to 1965, after
Faith 7). That work replaced the 84 ft 6 in umbilical mast with a 101 ft tower and took down the egress tower (HAER,
pp. 27 and 28); it was to begin on 1 January 1964 and take ten months (NASA SP-4002, entry of 27 April 1963), and
"Construction of Gemini-Agena facilities at complex 14 was completed" on 24 June 1964 (SP-4002, Part II(B)). Its real
start is not found. Seen from the other pads the complex is drawn in its Mercury state until 1 January 1964
(estimated) and with the 101 ft tower from 24 June 1964 (documented): `scenery.ts`, `COMPLEXES`. What stands round
Complex 14 on each Mercury flight's day is the Cape's, dated the same way (lc-5.md, "After 1961"): Complex 5 among
it, 6.2 km to the south-south-west.

## What a Launch site here still needs

- **The complex's model**, as Complex 5's is: a package `lc14/` beside this file, with `model/<date>.py` for its states
  (one state serves all four flights, so far as anything read says), its `.parts.ts`, and each flight's `flight.ts`
  given a model with `backdrop: 'ground'` and `surroundings`. What issue #25 documents for it: the stand (60 by 78 ft),
  the ramp (24 ft wide, 22 ft high over its northern six bays, the southern 170 ft on fill), the umbilical mast (84 ft
  6 in above the stand, ten levels), the service structure (154 ft 8 in by HAER, 145 ft by the NHL nomination; 14
  movable decks; an inverted U; the white room in its upper two decks; a passenger lift), the transfer table and its
  tracks, the parking area, the blockhouse (a twelve-sided dome, 10 ft 6 in thick at the base under 40 ft of sand),
  the Launch Pad Building under the ramp, the PTU enclosure, the POL building, the RP-1 and LOX/GN2 areas, the
  flume and skimming basin, the egress tower (not dimensioned: from Figure 4-8 and photographs), the cherry picker
  behind the blockhouse, the Ready Building and sentry house.
- **Its moves**: the structure north off the stand and east on the table (MA-7: first motion T−47, stowed T−33; MA-8:
  removal started T−64), the white room's doors, roof and floor opening first; the egress tower's platform held
  vertical through the count.
- **The service structure's height**: HAER's 154 ft 8 in against the NHL nomination's 145 ft, both with 14 decks.
- **The bearing**: 60° or 240° (above).
