# Launch Complex 39 in 1968–70

Apollo 8 and 9 stood at Pad A on mobile launchers 1 and 2. Apollo 10 stood at Pad B on mobile launcher 3. Apollo 11 returned to Pad A on mobile launcher 1 on July 16, 1969, and Apollo 12 flew from Pad A on mobile launcher 2 that November. Mobile launcher 3 returned to service at Pad A for Apollo 13 in April 1970. The three launchers share the base and umbilical tower reconstruction. The tower is north of the launch axis. Pad A's open-air electrical substation stands west of the slope. Pad B housed its substation under the slope and had its water valve pit on the east side. Pad B's hardstand stood five feet higher above sea level. ## Reconstruction limits

The exhibit covers the launch hardstand, approach, launcher and principal storage spheres. The outer crawlerway network, mobile service structure in its remote parking area, VAB, underground rooms and most peripheral equipment are omitted. Launcher details common to the three units are assumed. Individual cable routes, internal machinery and foundations are simplified. Apollo 8, 9 and 10 use the Cape’s default sky. Apollo 11, 12 and 13 use their own launch-day weather.

The crew boards from the approach ramp, launcher stairs and tower elevator, then crosses the level 320 arm. The two high-speed tower elevators were rated at 600 ft/min between level A and level 340. One car is operable here. Stair geometry, outboard hinge positions and the final 90° sweep are estimated. Shared standing A7L figures represent the IV and EV launch garments. Body proportions, faces, small connectors and starting positions are illustrative. The final stair rise is reconstructed against the spacecraft hatch.

Five inflight arms release at first movement. Retraction of the S-II aft access arm is placed before ingress, and the two S-IC arms follow the individual countdowns. The Apollo 8 and 10 launch-vehicle evaluations give intertank withdrawal to safe angle and the interval to the next arm. The model estimates the angles and represents full clearance as one stroke. Apollo 9's intertank arm clears the vehicle before launch and finishes latching against the tower at T+1.77 s. Its flight manual gives the S-IC forward withdrawal at T−20 s, while its press kit puts the fourth arm release at about T−6 s. The exhibit follows the manual for this command. The pressure hatch and its protective cover open on separate hinges for boarding. Their angles and travel durations use the shared Block II reconstruction. The white-room recess on the hinge side clears both leaves throughout their swing. Its width is estimated. Flight-specific PAO broadcasts put the crew arm at 12° standby before its final withdrawal at T−5 minutes. The broadcasts for Apollo 8–10 bound the first retraction without timing its start. ## Apollo 11 at Pad A, July 16, 1969

Launch Control used EDT. The spacecraft test conductor gave the crew-arm park command at T−50 minutes and motion was reported two seconds later. The 12° standby arrival at T−49:54 is estimated from the nearby hatch-gearbox exchange. The final retraction began at T−5:00. Full retraction was first reported at T−4:48 and confirmed at T−4:33. The access arm’s modelled terminal angle is approximate. [Launch-day voice record, crew arm park](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap11fj/01launch.html#t-0004958) · [Full retraction](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap11fj/01launch.html#t-0000448)

The S-IC intertank arm started at T−30 s. Its umbilical carrier took 5.06 s to withdraw. The entire arm reached its safe angle after 9.9 s (T−20.1), about 3.9 s before the forward arm command at T−16.2. The five inflight arms released on first motion. The launch-vehicle evaluation gives physical first motion and pneumatic hold-down release at T+0.3 s. The spacecraft mission report records lift-off at T+0.6 s. The nominal eight-second forward-arm sweep and the five inflight sweep durations come from the Saturn V manual. [AS-506 Flight Evaluation Report, §§3.6.1–2 and table 4-1](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS506-Apollo11.pdf) · [Apollo 11 Mission Report, table 3-I](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a11/A11_MissionReport.pdf)

The July reconstruction places the service-arm steelwork outside the stage skins and leaves out the flexible umbilicals at the ends of arms 1–8. Arm 9’s landing sits about 12 cm below the hatch sill. Its rigid front edge ends about 29 cm outboard of the closed hatch’s outermost point. The landing and the space for the open leaves are estimates based on the modelled hatch. The soft White Room interface is omitted. The exported meshes are checked against the stationary stack as the hatches and arms move. The collision boxes have a separate clearance check because their rounded edges can extend past the drawn surfaces.

The dated launcher has four cast hold-down arms at Saturn positions I–IV, between the first stage’s engine fairings. The castings were about 6 × 9 ft at the base and 10 ft tall. The support girders, bearing shoes, links and blast hoods follow the manual’s plan and *Moonport*’s side elevation. The shelf meets the vehicle model’s lower ring 19.44 ft above the engine exits. The drawing gives vehicle station 113.31 at the controlled-release detail. The common reference plane for the shelf and lower ring, girder sections, hinge positions and sweep angles are reconstruction estimates. The upper link represents the collapsing linkage; individual lower links and pneumatic actuators are omitted. The crane is parked crosswise, with its hook clear of the stack. Its east-facing direction is estimated. [Apollo 11 Press Kit, pp. 164–165](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/apollo50th/pdf/A11_PressKit.pdf) · [SA-504 manual, fig. 8-7](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/Saturn_V_Flight_Manual_SA_504.pdf) · [NASA, *Moonport*, ch. 13, hold-down arm schematic](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Schematic_of_hold-down_arm.gif) · [*Saturn V Step-by-Step*, T−17 h](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/afj/pdf/saturn-V-step-by-step.pdf)

Launch film shows No. 1’s protective hood stayed open, exposing the adjustable head and upper link to blast damage. The other three hoods appeared to close late. The evaluation identifies arm No. 1, while the manual labels the four positions on the plan. Their correspondence is assumed here, with No. 1 at position I, east. The exhibit gives all links half a second to swing clear after release, then closes the three working hoods from T+1.3 to T+2.3 s. Those durations are estimated. All three tail-service masts retracted normally and their hoods closed. Masts 1-2, 3-2 and 3-4 follow the arrangement in figure 8-7. At first motion each carrier draws back while its counterbalanced boom rises into the mast. The exhibit gives the boom 1.1 s to stand upright and the visor a further 0.6 s to close. Mast dimensions, exact azimuths and travel times are estimated from the drawing. Unsecured covers on the S-II forward arm and the restraints that kept service-arm control-console doors closed on this flight are omitted. [AS-506 Flight Evaluation Report, §§3.6.1, 3.6.3](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS506-Apollo11.pdf)

## Apollo 12 at Pad A, November 14, 1969

Mobile Launcher 2 carried SA-507. The SA-507 manual shows the four hold-downs at positions I–IV around the engine opening, three tail service masts and nine service arms. The exhibit reuses the articulated castings, carrier geometry, tower, approach stairs, paired hatch recess and parked crane from July as estimates for this launcher. The flight manual gives the same base, crane reach and 320 ft boarding level. The November reconstruction adds the industrial-water quench valve to the S-IC forward arm. Its presence on arm 2 is documented; the envelope, adjacent pipework, spray outlets, brackets and their placement on the truss are estimates. [NASA, Apollo 12 mission details](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/apollo/apollo-12-the-pinpoint-mission/) · [SA-507 manual, §VIII, figs. 8-6–8](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pdf/a12_sa507-flightmanual.pdf) · [AS-507 flight evaluation, §3.6.1](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pdf/a12-saturn-v-fer.pdf)

Launch Control reported the crew arm in its 12° standby position during the T−29:54 update. It observed motion away from standby at T−5:00. The unreported arrival at full retraction follows the flight manual’s nominal T−4:50 lock time. The two preflight S-IC arm strokes use the same manual’s planned retract-and-lock intervals, T−30 to −17 s and T−16.2 to −11 s. The flight evaluation confirms that the arms reached safe angles within specification. [Kennedy Launch Control broadcast](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/01launch_to_earth_orbit.html) · [SA-507 manual, fig. 8-20](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pdf/a12_sa507-flightmanual.pdf) · [AS-507 flight evaluation, §3.6.1](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pdf/a12-saturn-v-fer.pdf)

SA-507 first moved at T+0.25 s. The four hold-downs released within eight milliseconds of each other, and the three tail masts retracted normally. All four hold-down protective hoods close in the exhibit according to their nominal design; individual link, carrier, boom and hood travel times are estimates. A quench valve on arm 2 failed to open after liftoff. Favorable wind and water from higher arms kept the arm from apparent damage. Film also showed that the S-II forward umbilical cover failed to secure. During the countdown, wind pressed the Q-ball cover cable against the primary damper. The pad team twice freed the cable at the crane. The reconstruction omits the flexible umbilical covers, damper and cable, water spray and postlaunch damage. [AS-507 flight evaluation, table 2-2 and §§3.6.1–3](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pdf/a12-saturn-v-fer.pdf)

## Apollo 13 at Pad A, April 11, 1970

SA-508’s first stage was erected on mobile launcher 3 on June 18, 1969. The complete vehicle and launcher reached Pad A on December 15. ML-3 had stood on Pad B with Apollo 10. In April the launcher stood above Pad A’s flame trench, with the open-air substation and water valve pit to the west. The deck, tower elevator, three tail masts, four hold-downs and nine arms follow the shared reconstruction. The final countdown was directed from firing room 1. [AS-508 Flight Evaluation Report, table 3-1 and §3.3](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS508-Apollo13.pdf) · [NASA photograph of SA-508 on Pad A after rollout](https://www.nasa.gov/history/50-years-ago-apollo-13-launch-date-reset/)

Arm 1 supplied oxygen through the intertank. The S-IC had two six-inch fill and drain lines, and the launcher withdrew the carrier with pneumatically operated parallel links. LOX loading was complete across all three stages by T−5 h 41 min, when replenishment began. Later the S-IC’s No. 2 vent valve stuck open. The launch team freed it with a gaseous-nitrogen purge and 13 close commands. The ground-side carrier is visible in the arm 1 inspection view. Its paired lines, coupling collars and linkage show how the hard fittings travel with the truss. [SA-508 Flight Manual, pp. 4-12 and 8-10, figs. 4-13 and 8-8](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/MSFC-MAN-508%20Saturn%20V%20Flight%20Manual%20SA-508%201-March-1970.pdf) · [AS-508 Flight Evaluation Report, §3.4.2](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS508-Apollo13.pdf)

Launch Control reported the white room moving away from Odyssey at T−43 minutes and about ten feet away before T−42:31. Arm 9 stood at 12° until about T−5 minutes, when the final withdrawal was called. Its arrival at the fully retracted position uses the manual’s nominal T−4:50. During the final minute the announcer forecast T−36 s for arm 1. The clock uses the manual’s retract-and-lock sequence, T−30 to −17 s for arm 1 and T−16.2 to −11 s for arm 2. The broadcast does not identify the instant of either stroke from observation. [Kennedy Launch Control, April 11 countdown](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap13fj/01launch_ascent.html) · [SA-508 Flight Manual, fig. 8-20](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/MSFC-MAN-508%20Saturn%20V%20Flight%20Manual%20SA-508%201-March-1970.pdf)

The four hold-downs released at T+0.3 s. The five inflight arms then swung clear. The DEE-6 digital event recorder failed just before liftoff. Control-panel indications confirmed every arm withdrew and latched. Television and panel indications showed the three tail masts retracted together and their hoods closed within four seconds of aft-plate separation. Film showed two covers on the S-II that failed to secure, at the arm 4 intermediate connection and the arm 5 forward connection. [AS-508 Flight Evaluation Report, table 2-2 and §§3.6.1–3](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS508-Apollo13.pdf)
