# Launch Complex 39 in 1968–72

Apollo 8 and 9 stood at Pad A on mobile launchers 1 and 2. Apollo 10 stood at Pad B on mobile launcher 3. Apollo 11 returned to Pad A on mobile launcher 1 on July 16, 1969, and Apollo 12 flew from Pad A on mobile launcher 2 that November. Mobile launcher 3 returned to service at Pad A for Apollo 13 in April 1970. Mobile launcher 2 carried Apollo 14 there in January 1971. ML-3 carried Apollo 15 there in July 1971, Apollo 16 in April 1972 and Apollo 17 that December. The three launchers share the base and umbilical tower reconstruction. The tower is north of the launch axis. Pad A's open-air electrical substation stands west of the slope. Pad B housed its substation under the slope and had its water valve pit on the east side. Pad B's hardstand stood five feet higher above sea level. ## Reconstruction limits

The exhibit covers the launch hardstand, approach, launcher and principal storage spheres. The outer crawlerway network, mobile service structure in its remote parking area, VAB, underground rooms and most peripheral equipment are omitted. Launcher details common to the three units are assumed. Individual cable routes, internal machinery and foundations are simplified. Apollo 8, 9 and 10 use the Cape’s default sky. Apollo 11 through 17 use their own launch-day weather.

The crew boards from the approach ramp, launcher stairs and tower elevator, then crosses the level 320 arm. The two high-speed tower elevators were rated at 600 ft/min between level A and level 340. One car is operable here. Stair geometry, outboard hinge positions and the final 90° sweep are estimated. Shared standing A7L figures represent the IV and EV launch garments. Body proportions, faces, small connectors and starting positions are illustrative. The final stair rise is reconstructed against the spacecraft hatch.

Five inflight arms release at first movement. Retraction of the S-II aft access arm is placed before ingress, and the two S-IC arms follow the individual countdowns. The Apollo 8 and 10 launch-vehicle evaluations give intertank withdrawal to safe angle and the interval to the next arm. The model estimates the angles and represents full clearance as one stroke. Apollo 9's intertank arm clears the vehicle before launch and finishes latching against the tower at T+1.77 s. Its flight manual gives the S-IC forward withdrawal at T−20 s, while its press kit puts the fourth arm release at about T−6 s. The exhibit follows the manual for this command. The pressure hatch and its protective cover open on separate hinges for boarding. Their angles and travel durations use the shared Block II reconstruction. The white-room recess on the hinge side clears both leaves throughout their swing. Its width is estimated. Flight-specific PAO broadcasts put the crew arm at 12° standby before its final withdrawal at T−5 minutes. The broadcasts for Apollo 8–10 bound the first retraction without timing its start. ## Apollo 11 at Pad A, July 16, 1969

Launch Control used EDT. The spacecraft test conductor gave the crew-arm park command at T−50 minutes and motion was reported two seconds later. The 12° standby arrival at T−49:54 is estimated from the nearby hatch-gearbox exchange. The final retraction began at T−5:00. Full retraction was first reported at T−4:48 and confirmed at T−4:33. The access arm’s modelled terminal angle is approximate. [Launch-day voice record, crew arm park](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap11fj/01launch.html#t-0004958) · [Full retraction](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap11fj/01launch.html#t-0000448)

The S-IC intertank arm started at T−30 s. Its umbilical carrier took 5.06 s to withdraw. The entire arm reached its safe angle after 9.9 s (T−20.1), about 3.9 s before the forward arm command at T−16.2. The five inflight arms released on first motion. The launch-vehicle evaluation gives physical first motion and pneumatic hold-down release at T+0.3 s. The spacecraft mission report records lift-off at T+0.6 s. The nominal eight-second forward-arm sweep and the five inflight sweep durations come from the Saturn V manual. [AS-506 Flight Evaluation Report, §§3.6.1–2 and table 4-1](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS506-Apollo11.pdf) · [Apollo 11 Mission Report, table 3-I](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a11/A11_MissionReport.pdf)

The July reconstruction places the service-arm steelwork outside the stage skins and leaves out the flexible umbilicals at the ends of arms 1–8. Arm 9’s landing sits about 12 cm below the hatch sill. Its rigid front edge ends about 29 cm outboard of the closed hatch’s outermost point. The landing and the space for the open leaves are estimates based on the modelled hatch. The soft White Room interface is omitted. The exported meshes are checked against the stationary stack as the hatches and arms move. The collision boxes have a separate clearance check because their rounded edges can extend past the drawn surfaces.

The dated launcher has four cast hold-down arms at Saturn positions I–IV, between the first stage’s engine fairings. The castings were about 6 × 9 ft at the base and 10 ft tall. The support girders, bearing shoes, links and blast hoods follow the manual’s plan and *Moonport*’s side elevation. The shelf meets the vehicle model’s lower ring 19.44 ft above the engine exits. The drawing gives vehicle station 113.31 at the controlled-release detail. The common reference plane for the shelf and lower ring, girder sections, hinge positions and sweep angles are reconstruction estimates. The upper link represents the collapsing linkage; individual lower links and pneumatic actuators are omitted. The crane is parked crosswise, with its hook clear of the stack. Its east-facing direction is estimated. All three tail-service masts retracted normally and their hoods closed. Masts 1-2, 3-2 and 3-4 follow the arrangement in figure 8-7. At first motion each carrier draws back while its counterbalanced boom rises into the mast. The exhibit gives the boom 1.1 s to stand upright and the visor a further 0.6 s to close. Mast dimensions, exact azimuths and travel times are estimated from the drawing. Unsecured covers on the S-II forward arm and the restraints that kept service-arm control-console doors closed on this flight are omitted. ## Apollo 12 at Pad A, November 14, 1969

Mobile Launcher 2 carried SA-507. The SA-507 manual shows the four hold-downs at positions I–IV around the engine opening, three tail service masts and nine service arms. The exhibit reuses the articulated castings, carrier geometry, tower, approach stairs, paired hatch recess and parked crane from July as estimates for this launcher. The flight manual gives the same base, crane reach and 320 ft boarding level. The November reconstruction adds the industrial-water quench valve to the S-IC forward arm. Its presence on arm 2 is documented; the envelope, adjacent pipework, spray outlets, brackets and their placement on the truss are estimates. [NASA, Apollo 12 mission details](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/apollo/apollo-12-the-pinpoint-mission/) · [SA-507 manual, §VIII, figs. 8-6–8](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pdf/a12_sa507-flightmanual.pdf) · [AS-507 flight evaluation, §3.6.1](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pdf/a12-saturn-v-fer.pdf)

Launch Control reported the crew arm in its 12° standby position during the T−29:54 update. It observed motion away from standby at T−5:00. The unreported arrival at full retraction follows the flight manual’s nominal T−4:50 lock time. The two preflight S-IC arm strokes use the same manual’s planned retract-and-lock intervals, T−30 to −17 s and T−16.2 to −11 s. The flight evaluation confirms that the arms reached safe angles within specification. [Kennedy Launch Control broadcast](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/01launch_to_earth_orbit.html) · [SA-507 manual, fig. 8-20](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pdf/a12_sa507-flightmanual.pdf) · [AS-507 flight evaluation, §3.6.1](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pdf/a12-saturn-v-fer.pdf)

SA-507 first moved at T+0.25 s. The four hold-downs released within eight milliseconds of each other, and the three tail masts retracted normally. All four hold-down protective hoods close in the exhibit according to their nominal design; individual link, carrier, boom and hood travel times are estimates. A quench valve on arm 2 failed to open after liftoff. Favorable wind and water from higher arms kept the arm from apparent damage. Film also showed that the S-II forward umbilical cover failed to secure. During the countdown, wind pressed the Q-ball cover cable against the primary damper. The pad team twice freed the cable at the crane. The reconstruction omits the flexible umbilical covers, damper and cable, water spray and postlaunch damage. ## Apollo 13 at Pad A, April 11, 1970

SA-508’s first stage was erected on mobile launcher 3 on June 18, 1969. The complete vehicle and launcher reached Pad A on December 15. ML-3 had stood on Pad B with Apollo 10. In April the launcher stood above Pad A’s flame trench, with the open-air substation and water valve pit to the west. The deck, tower elevator, three tail masts, four hold-downs and nine arms follow the shared reconstruction. The final countdown was directed from firing room 1. [AS-508 Flight Evaluation Report, table 3-1 and §3.3](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS508-Apollo13.pdf) · [NASA photograph of SA-508 on Pad A after rollout](https://www.nasa.gov/history/50-years-ago-apollo-13-launch-date-reset/)

Arm 1 supplied oxygen through the intertank. The S-IC had two six-inch fill and drain lines, and the launcher withdrew the carrier with pneumatically operated parallel links. LOX loading was complete across all three stages by T−5 h 41 min, when replenishment began. Later the S-IC’s No. 2 vent valve stuck open. The launch team freed it with a gaseous-nitrogen purge and 13 close commands. The ground-side carrier is visible in the arm 1 inspection view. Its paired lines, coupling collars and linkage show how the hard fittings travel with the truss. [SA-508 Flight Manual, pp. 4-12 and 8-10, figs. 4-13 and 8-8](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/MSFC-MAN-508%20Saturn%20V%20Flight%20Manual%20SA-508%201-March-1970.pdf) · [AS-508 Flight Evaluation Report, §3.4.2](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS508-Apollo13.pdf)

Launch Control reported the white room moving away from Odyssey at T−43 minutes and about ten feet away before T−42:31. Arm 9 stood at 12° until about T−5 minutes, when the final withdrawal was called. Its arrival at the fully retracted position uses the manual’s nominal T−4:50. During the final minute the announcer forecast T−36 s for arm 1. The clock uses the manual’s retract-and-lock sequence, T−30 to −17 s for arm 1 and T−16.2 to −11 s for arm 2. The broadcast does not identify the instant of either stroke from observation. [Kennedy Launch Control, April 11 countdown](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap13fj/01launch_ascent.html) · [SA-508 Flight Manual, fig. 8-20](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/MSFC-MAN-508%20Saturn%20V%20Flight%20Manual%20SA-508%201-March-1970.pdf)

The four hold-downs released at T+0.3 s. The five inflight arms then swung clear. The DEE-6 digital event recorder failed just before liftoff. Control-panel indications confirmed every arm withdrew and latched. Television and panel indications showed the three tail masts retracted together and their hoods closed within four seconds of aft-plate separation. Film showed two covers on the S-II that failed to secure, at the arm 4 intermediate connection and the arm 5 forward connection. [AS-508 Flight Evaluation Report, table 2-2 and §§3.6.1–3](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS508-Apollo13.pdf)

**Reconstruction limits.** Ground-side pipe jacket diameters, linkage dimensions, truss stations and rigid clearances are estimated. The model omits the soft umbilical interfaces, individual linkage strokes, stage-side cover faults and cryogenic vapour. The Q-ball cover and its removal cable are omitted. The model estimates the travel of each hold-down, mast and hood within the documented sequence. The park angle of the crane, stair and white-room layout and lift-car geometry follow the common launcher reconstruction.

## Apollo 14 at Pad A, January 31, 1971

S-IC-9 was erected on mobile launcher 2 on January 14, 1970. The assembled launcher and vehicle reached Pad A on November 9. The exhibit carries forward the arm 2 industrial-water header from this launcher’s Apollo 12 configuration and the arm 1 intertank oxygen carrier from the SA-508 reconstruction. The S-IC vent valve was replaced before countdown. At 08:46 EST all three stages were receiving replenishment oxygen. [AS-509 Flight Evaluation Report, table 3-1 and §§3.3–3.4.2](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_saturn-v-fer.pdf)

At T−55 minutes on the countdown, Launch Control forecast arm 9’s departure in about 1 minute 40 seconds. By the T−52:13 call the arm had reached its 12° standby position. The weather hold added 40 minutes 2 seconds between these events and range zero, placing the forecast withdrawal at −1:33:22 and the standby report at −1:32:15 on the exhibit clock. Launch Control scheduled the final crew-arm withdrawal for T−5 minutes, after the hold. The exhibit uses that scheduled time. Its travel and the two preflight S-IC arm strokes use the nominal schedule for ML-2. [Kennedy Launch Control broadcast](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/01_day1_launch.html) · [SA-507 flight manual, fig. 8-20](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/pdf/a12_sa507-flightmanual.pdf)

The four hold-down arms released pneumatically within four milliseconds. The launch-vehicle report puts first motion at T+0.2 s and the instrument unit’s sensed umbilical disconnect at T+0.57 s. Its separate service-arm switch readings of 418 and 424 milliseconds are measured from launch commit. The three tail service masts took **2.168 s (1-2), 2.496 s (3-2), and 2.131 s (3-4)** from umbilical plate separation to full retraction. Each boom follows its own measured duration. The exhibit places all three plate separations at first motion and estimates the carrier and visor travel. [AS-509 Flight Evaluation Report, tables 2-1 and 2-2 and §3.6.1](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_saturn-v-fer.pdf)

The S-IC fuel load needed replenishment after cool weather reduced its level. Mast 1-2 carried the RP-1 fill line. Shortly after launch, securing mast power allowed cutoff valve A18651 to open, admitting residual fuel into the piping in launcher room 4A. The flight evaluation records that the same condition had recurred on earlier launches. A replacement that retained its last commanded position was scheduled for AS-510. The reconstruction places a flanged valve and pneumatic actuator on the ground-side riser. Its trunnion connection feeds the pipe on the rotating boom. [AS-509 Flight Evaluation Report, §3.4.1](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap14fj/pdf/a14_saturn-v-fer.pdf) · [AS-506 Flight Evaluation Report, §3.6.1, mast identification and pneumatic control](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lvfea-AS506-Apollo11.pdf)

**Reconstruction limits.** The cutoff-valve body, control piping, fittings, riser and installation height are estimated. The room 4A pipework, internal valve travel, interface seals and postlaunch contamination are omitted. The mast strokes use interpolated angles between their documented endpoints. The individual arm and hold-down strokes, blast-hood angles, crane orientation, boarding recess and lift car use the shared reconstruction.

## Apollo 15 at Pad A, July 26, 1971

S-IC-10 was erected on ML-3 on July 8, 1970. The assembled Saturn and launcher moved to Pad A on May 11, 1971. The exhibit carries forward this launcher's tower, paired intertank oxygen couplings, three tail service masts and four pneumatic hold-downs from Apollo 13. The AS-510 countdown started on July 24 at 19:00 EDT. Both holds were scheduled. Propellant crews reached all-stage oxygen replenishment by 03:00 EDT on launch day. A leak at filter A224 in the lower S-IC fill-and-drain line stopped after about five minutes as the filter chilled. [AS-510 Flight Evaluation Report, table 3-1 and §§3.3–3.4.2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025086)

The environmental control room occupied 96 × 112 ft of the west pad fill, north of the pad terminal connection room. Its air-handling units, chillers and compressors supplied conditioned air or nitrogen to the space vehicle. The change to nitrogen purge occurred at 23:10 EDT on July 25. Purging ended at 09:44 EDT on July 26, ten minutes after liftoff. Inspection found scorched ductwork and loose anchors at the remote air plenum mounted on the room's exterior wall. The exhibit opens a frontage in the west embankment so the plenum can be reached on foot. Its presence is documented for this flight. The date of installation is unknown. [SA-510 Flight Manual, §VIII, fig. 8-10 and p. 8-12](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap15fj/pdf/as15-sa510-flight-manual.pdf) · [AS-510 Flight Evaluation Report, §3.6.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025086)

Scott boarded Endeavour at 06:53 EDT. Launch Control saw the access arm pull away at T−53 minutes, ten minutes earlier than planned. It went to the 12° standby position, about six feet from the spacecraft. The exhibit estimates 30 seconds to reach standby. The announcer reported that the arm was moving to full retraction just before calling the T−5-minute mark. Its lock time at T−4:50 follows the nominal flight manual. The first two service arms also use the manual's nominal retract-and-lock intervals, T−30 to −17 seconds for the intertank and T−16.2 to −11 seconds for the forward arm. [Kennedy Launch Control broadcast](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap15fj/01launch_to_earth_orbit.html) · [SA-510 Flight Manual, fig. 8-20](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap15fj/pdf/as15-sa510-flight-manual.pdf)

The four hold-downs released within three milliseconds of one another. SA-510 first moved at T+0.30 s, and the instrument unit sensed umbilical disconnect at T+0.58 s. The service-arm controls used a separate time reference. Their primary switches closed 449 and 476 milliseconds after launch commit. Mast 1-2 took **2.307 s** from umbilical plate separation to full retraction, mast 3-2 **2.151 s**, and mast 3-4 **2.688 s**. At about T−6 h 25 min the pressure meter for mast 3-2 in the Launch Control Center read about 150 psi low. Controllers used pressure-switch indications to establish readiness for the rest of the countdown. [AS-510 Flight Evaluation Report, table 2-2 and §3.6.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025086)

**Reconstruction limits.** The room's interior, buried utility runs and postlaunch damage are omitted. The model places its documented 96 ft width along the exposed frontage. The upstream filter and its gasket are omitted. The model places all three tail-plate separations at first motion and estimates linkage, carrier and visor trajectories. White-room geometry, crane parking angle and lift-car dimensions follow the shared reconstruction.

## Apollo 16 at Pad A, April 16, 1972

S-IC-11 was erected on mobile launcher 3 on September 21, 1971. The complete vehicle and launcher rolled out to Pad A on December 13, returned to the VAB on January 27 and rolled back to the pad on February 9. April's installation retains ML-3's intertank oxygen carrier and Pad A's west-side environmental control room. The purge switched from air to nitrogen 23 minutes before the count resumed at T−9 hours. Reduced flow during changeover softened the pressure surge seen in the countdown demonstration. [AS-511 flight evaluation, table 3-1 and §§3.3, 3.5.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025090)

The tower’s 200-foot level carried a purge console for the liquid-hydrogen ground system. An electrical terminal strip on the console was found broken after launch. Take the elevator to level 200 to see the reconstructed cabinet and strip beside the shaft. Oxygen replenishment had reached all stages at 06:13 EST. At about T−1 h 23 min the S-IC replenish valve stuck shut. It responded on the third cycle and the team kept replenishment under manual control until T−187 s. During arm 1 disconnect the carrier-connected indication persisted for 35.9 s after the retract command, compared with less than 0.3 s in two warm ground tests. The actual arm operated normally. [AS-511 flight evaluation, §§3.4.2–3 and 3.5.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025090)

Launch Control reported both elevators parked at the 320-foot level and arm 9 already in its 12° standby position during the T−50:57 to T−49:55 broadcast. The exhibit places the arrival at the end of that report and estimates the preceding 30-second stroke. At T−4:32 Launch Control observed arm 9 moving to full retraction. The clock centres a ten-second nominal stroke on that observation, placing departure at T−4:37 and arrival at T−4:27. The same launcher's SA-510 manual supplies the two S-IC arms' assumed nominal intervals of T−30 to −17 s and T−16.2 to −11 s. [Launch Control voice record, tapes 6/1 and 16/1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/as16-pao.pdf) · [SA-510 flight manual, fig. 8-20](https://www.apollojournals.org/afj/ap15fj/pdf/as15-sa510-flight-manual.pdf)

All four hold-downs released within twelve milliseconds. SA-511 first moved at T+0.3 s. The primary service-arm switches closed 423 and 417 milliseconds after launch commit. Measured from umbilical plate separation, mast 1-2 retracted in **2.649 seconds**, mast 3-2 in **2.292** and mast 3-4 in **2.505**. Fixed-hood bolts on one side of hold-down No. 4 sheared, warping both hoods. [AS-511 flight evaluation, table 2-2 and §3.5.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025090)

**Reconstruction limits.** The record locates the purge console by level. Its exact position, cabinet dimensions, instruments, conduit routing and terminal layout are estimates. Its installation date is unknown. The model shows the prelaunch console and hold-down hoods. Individual carrier breaks are placed at first motion, while hood and link trajectories, the crane’s parking angle and the White Room recess follow the shared ML-3 reconstruction. The west-side ECS façade and plenum follow the July 1971 installation.

## Apollo 17 at Pad A, December 7, 1972

S-IC-12 was erected on ML-3 on May 15. The assembled vehicle and launcher reached Pad A on August 28. During the countdown a sensor at the third-stage hydrogen tank vent disconnect on arm 7 intermittently detected gaseous hydrogen for about an hour and a half. The reading was accepted for launch. The ECS met its flow and pressure specifications through the air-to-nitrogen changeover. The reconstruction retains the previous ML-3 intertank oxygen carrier and level 200 purge console, and the west-side environmental control room. [AS-512 flight evaluation, table 3-1 and §§3.2.3, 3.5.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025089)

Seventy-two 20 kW xenon lamps and two 60 kW searchlight banks lit the Saturn. Launch Control reported about 225 foot-candles at the vehicle. The October egress exercise photograph shows light rising from low fixtures around the pad. The exhibit groups the smaller heads on eight racks with nine lamps apiece. A yoke carries each housing, reflector and lens. The larger banks are each represented by one head. Their exact grouping, coordinates, optics and aiming angles are estimates. The site renderer groups nearby lenses into spotlights and compresses the intensity for the display. Outside the documented night countdown the lighting follows the simulator’s estimated dusk and dawn switching schedule. KSC had already tested 20 kW xenon banks here on November 7, 1968. The exercise photograph and countdown commentary establish the 1972 installation. The locations and assignments of earlier banks are unknown. [KSC TR-918 as abstracted in WDL-TR4201, vol. II, §2.2.3.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19710017123) [Kennedy Launch Control, MC2/1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/as17-pao.pdf) · [KSC-72P-468, October 16 egress exercise](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a17/ap17-KSC-72P-468.jpg) · [NASA S72-54814, November prelaunch](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S72-54814)

First motion was at T+0.24 s and instrument unit umbilical disconnect at T+0.63 s. All four pneumatic hold-downs released within six milliseconds.
