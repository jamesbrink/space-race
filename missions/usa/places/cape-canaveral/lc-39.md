# Launch Complex 39 in 1968–69

Apollo 8 and 9 stood at Pad A on mobile launchers 1 and 2. Apollo 10 stood at Pad B on mobile launcher 3. The model uses the common Saturn V launcher structure for all three. The tower is north of the launch axis. Pad A's open-air electrical substation stands west of the slope. Pad B housed its substation under the slope and had its water valve pit on the east side. Pad B's hardstand stood five feet higher above sea level. The Blender scripts use feet from the upper surface of the hardstand. The viewer uses metres from the vehicle axis at deck level. Each launch uses the civil time in force on its date. The Gallery strips the site from the stack.

## Reconstruction limits

The exhibit covers the launch hardstand, approach, launcher and principal storage spheres. The outer crawlerway network, mobile service structure in its remote parking area, VAB, underground rooms and most peripheral equipment are omitted. Launcher details common to the three units are assumed. Individual cable routes, internal machinery and foundations are simplified. The site uses the Cape’s default weather.

The crew boards from the approach ramp, launcher stairs and tower elevator, then crosses the level 320 arm. The two high-speed tower elevators were rated at 600 ft/min between level A and level 340. One car is operable here. Stair geometry, outboard hinge positions and the final 90° sweep are estimated. Shared standing A7L figures represent the IV and EV launch garments. Body proportions, faces, small connectors and starting positions are illustrative. The final stair rise is reconstructed against the spacecraft hatch.

Five inflight arms release at first movement. Retraction of the S-II aft access arm is placed before ingress, and the two S-IC arms follow the individual countdowns. The Apollo 8 and 10 launch-vehicle evaluations give intertank withdrawal to safe angle and the interval to the next arm. The model estimates the angles and represents full clearance as one stroke. Apollo 9's intertank arm clears the vehicle before launch and finishes latching against the tower at T+1.77 s. Its flight manual gives the S-IC forward withdrawal at T−20 s, while its press kit puts the fourth arm release at about T−6 s. The exhibit follows the manual for this command. The pressure hatch and its protective cover open on separate hinges for boarding. Their angles and travel durations use the shared Block II reconstruction. The white-room recess on the hinge side clears both leaves throughout their swing. Its width is estimated. Flight-specific PAO broadcasts put the crew arm at 12° standby before its final withdrawal at T−5 minutes. The broadcast reports bound the first retraction without timing its start.
