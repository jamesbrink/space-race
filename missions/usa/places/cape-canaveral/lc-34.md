# Launch Complex 34 on 11 October 1968

The Apollo 7 site is the tall fixed tower and the much larger steel building on its railway. The
fixed tower stayed by the rocket and brought the crew to the hatch in an elevator. The mobile
structure held the cranes that stacked Saturn IB and the platforms technicians worked from. It
stood about 600 feet away when Apollo 7 lifted off.

## Frame and sources

The Blender origin is the Saturn axis at the concrete apron. +X follows the service railway
toward the fixed tower side (42° true, estimated from Esri World Imagery), +Y is northwest, and
+Z is up. The apron is 0.12 m above the viewer's local ground, an estimated amount. The launch
vehicle's pad datum is 27 feet above the Blender origin. The postflight trajectory report
(§2.0) gives the historical latitude of 28.521963° N, longitude of 80.561141° W and pedestal
base height of 5.7 m above its Fischer 1960 ellipsoid. Its launch-azimuth reference was 100°
true and its flight azimuth was 72° true. Those references serve the flight trajectory. The
railway sets the model's axis, and surviving features in modern imagery inform its position.
[AS-205/CSM-101 Postflight Trajectory](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/afj/ap07fj/pdf/as205-postflight-traj-rep-19920075302.pdf)

The period baseline is NASA's *Apollo 7 Press Kit*, pp. 39–44, especially the tower drawing on
p. 42a and site perspective on p. 42b. The evidence for the pedestal's open concrete frame,
refractory fields and flame-deflector tracks is the Argonne/Space Launch Delta 45 overhead
survey and measured drawings. Present-day photos show only the surviving geometry, so the
1968 drawings and flight photographs govern the state of the hardware. Paint is estimated
from color photographs. No modern Apollo 1 memorial plaques or stored-in-the-open
post-abandonment objects appear.

## How it stood on Apollo 7's morning

At the beginning of Apollo 7's launch-day sequence the service structure covers the rocket
for closeout. The press kit schedules its withdrawal to the park site at T−6h50m. Crew ingress
is from the fixed tower once the service structure is away. Launch Control set the access
arm 12° (about 3 ft) back at roughly T−33 minutes, and moved it all the way back at T−5 minutes.
The white room therefore exports fully withdrawn. Its illustrated 90° travel and exact pivot
position are estimates. The recorded all-hold-downs-released indication followed range zero
by 0.17 s. The umbilical-disconnect indication followed by 0.36 s.
[NASA Apollo 7 Launch Control commentary](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/as07-pao.pdf)
[NASA Apollo 7 Timeline](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4029/Apollo_07f_Timeline.htm)

The complex opened in 1961. This build represents the uprated Saturn IB hardware and the
post-Apollo 1 white room, including the simplified emergency escape wire, after the 1967
fire and by Apollo 7's October 1968 countdown. A 1961 Saturn I model would need different
tower, tower arms and service platforms. LC-34 was mothballed in November 1971 and the two
steel towers were razed in April 1972.
[HAER history of LC-34](https://tdglobal.ksc.nasa.gov/servlet/sm.web.Fetch/LC34.pdf?did=941730&rhid=1000&type=released)

## Boarding

Apollo 7’s Launch site opens at 08:35 EDT, the recorded start of crew ingress. The crew stand
near the apron-level elevator. On foot, enter the car and choose the 220-foot level. From the
landing, turn toward the Saturn, cross the access arm, and choose a couch at the open side
hatch. The blockhouse entrance and crew access arm can also be worked by hand.

The revised white room gave the redesigned CM hatch room to swing. The hinged boost-cover
leaf opens before the pressure hatch and closes after it, following the Block II mechanism’s
sequence. The pivots, leaf clearance and exact closeout timing are estimated. The reconstruction
allows 25 minutes for boarding from the recorded 12:35 UTC ingress. The upper and lower bounds of
that period are the viewer’s boarding window.

## What is simplified

- Beam sections, joints, finish, trolley framing, interior bay layouts, stair geometry,
  platform dimensions, wall details, cabinet placement and shade are estimates. The white room
  includes an open approach and firefighting cabinet. The after-fire ventilation, purge, and full plumbing remain unverified. The 213-ft boarding
  floor and its short stair within the 220-ft arm reconcile the press-kit landing level
  with the hatch on the modeled vehicle. The original transition is unverified.
- The concrete apron follows a level ground plane. Drainage and road centerlines are estimates
  traced from modern survivors and the contemporary perspective. Rail heads are solid steel
  strips and wheel bearings, drive motors and most signal/fueling lines are omitted.
- The blockhouse has a playable entrance and two floor levels; its console faces, machine
  rooms, wall profile and interior fittings are representative. Its roof deck and periscopes
  are simplified from the HAER photography. The Operations Support Building has an exterior
  only. Propellant fields do not reproduce all ancillary piping, pumps or burn facilities.
- The movable service structure's deck machinery is stowed. Its four lift cages are modeled;
  exact platform staging, lift doors, weather panels and crew access at each level are incomplete.
  The slide wire is visible and its emergency carriage is omitted.
- The crew transfer coach is parked clear of the launch pedestal at an estimated point on
  the apron. The launch-day photo proves the journey and rear entrance. It does not show
  the exact stopping position, parking at ignition, the cabin's seating plan, or the NASA
  conversion's dimensions. The playable coach has estimated paint, cabin fittings, mass
  and steering lock, with flange/tyre dimensions taken from the stock vehicle.
- Four estimated flood banks are aimed up from the apron, and the smaller tower floods
  face the lower parts of the rocket. NASA's night photo shows the original effect and an
  apron light pole; individual fixture positions, lamp count and photometry are estimated.
- The rail joints sound at an estimated 39-ft spacing, using period US practice. The launch kit
  documents diesel-electric motion but does not give the rail section length. Clark lists the
  Chrysler HB-225-450 inline six for the stock 1968 coach. Its 500-rpm idle and 4,000-rpm rated
  speed are assumed for the crew van from Chrysler’s industrial HB-225 manuals. Sound levels are
  synthesized estimates.
