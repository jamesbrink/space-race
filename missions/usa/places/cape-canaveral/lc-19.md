# Launch Complex 19, Cape Kennedy

Where every crewed Gemini left from, Gemini 3 (23 March 1965) first. The pad's own model is not built yet: the complex
is a stand-in in the Cape's scenery (`scenery.ts`, seen from the other pads), and its `SiteDef` (`lc-19.ts`) gives the
sky, the grade and the frame a flight from it stands in. The research behind this record, with every figure's source,
is issue #25 (the notes of 14 November 2026); the figures used here are quoted from it with their pages.

## The site's frame

The origin is the middle of the launch stand, on the deck the Titan's thrust mount stood on (OpenStreetMap way
195727094: 28.506719° N, 80.554253° W). +Y is up. **+X is the side the spacecraft's hatches faced: 84.9° true,
estimated** (the spacecraft model's +X is its top, between the hatches: vehicles/gemini-spacecraft.md).

What else is documented of the stand and does not settle it: the erector lay "parallel to the deck of the test
stand" when down and the stage trailers came up "the long ramp" (SP-4203, PDF p. 208), and the ramp runs south (the
Martin/NASA diagram of the complex; OpenStreetMap), so the erector lowered south over the ramp (derived); the
umbilical tower stood on the vehicle's north-north-west side as the diagram draws it (estimated: scaled off an
isometric); the blockhouse is 738 ft west on 273° (OpenStreetMap way 378204625: derived). None of these places the
hatches: the white room wrapped the spacecraft at the erector's top. **So 84.9° is kept, estimated as a whole**: the
line is now argued from the stand, the side from another flight. The complex's drawings would settle both; no HAER
for Complex 19 is in the Library of Congress, and the NHL nomination's appendix G (LC-19's site plans) is not in its
online copy (issue #25).

## The grade

The stand was "three stories high" (the NHL nomination, Item 7, p. 9: documented); a storey's height is not given, and
10 ft is taken, so the deck is **30 ft over the ground** (`lc-19.ts` `grade`, −9.144 m: estimated). `height` takes the
deck as about 7 m above mean sea level, which with that grade would put the ground under the sea; the ground at the
Cape is about 2 m above it (Complex 5's). One of the two is wrong and neither has been read: the drawings would settle
both.

## What stood when

Built 1957 to 1959 for Titan I (accepted 16 June 1959: the Cape Canaveral Space Force Museum); its last Titan I flew
on 29 January 1962 (GCAT). Rebuilt for Gemini: designed from February 1962, an erector chosen over a gantry in April,
built from September 1962, complete and activated on 17 October 1963 (NASA SP-4002, entry of 21 May 1962); Cleary
(*The 6555th*, ch. IV §3) has an eighteen-month remodelling done in August 1963; the white room by March 1963 (the
museum's photograph of 14 March 1963). Seen from Complex 14 it is drawn as Titan I's until September 1962 and not
after: the rebuilt complex has no stand-in (`scenery.ts`), which no flight from another pad here needs before 1966.
What stands round it on Gemini 3's day is the Cape's, dated (lc-5.md, "After 1961"): Complex 14 rebuilt for
Atlas-Agena, Complex 5, Complex 34 without its umbilical tower; Complexes 20 (rebuilt for Titan IIIA, 500 m off), 36,
37 and 40 stood and are not drawn. Its state on 23 March 1965 is the one issue #25 describes: the stand, the
launch vehicle erector (127 t, hinged at its foot, the white room of four levels at its top), the second-stage erector
(lowered), the umbilical tower (102 ft by the NHL nomination; 31 m with seven booms by SP-4203), the blockhouse (two
storeys, 156 ft across, 50 ft high), the propellant farms, the flume and skimming basin.

## What a Launch site here still needs

- **The complex's model**, as Complex 5's is: a package `lc19/` beside this file, with `model/<date>.py` for its
  states (Gemini 3's first), its `.parts.ts`, and each flight's `flight.ts` given a model with `backdrop: 'ground'`
  and `surroundings`. What is not dimensioned in anything read, and has to be scaled off the Martin/NASA diagram
  (*Project Gemini*, PDF p. 44) and photographs: the erector's height, its hinge line and its lowered length; the
  umbilical tower's booms' heights; the white room's size; the flame deflector's shape.
- **Its moves**: the erector lowered at T−35 (planned: the press kit, p. 11), about 08:49 EST on 23 March 1965
  (derived from the 24-minute hold that ended 35 minutes before liftoff: SP-4203, PDF p. 253); how long it took to
  lower is not read. The white room dismantled from T−75.
- **The grade and the bearing** (above), both estimated.
