# Launch Complex 19, Cape Kennedy

Where every crewed Gemini left from: Gemini 3 (23 March 1965) first, then Gemini 4 (3 June 1965) and Gemini 5
(21 August 1965), each on its own morning ("What stood when"). The pad's model is `lc_19.py` and the package
`lc19/`: the stand, the erector and what stood on the deck are a flight's `pad.glb` (its `model/pad.py` names the
date), and the rest of the complex is the place's own model, `lc19/model/1965-03.py`. `lc-19.ts` is its `SiteDef`,
`lc-19.parts.ts` its parts as flight data, `lc-19.ground.ts` its own ground. From the other pads the complex is a
stand-in in the Cape's scenery (`scenery.ts`).

Dimensions are feet, as the sources give them. Confidence labels are the repo's: `documented` (a published figure,
with its page), `derived`, `estimated` (read off a photograph, which is named, or chosen).

## The site's frame

The origin is the launch vehicle's axis on the launch deck. +Z is up. **+X runs along the stand toward its ramp,
175° true**; +Y is 85° true, east, toward the sea.

**The launch vehicle's pitch plane is not the hatches' side.** Gemini IV, from this stand to the same 72° azimuth,
rolled 12.9° after liftoff (1.25°/s from 10.16 s to 20.48 s: its mission report, Tables 4-I and 5.2-V), so the launch
vehicle's reference plane lay on 84.9° or 59.1° on the stand: 84.9° is the thrust mount's centre line turned about
(85°). Until November 2026 `bearingX` was that 84.9°, taken for the hatches. With the hatches on 175°, the spacecraft's
top was a quarter turn from the plane the vehicle pitched over in, which would have the crew ride the ascent on their
sides, Grissom's seat uppermost. Nothing read says so in words, and Gemini IV's crew rolling "to a heads-up position"
after insertion fits it no better than a ride heads down. **It is for whoever writes the flight's plan to settle**;
the stand's evidence for the hatches is the three things in the table.

## Getting about on foot

From the land: the stair down each long wall of the stand (the east one is in S65-49568), or the ramp. On the deck:
the crew's red ramp (S65-20994) to the floor at the erector's foot and the express elevator; steps on the other side
(ours) to the service elevator and the stairs. Up the erector: either elevator; the stairs from the foot to the white
room's top level, entering each work platform and each white room floor through the west face; a ladder (ours) from
the top level to the bridge crane's rail. The umbilical tower, the second-stage umbilical tower and the camera tower
have ladders from floor to floor. `tools/walk-test.mjs` walks four ways here.

## What stood when

Built 1957 to 1959 for Titan I (accepted 16 June 1959); its last Titan I flew on 29 January 1962. Rebuilt for Gemini
from September 1962: the white room is in a photograph of 14 March 1963, and the complex "was ready when GT-1 arrived
October 28, 1963" (the handbook, p. 7-2). Martin's paper lists what the rebuilding changed: the ready building
doubled, the launch deck's north end, a white room and a second elevator on the complete vehicle erector, the
second-stage erector's platforms moved, the umbilical tower lengthened for two more booms, the second-stage umbilical
tower's booms moved, the flume enlarged, the south road changed for the two holding areas, and new: a road at the
north end for the spacecraft's hydrogen, the oxidizer and fuel holding areas, the decontamination building, an air
conditioning facility. Each thing the builders make says when it stood (`sitelib.stood`); a build prints the list.
Nothing is known to have differed between Gemini 3 and the flights after it except what the handbook, written in
late 1965, may describe that was not yet there in March: none is identified.

Each flight's `model/pad.py` names its own date and builds the pad from the same modules, and each shows the same
place (`lc19/1965-03.glb`) round it. What each morning was is the flight's `pad.ts`:

## What the model gets wrong or leaves out

- **The erector's structure is a reading of photographs.** Its columns, girts and bracing are placed to look as the
  south and east faces do, not from a drawing. The two great raking members at its foot are built as part of its
  south face; the stand "houses ... a pit for the actuator arms", which they may be: the arms, their pit, the winch
  and its ropes are not modelled, and the erector turns about its hinge with nothing seen to drive it.
- **The work platforms are two leaves a level.** The nine documented are built as eight that open and the white
  room's top floor, whole. How the real ones folded is not known.
- **The white room's taper** (25 ft square to 23) is not built: its walls are plumb. Its north door is four sliding
  lengths, not a roll. Its roof door does not open. Its inside is bare: floors, a lamp to each of the lower three,
  the bridge crane. The
  benches, the air ducts, the plastic curtains and the spacecraft's work stands are not built.
- **The stairs and the ladders on the erector are ours**: that it had stairs is documented, where they ran is not.
- **Which white room levels the express elevator served** is taken as the lower three.
- **The umbilical tower's place** is good to six feet and was chosen within that to clear the white room. Its booms
  are all alike but in length; the lines are three hoses a boom, not 31 in all.
- **The stand's rooms are not built.** Its doors are shut and its windows dark; where they are on its walls is
  estimated. The steel deck's framing is a reading of S65-43447 and S65-49568. The 80 deluge outlets round the erector
  and the water are not built.
- **The ramp's slope** is not recorded, and its length does not agree with the stand's 450 ft.
- **The flume and the skimming basin stand on the land**, a floor between kerbs with water in the basin: they were
  cut and banked. The flume's apron is flat.
- **The blockhouse is its mound, its crown and its entrance.** The door is shut: nothing inside is built. Its
  entrance is put on the west; which side it was on is read off the LSFR's aerial view.
- **Each holding area has one tank**, its roof and its revetment. The pumps, the heat exchangers, the control
  console, the vapour disposal units of the diagram and the propellant distribution shelter are not built. The
  transfer lines are two pipes on posts, straight to the stand.
- **Not built**: the ready building; the gates; the tall mast north-east of the stand in the handbook's aerial view;
  the trailers and vehicles; the hydrogen road's tank; the lights "flush in the ground" of the abort area, which is
  not cleared in the model beyond the complex's own clearing; the crew's transfer van.
- **The people** of the morning are not built, but the two who flew.
- **The handbook is of late 1965.** Everything documented from it is assumed to hold for March.
