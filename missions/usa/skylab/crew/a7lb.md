# Skylab 2 launch pressure garments

Conrad, Kerwin and Weitz crossed the access arm in modified A7LB pressure garments on May 25, 1973. Conrad's and Weitz's surviving suits show the same front arrangement. Two red disconnects sit on the wearer's right and four blue ones on his left, with a smaller fitting and metal loop between the two banks. Conrad's photograph from launch morning shows his name, the circular Skylab I mission emblem, brown shoulder webbing and the red sealing ring at his neck. The crew closed the clear bubble helmets for launch. The commander's garment lacks lunar identification bands. [Conrad's flown suit](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/pressure-suit-a7-lb-conrad-skylab-2-flown/nasm_A19761192000), [Weitz's flown suit](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/pressure-suit-a7-lb-weitz-skylab-2-flown/nasm_A19761196000), [NASA S73-25901](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S73-25901), [NASA mission emblem S72-52630](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S72-52630).

The biomedical report lists Conrad at 5 ft 6½ in, Kerwin at 6 ft and Weitz at 5 ft 10 in. Those documented statures set the body proportions before boots and helmet are fitted. Each figure has movable limbs, an eye anchor, individual gloved fingers, wrist disconnects, thigh pockets, laced boots and its own name tape. [NASA TM X-58163, Appendix A](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760004639).

The crew entered CSM-116 with Conrad in the left seat, Kerwin in the centre and Weitz on the right. Hartsfield, who put the crew into the spacecraft, recalled Kerwin in the centre. Prelaunch plans scheduled ingress at countdown T−2 h 40 min. The launch-day commentary has all three aboard with their emergency detection system check complete by 06:32 CDT, 88 minutes before liftoff. At 07:02 the white room was secured and the closeout crew was clearing the pad. At 07:13 the access arm moved to its 12-degree standby position. [NASA Skylab 1 and 2 Press Kit, pp. 30–31](https://web.archive.org/web/20070626195350id_/http://www-lib.ksc.nasa.gov/lib/archives/apollo/pk/SKYLAB12PK.PDF), [Hartsfield oral history, 12 June 2001](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/hartsfieldhw-6-12-01.pdf), [NASA launch commentary, MC1/1–MC3/1](https://digitalprojects.uah.edu/files/original/21/14413/sdsp_skyl_000078_001.pdf).

## Simplifications

- The surviving suits and photography establish the fitting colours and grouping. Diameters, spacing, tape, suit girths, webbing width and embroidered patch size are photographic estimates. Kerwin's coupling layout is assumed from his crewmates' surviving suits.
- The external form uses the shared A7LB launch garment, with an approximate side-entry closure, gloves and boots. Layered pressure bladders, portable ventilators, hoses, webbing fasteners and the crew's individual facial likenesses are omitted. Oxygen umbilicals and orbital EVA equipment belong to later mission configurations.
- Each crew member starts on the approach for a walk to the tower. Their starting positions and simultaneous boarding window are reconstructions. The available launch commentary gives bounds on closeout without timing each man's ingress or hatch closure.
