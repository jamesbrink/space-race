# Skylab launch pressure garments

## Skylab 2

Conrad, Kerwin and Weitz crossed the access arm in modified A7LB pressure garments on May 25, 1973. Conrad's and Weitz's surviving suits show the same front arrangement. Two red disconnects sit on the wearer's right and four blue ones on his left, with a smaller fitting and metal loop between the two banks. Conrad's photograph from launch morning shows his name, the circular Skylab I mission emblem, brown shoulder webbing and the red sealing ring at his neck. The crew closed the clear bubble helmets for launch. The commander's garment lacks lunar identification bands. [Conrad's flown suit](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/pressure-suit-a7-lb-conrad-skylab-2-flown/nasm_A19761192000), [Weitz's flown suit](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/pressure-suit-a7-lb-weitz-skylab-2-flown/nasm_A19761196000), [NASA S73-25901](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S73-25901), [NASA mission emblem S72-52630](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S72-52630).

The biomedical report lists Conrad at 5 ft 6½ in, Kerwin at 6 ft and Weitz at 5 ft 10 in. Those documented statures set the body proportions before boots and helmet are fitted. Each figure has movable limbs, an eye anchor, individual gloved fingers, wrist disconnects, thigh pockets, laced boots and its own name tape. [NASA TM X-58163, Appendix A](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760004639).

The crew entered CSM-116 with Conrad in the left seat, Kerwin in the centre and Weitz on the right. Hartsfield, who put the crew into the spacecraft, recalled Kerwin in the centre. Prelaunch plans scheduled ingress at countdown T−2 h 40 min. The launch-day commentary has all three aboard with their emergency detection system check complete by 06:32 CDT, 88 minutes before liftoff. At 07:02 the white room was secured and the closeout crew was clearing the pad. At 07:13 the access arm moved to its 12-degree standby position. [NASA Skylab 1 and 2 Press Kit, pp. 30–31](https://web.archive.org/web/20070626195350id_/http://www-lib.ksc.nasa.gov/lib/archives/apollo/pk/SKYLAB12PK.PDF), [Hartsfield oral history, 12 June 2001](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/hartsfieldhw-6-12-01.pdf), [NASA launch commentary, MC1/1–MC3/1](https://digitalprojects.uah.edu/files/original/21/14413/sdsp_skyl_000078_001.pdf).

## Skylab 3

On July 28, 1973, the second visiting crew left the Manned Spacecraft Operations Building with their bubble helmets sealed. Bean led Garriott and Lousma toward the transfer van. Photographs taken that morning show the brown shoulder webbing, dark gloves, red neck rings and front-mounted hose disconnects. The surviving flight garments retain their individual name tapes and round Skylab II patches. The emblem places Leonardo da Vinci’s human figure between the Sun and Earth. The numeral counts the crews to visit the workshop. [NASA suit-up photograph S73-31800](https://images.nasa.gov/details/s73-31800) · [NASA walkout photograph S73-31801](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S73-31801) · [NASA emblem S72-51123](https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/skylab_3_launch_4_crew_patch_s72-51123/)

NASA’s biomedical report lists Bean at 5 ft 9½ in, Garriott at 5 ft 9 in, and Lousma at 6 ft. The figures use these statures before adding boots and helmet. CSM-117’s launch assignments put Bean on the left, Garriott in the centre and Lousma on the right. [NASA TM X-58163, Appendix A](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760004639) · [Reference Skylab Flight Plan, Crew Scheduling A.1–2](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/46377138-Reference-Skylab-Flight-Plan-SL-1-SL-2-SL-3-And-SL-4-April-30-1973-SL-1-Launch.pdf)

The two red disconnects sit on the wearer’s right. Four blue fittings rise across the front towards the emblem on his left. A smaller fitting and a metal loop lie between the banks. The Smithsonian holds all three flown garments. [Bean’s garment](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/pressure-suit-apollo-a7-lb-skylab-3-bean-flown/nasm_A19761193000) · [Garriott’s garment](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/pressure-suit-apollo-a7-lb-skylab-3-garriott-flown/nasm_A19761194000) · [Lousma’s garment](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/pressure-suit-a7-lb-lousma-skylab-3-flown/nasm_A19761191000)

## Skylab 4

On November 16, 1973, Carr led Gibson and Pogue from the crew quarters to the transfer van for Pad 39B. The launch photographs show their clear bubble helmets, brown shoulder straps, dark gloves and white pressure garments. Each carried a ventilator. At the pad the crew rode the tower elevator and crossed the access arm to the white room. Carr occupied CSM-118’s left couch, Gibson the centre couch and Pogue the right. The walking route starts on the approach to the tower. [NASA’s launch account and suit-up photograph](https://www.nasa.gov/history/50-years-ago-launch-of-skylab-4-the-final-mission-to-skylab/) · [KSC walkout photograph 73PC-725](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/skylab-4-launch-16-73pc-725-nov-16-1973.jpg) · [Reference Skylab Flight Plan, Crew Scheduling A.1–2](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/46377138-Reference-Skylab-Flight-Plan-SL-1-SL-2-SL-3-And-SL-4-April-30-1973-SL-1-Launch.pdf)

The biomedical report lists all three men at 5 ft 9 in. That common stature sets their figures before boots and helmets are added. The rounded triangular patch on each left breast has a large black 3 for the third visiting crew, a human figure surrounded by a rainbow, a tree, and a hydrogen atom. Its outline and colours follow NASA’s artwork. The dimensions on the garment are estimated from the suit photographs. Each suit also carries its wearer’s name. [NASA TM X-58163, Appendix A, Skylab 4 crew](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760004639) · [NASA mission emblem S72-53094](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S72-53094)

## Simplifications

- The surviving suits and photography establish the fitting colours and grouping. Diameters, spacing, tape, suit girths, webbing width and embroidered patch size are photographic estimates. Kerwin's coupling layout is assumed from his crewmates' surviving suits.
- The external form uses the shared A7LB launch garment, with an approximate side-entry closure, gloves and boots. Layered pressure bladders, portable ventilators, hoses, webbing fasteners and the crew's individual facial likenesses are omitted. The transfer van photograph also shows portable ventilators, hoses and yellow protective overshoes. The standing figures wear their pressure boots. Oxygen umbilicals and orbital EVA equipment belong to later mission configurations.
- Each crew member starts on the approach for a walk to the tower. Their starting positions and simultaneous boarding window are reconstructions. The available launch commentary gives bounds on closeout without timing each man's ingress or hatch closure.
