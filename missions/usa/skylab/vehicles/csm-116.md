# CSM-116 · Skylab’s first crew ferry

Pete Conrad, Joseph Kerwin and Paul Weitz took CSM-116 to the damaged Skylab workshop on 25 May 1973. The service module supplied manoeuvring propellant, water and electricity. The command module carried the crew, repair tools and emergency sunshades. After inspecting the workshop, Conrad held station alongside while Weitz worked out of the side hatch. The crew later made a hard dock at the axial port and returned to Earth on 22 June. NASA’s first-visit mission report covers the soft dock, the stand-up EVA and the repeated capture attempts in §§2, 7.1 and 17.1.3. [JSC-08414, August 1973](https://www.scribd.com/document/44173060/Skylab-Mission-Report-First-Visit)

## Cabin and return equipment

From launch through docking, Conrad occupied the left couch, science pilot Kerwin the centre and pilot Weitz the right. The flight plan identifies the assigned positions by crew role, and Hartsfield recalls bringing Kerwin into the centre seat. [Reference Skylab Flight Plan, Flight Plan Notes](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/46377138-Reference-Skylab-Flight-Plan-SL-1-SL-2-SL-3-And-SL-4-April-30-1973-SL-1-Launch.pdf) · [Hartsfield oral history](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/hartsfieldhw-6-12-01.pdf)

The five window apertures, 154-inch outer diameter, pressure boundary, side-hatch outline, main console and engine envelope use the shared Block II references. The underlying survey is of Columbia. The later lightweight crew hatch, three foldable couches, navigation optics, handheld water dispenser and active docking probe are fitted to this ship. The Skylab handbook illustrates the CSM-116 cabin stowage in figure 1-27 and specifies the dispenser, coiled hose and under-panel bracket in §2.12. The Gallery exposes the shell, recovery compartment, cockpit instruments, couch frames, tank bays, main engine and docking hardware. [CSM 116–118 Skylab Operations Handbook, vol. I](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160013589) · [Common Block II structure and source measurements](../../apollo/vehicles/apollo-csm.md)

The parasol rode to orbit in a spare T027 experiment canister adapted to fit the workshop’s solar scientific airlock. Its envelope was 8 inches square by 53 inches long. The backplate sealed the deployment-rod passage. The orange aluminized-Mylar and nylon canopy lay folded inside for flight. A separate JSC sail and a Marshall twin-boom shade also went aboard as alternatives. Henry Hartsfield, who helped load the crew, recalled the sunshade equipment occupying the usual space under the seats. The Gallery places the canister transversely under the couch backs. Its local position, fittings and restraint bands are estimates. The ferry could spend weeks docked with one side facing the Sun. The model assigns white thermal paint to the sunny half of the existing shell, leaving the fairing, barrel and command-module aperture geometry intact. After the workshop lost a solar wing, technicians added thermal tape to the command-module shoulder and to EPS radiators 1, 5 and one quarter of panel 4. The first-visit report credits the two changes with saving about 100 watts. Figure 7-1 locates the coverage relative to the quads. The model assigns the tape to the existing shell faces. [JSC-08414, §7.2 and fig. 7-1](https://www.scribd.com/document/44173060/Skylab-Mission-Report-First-Visit)

## Simplifications

- The half-shell paint boundary and colour are estimated. The tape edges on the shoulder and the location of the quarter-area strip on EPS radiator 4 are estimated from fig. 7-1. Individual foil strips and stencils are omitted.
- Tank sizes in the auxiliary thruster pack use published vessel envelopes. Individual valve bodies, pressure regulators, internal bladders and feed-line routing around the quads are omitted.
- Exterior case details on the three descent batteries, the water tank outlet and the scimitar mount are estimated. The batteries’ internal cells and the pressure plumbing are omitted.
- The console uses CM-106 control coordinates. The Skylab-specific panel legends and switches, complete repair-tool stowage, spare shade packages and deployed parasol are omitted. The stowed canister shows the external envelope and fittings.
- The docking probe uses the common Apollo mechanical reconstruction. The station, its drogue, on-orbit docking attempts and reentry are outside this Gallery exhibit.
