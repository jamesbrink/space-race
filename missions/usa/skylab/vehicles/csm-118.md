# CSM-118 · Skylab’s third crew ferry

Gerald Carr, Edward Gibson and William Pogue launched on November 16, 1973 and landed in the Pacific on February 8, 1974. Their ferry delivered the crew, consumables for an extended visit, replacement hardware and equipment for observing Comet Kohoutek. Its mass changed with its cargo. NASA gives 6,103.72 kg for the command module at launch, 5,872.71 kg under the main parachutes and 5,633.80 kg at landing. [Third Visit Mission Report, JSC-08963, §§2 and 6.9](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740027126)

## Cabin and docking equipment

Carr occupied the left couch, Gibson the centre and Pogue the right. The five windows, main instrument console, navigation optics, drinking-water pistol, pressure shell and lightweight side hatch use the shared Block II reconstruction. The 154-inch shield diameter and shell stations are assumed from common Apollo hardware. The probe engaged Skylab’s axial docking port on the third approach. During preparations for the return, the crew found a light brown deposit on all five windows. Examination of the rendezvous panes revealed layers 0.4 and 1.3 micrometres thick. ## S201 ultraviolet camera

CSM-118’s launch inventory included the S201 camera developed by George Carruthers at the Naval Research Laboratory. The Apollo 16 backup camera was removed from its lunar tripod and mounted with its power supply and sequencer inside a vacuum-tight aluminium canister. The published assembly measured **49.5 × 35.6 × 31.8 cm** and weighed **26.3 kg**. The mesh measures 49.5 × 35.75 × 31.8 cm including its estimated switch tips. The model shows the removable covers, the sight rings on a common axis, and the power and sequence switches and indicators on the adjacent face. The camera could use Skylab’s scientific airlock or be mounted outside the workshop on a spacewalk. Its far-ultraviolet images recorded the hydrogen cloud around Kohoutek, invisible to the eye. [Third-visit report, §§3.1.5, 3.2.3 and A.5, figs. A-4 and A-5, table B-I](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740027126) · [Naval Research Laboratory, Measurements of Far-Ultraviolet Photographs from Skylab 4 and Apollo 16](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19770014572)

## Service module

A new low-absorptance finish covered the forward corner of the battery compartment in bay I, toward quad A. Its extent is scaled from figure 6-1. Without the change, NASA expected dormant batteries to reach about 315 K as the direction of sunlight changed near the end of the mission. The maximum observed was 303 K. The model puts the three battery cases underneath that compartment and the auxiliary propellant rack aft of them. These internal mounting positions are estimates. The eight electrical-power radiator panels flew bare. Three were bypassed in the cooling loop. The launch finish precedes the discoloration and plume damage photographed on the fourth spacewalk. [Third-visit report, §§6.2, 6.3.2 and A.1, figs. 6-1 and 6-2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740027126)

The Skylab ferry carried two fuel cells, three 500 Ah silver-zinc descent batteries, two service propulsion sump tanks, one service propulsion helium sphere, an extra water tank and two external VHF scimitar aerials. Its auxiliary thruster propellant module contained nine propellant vessels and three helium spheres. The common tanks, engine and cabin equipment follow the Skylab Operations Handbook and the shared Apollo model. On this visit the fuel cells supplied 19,500 Ah before shutdown on day 19. For the return, descent batteries 1 and 3 delivered 715 Ah between power-up and module separation. The main engine fired seven times for a combined 41 seconds. [Skylab Operations Handbook, vol. I, fig. 1-29 and §§2.4–2.8](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160013589) · [Third-visit report, §§6.3 and 6.7](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740027126)

## Simplifications

- The cabin panels, hull geometry and national markings use the shared Apollo reconstruction. Individual CSM-118 tape strips, serial labels, paint colours and precise paint boundaries are estimated.
- The launch drawing establishes the cargo groups over the aft lockers. Exact parcel counts, shapes, restraint hardware, clearances and dimensions are estimated. The stowed S201 camera’s location and orientation are estimated separately. The camera envelope is documented. Its external fittings are scaled from the flight report photograph. The sealed camera has no interior optics or operating sequencer.
- The service equipment envelopes follow the Skylab handbook. The reconstructed supports, terminal details, lateral and axial positions, lines and fittings simplify the installation. The thermal coating is part of the barrel mesh. Exact paint thickness, gradual discolouration, nozzle wrinkles and orbital contamination are omitted.
