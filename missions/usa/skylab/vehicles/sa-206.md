# Saturn IB SA-206

Skylab 2 flew S-IB-6, S-IVB-206 and IU-206 under adapter SLA-23 on May 25, 1973. The adapter supported CSM-116 and enclosed its service engine. A lunar module did not fly. The rocket lifted off from Kennedy’s Pad 39B. [Flight Evaluation Report, appendix B](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025087) · [KSC launch summary, XI-2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740015280)

## Flight hardware

- **S-IB-6.** Four outer kerosene tanks alternate with four oxygen tanks around the central oxygen tank. The cutaway includes tank end domes, folded baffles, the upper spider and engine feeds. Each H-1 was nominally rated at 205,000 lbf at sea level. The four inboards were fixed and the outboards gimballed. Section IV dates the switch to white fuel tanks to SA-206. The exposed tank paint, dark tail rectangles and upright lettering follow the flight hardware in the VAB. Comparing the photographed upper-stage diameter to the letter spacing gives approximately 41 in between lower-tank letters. The model uses a substitute sans face and estimates the spacing, width and height of the smaller serial marking. [MSFC-MAN-206, §IV](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740021163) · [NASA KSC-72P-506](https://images.nasa.gov/details/72P-0506) · [NASA S73-27095 at launch](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S73-27095)
- **S-IVB-206.** The interstage is 224.5 in long. Four retrorocket fairings are spaced 90° apart on the interstage. Three ullage fairings at 120° intervals ride above the joint on the S-IVB. The cutaway shows the hydrogen and oxygen tanks, common bulkhead, thrust cone, J-2 and six cold helium spheres in the hydrogen tank. One helium sphere outside it represents the ambient supply. The J-2 rated 225,000 lbf in vacuum at 5.5 mixture ratio. In NASA S73-27095 the forward skirt is black up to the instrument unit, and a narrower black band circles the aft skirt above the segmented interstage markings. Using the station drawing to scale that photograph gives approximately 60 in of black below the IU and 50 in above the interstage. Two 80-in attitude modules on opposite sides of the skirt each contain propellant and helium vessels and three attitude engines. The slider draws the pod covers aside for inspection. [MSFC-MAN-206, figs. 5-1, 5-55–56, 5-75–76 and §V “Lox Pressurant Loading”](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740021163) · [Flight Evaluation Report, appendix B, p. B-6](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025087)
- **IU-206 and M415.** Two sensor panels faced different exposures to one interstage retrorocket, one on its centreline, the other about 51° away. Each had four rows of three samples, including white coatings and a black control. Figure 6-30 identifies each specimen. The first row was uncovered at launch. Three covers on each panel concealed the other rows until before retrofire, before the escape tower left and after CSM separation. The postflight evaluation records that temperatures reached the ground. [MSFC-MAN-206, figs. 6-29–30](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740021163) · [Flight Evaluation Report, §18](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025087)

- The M415 specimens and covers follow the drawn row arrangement and panel spacing. Specimen sizes, coating colours, fairing profiles and fasteners are estimated. The slider separates the covers by an illustrative distance.

Panel cores, explosive seams and service umbilicals are omitted. The slider opens the panels for inspection and separates the two launcher stages by display distances.
