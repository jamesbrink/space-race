# Saturn IB SA-207

Skylab 3 lifted off from LC-39B on July 28, 1973, with S-IB-7, S-IVB-207, S-IU-208, SLA-25 and CSM-117. The instrument unit had a different serial number from the stage it guided. After spacecraft separation the S-IVB and its instrument unit became an X-ray observatory. [SA-207 flight evaluation, §§2, 18, appendix B](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025088) · [KSC launch summary, Skylab 3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740015280)

## Stack

The flight evaluation gives a 223.5 ft vehicle height, 40.7 ft fin span and 260 in upper-stage diameter. The station geometry uses the Skylab Saturn IB manual's figure 1-1 as a sister-vehicle drawing. The upper end of the S-IB is at station 962.304 in, the stage separation plane at 1186.804, the aft and forward ends of the instrument unit at 1662.859 and 1698.859, the service module aft interface at 2034.859 and the Q-ball tip at 2681.859. Those station values are assumed for SA-207. [SA-207 flight evaluation, appendix B](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025088) · [Skylab Saturn IB flight manual, fig. 1-1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740021163)

- **S-IB-7.** Eight H-1 engines burned kerosene and oxygen. The outboard four could gimbal. The white tank barrels carry upright United States lettering, with roll markings at the tail. The markings use the July launch photographs for colours and the earlier Skylab vehicle for letter outlines and spacing. The cutaway shows the clustered vessels, tank heads, spider and engine feeds. [NASA S73-32570](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S73-32570) · [NASA S73-32568](https://images.nasa.gov/details/S73-32568) · [SA-207 flight evaluation, §6](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025088)
- **S-IVB-207.** A single J-2 burned hydrogen and oxygen above a four-retromotor interstage. Three ullage rockets settled the propellants at staging. Two opposing attitude-control modules, each with three jets, steered the stage after cutoff. Their 80 in length is taken from the Skylab manual. The cutaway shows both propellant tanks, their common bulkhead, the thrust cone, pressurant spheres and the removable pod covers. [SA-207 flight evaluation, §§7, 11](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025088) · [Skylab Saturn IB flight manual, §V](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740021163)
- **S-IU-208 and SLA-25.** The instrument unit carried the guidance platform, launch vehicle digital computer and the S150 experiment. The 36 in ring sat below the adapter for CSM-117. Four hinged upper panels opened for spacecraft separation, leaving the upper stage and its X-ray detector with a clear field of view. The Gallery uses the 45° travel given for this adapter family. [SA-207 flight evaluation, §§2, 18](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025088) · [Skylab Saturn IB flight manual, §§VI, IX](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740021163)

## S150 galactic X-ray mapping

Location 24 housed a gas-flow proportional counter, a collimator, a protective cover and two star sensors used to reconstruct the pointing direction. When the spacecraft had left, the detector swung through 62.5°. The stage carried out seven observation maneuvers. The model's covered, upright detector opens with the Gallery separation slider. [Skylab Saturn IB flight manual, fig. 6-31](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740021163) · [SA-207 flight evaluation, §18 and table B-3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025088)

A separate panel in location 14 carried a pressure sphere and regulators. Its P-10 mixture was 90 percent argon and 10 percent methane. When pressure fell below 12 psia, the detector's high voltage shut off. The flight returned 110 minutes of X-ray data in the planned 265-minute operating interval. Location 23 carried the five components of the auxiliary storage and playback system, with 5,400 seconds of tape storage. The three assemblies follow photographs of the actual detector, gas panel and recorder. [Experiment final report, vol. II, figs. 1-1 to 1-3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740013315) · [SA-207 flight evaluation, §18 and table B-3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730025088)

## Simplifications

- The [Saturn IB model](../../apollo/vehicles/saturn-ib.md) supplies the common stage shells, engines, tank sections and ordinary instrument racks. The latter follow the SA-205 checklist, with space reserved for the three photographed experiment assemblies. Tank lines, fasteners, brackets, electrical harnesses and cooling connections are incomplete.
- S150's bay numbers and tilt angle are documented. The azimuth within each bay, mounts, case dimensions, sphere size, tubing runs, sensor faces, collimator vanes, finish and protective-cover hinge geometry are visual estimates. The cover travel and slider timing illustrate deployment. The gas plumbing and detector electronics are static.
- The smaller roll-pattern and serial stencils, exact spacecraft clocking, attitude-pod sections and staging-motor fairing contours are approximate. The Gallery separates stages by illustrative distances. The propulsion figures are nominal Saturn IB ratings.
