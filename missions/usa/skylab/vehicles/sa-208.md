# Saturn IB SA-208

On 16 November 1973, S-IB-8, S-IVB-208 and S-IU-207 lifted CSM-118 from Kennedy’s Pad 39B. The instrument unit serial belongs to an earlier place in the production sequence than the booster number suggests. SLA-24 joined the spacecraft to the instrument unit. [SA-208 flight evaluation, mission plan and §§2–3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750002068) · [NASA SP-4011, 14 November 1973, p. 338, citing the CSM-118/SLA-24 readiness review](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/sp-4011.pdf)

## The stack

- **S-IB-8** had four fixed and four gimballed H-1 engines. The eight white outer tanks alternated between kerosene and oxygen around a ninth, central oxygen tank. The cutaway exposes the heads, baffles, engine feeds and spider beam. During pad testing the forward domes of fuel tanks 3 and 4 distorted under negative pressure. They recovered their contour under pressure and passed proof testing before flight. [SA-208 flight evaluation, §§6 and 8.3.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750002068)
- **S-IVB-208** used one J-2. Four retromotors slowed the spent booster, and three ullage motors settled propellants for the start of the upper stage. Each of the two attitude-control pods carried three jets. After CSM-118 left, the stage turned retrograde. On its fourth revolution it expelled liquid oxygen for 475 seconds and hydrogen for 86 seconds to bring itself down over the Pacific. [SA-208 flight evaluation, mission plan and §§5, 7, 10](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750002068)
- **S-IU-207 and SLA-24.** The instrument unit guided the stack, received commands for the deorbit sequence and carried its computer, stable platform, telemetry and environmental equipment. The service engine rode inside the four-panel adapter until separation. The upper panels opened to clear its nozzle. The Gallery shows the ring without the preceding flight’s S150 instrument. [SA-208 flight evaluation, §§5, 9 and 15](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750002068) · [Skylab Saturn IB manual, §§VI and IX](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740021163)

The model assumes the Skylab Saturn IB manual’s stations for this vehicle, in inches. The S-IB ends at 962.304, the stages separate at 1186.804, the instrument unit spans 1662.859–1698.859, the upper adapter hinges are at 1781.309, and the aft service module interface is at 2034.859. The escape tower tip is at 2681.859 (68.12 m) and the fin span is 40.7 ft. The Gallery uses the manual’s 45° lid travel. [Skylab Saturn IB flight manual, fig. 1-1 and §IX](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740021163)

## The fins that flew

Before the countdown demonstration test, a crack turned up in the outrigger at fin position four. Technicians removed a 1 × 3 × ¾ in piece of cracked material from the lower web of the upper E-beam. They fitted a spacer, shim and bolted splice plate across the channel. After the test they found cracks at the rear-spar attachment fittings of all eight fins. Seven fins had cracks on both sides. All eight fins were exchanged, and reinforcing blocks were fitted around the mounting bolts of the replacements. The Gallery’s fin 4 view shows the stepped reinforcement on the fin side of the joint, the radial fasteners and the splice on the channel. The other seven positions have the fin reinforcement. [SA-208 flight evaluation, §8.3.2 and figs. 8-16 and 8-17](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750002068)

Seven interstage reaction beams also had cracks. Stress analysis found a safety factor above 1.5 against a required 1.4, and those beams flew as inspected. The launch was postponed to 16 November so the pad crew could exchange the fins. [SA-208 flight evaluation, flight summary and §8.3.2, fig. 8-18](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750002068)

## Simplifications

- The [common Saturn IB model](../../apollo/vehicles/saturn-ib.md) supplies the fin planform, engine profiles, stage shells, tanks and ordinary instrument-unit racks. The white tank cluster and dark roll markings follow the [launch photographs of SA-208](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/skylab-4-saturn-1b-space-vehicle-launch/). Their boundaries use the common Skylab layout. Small stencils, exact spacecraft clocking and individual fin serial markings remain unresolved.
- Figures 8-16 and 8-17 document the repair geometry and the removed coupon size. Fin-fitting dimensions, radial position, azimuth registration, channel length, finish and fastener detail are estimates. The splice and spacer are shown together; the thin shim is unresolved. The model omits microscopic cracks and tank-test deformation.
- The Gallery’s stage separation distances and timing are illustrative. The pod covers open for inspection. Engine ratings are nominal figures assumed from the shared Saturn IB sources.
