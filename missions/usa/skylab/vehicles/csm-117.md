# CSM-117 · Skylab’s second crew ferry

Alan Bean, Owen Garriott and Jack Lousma flew CSM-117 to the axial docking port on Skylab’s multiple docking adapter on July 28, 1973. They returned in the same command module on September 25. The Gallery shows the spacecraft in launch configuration, including the hardware they brought to repair and resupply the station. [Skylab Mission Report, Second Visit, JSC-08662, §§2, 3, 7 and 10](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740011377)

## Command module

The Skylab flight plan assigns the commander the left couch, the scientist pilot the centre couch and the pilot the right couch. On return day the centre couch would not lock upright until the crew tightened the screws at its shoulder break points. The reconstruction includes the three foldable couch frames, pressure vessel, five windows, lightweight outward-opening crew hatch, instrument panel, optics, hand-held water dispenser and docking tunnel. The 154-inch outer diameter, window geometry and other measured shell dimensions use Columbia as a Block II reference. [Reference Skylab Flight Plan, Flight Plan Notes](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/46377138-Reference-Skylab-Flight-Plan-SL-1-SL-2-SL-3-And-SL-4-April-30-1973-SL-1-Launch.pdf) · [Second-visit report, §10](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740011377) · [Common Block II structure and measurements](../../apollo/vehicles/apollo-csm.md)

Figure 7-1 of the mission report shows the improved parasol and rate-gyro packages on top of the aft bulkhead lockers. The flight stowage list includes two gyro processors with a bracket, four L-shaped processors, a distributor, cables and tools. The Gallery presents the covered launch packages beneath the couches. The crew left the improved parasol as a spare and deployed a different shade, the Marshall twin-pole sail, over the station’s existing parasol. [Second-visit report, §§3, 7.9, fig. 7-1 and table 7-V](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740011377)

## Service module

The spacecraft carried a single service propulsion helium sphere above the water tank, two main propellant sump tanks in sectors II and V, and fuel cells 1 and 3 with two oxygen and two hydrogen tanks in sector IV. Bay I accommodated the additional reaction-control propellant module, three 500 Ah descent batteries and a biological experiment. The extra propellant module used five oxidizer vessels, four fuel vessels and three helium spheres. Tank and battery envelopes follow the contractor’s Skylab handbook. The cells delivered 20,700 ampere-hours through shutdown on visit day 20. [Skylab Operations Handbook, vol. I, fig. 1-29 and §§2.5–2.6](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160013589) · [Second-visit report, §7.3](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740011377)

NASA reported one change to the first visit’s thermal configuration. The electrical power radiator panels on CSM-117 flew without the thermal tape applied to CSM-116. The model keeps the white sun-facing coating and shoulder tape on the command module. On orbit, low temperatures led to a change from eight active electrical power radiator panels to five. Two opposing VHF scimitar fairings and four reaction-control quads are visible on the service module. Quad B’s positive-yaw engine leaked oxidizer during rendezvous. On visit day six the crew isolated quad D following a second oxidizer leak. The investigation identified a likely connector leak in D. Quads A and C remained available. [Second-visit report, §§7.2–7.3, 17.1.1–2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740011377) · [Skylab Operations Handbook, figs. 2.4-2 and 2.8-31/32](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160013589)

### S071/S072 circadian-periodicity experiment

The 243-pound flight package measured 40 × 24 × 20 inches. It carried six pocket mice and four compartments of vinegar gnat pupae. Northrop’s front and rear photographs show the mouse chamber and environmental control enclosure, the instrument shelf, insect enclosures, oxygen sphere, coolant vessels, pump and electronics. The flight and ground packages were loaded six days before launch. The flight package entered the spacecraft three days before launch. Northrop records 31 hours 48 minutes of telemetry after launch (p. 59). The NASA mission report summarizes the time to loss of data as about 34 hours (§4.2.13). The comparison package at Kennedy ran for 29 days. [Skylab Operations Handbook, §2.11.4](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160013589) · [Northrop final report, figs. 1-1 and 1-2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740006734) · [Second-visit report, §§4.2.13, 17.2.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740011377)

## Simplifications

- The CSM shell, cockpit instruments and populated panel use the common Block II reconstruction. Exact CSM-117 paint edges, individual thermal tape strips, console legends and switches are approximate.
- The second-visit stowage drawing establishes which cargo occupied the aft locker area. The package envelopes, exact positions, wrappings and restraints are estimated. Additional boots, tools, film and return stowage are omitted.
- The experiment’s overall dimensions and sector are documented. Its exact mounting station, supports, individual enclosure dimensions, colours and pipe routes are estimated from the contractor’s photographs. The sealed enclosures have no modeled specimens or internal equipment.
- The propellant rack, tank fittings, battery terminals, service-module water vessel and antenna contours use the [common Skylab ferry reconstruction](csm-116.md). Individual valve mechanisms, internal plumbing, leak effects and station hardware are omitted.
